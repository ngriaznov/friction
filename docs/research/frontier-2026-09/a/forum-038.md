**Re: How do you guys adjust chain slack properly?**

Seems like this comes up every few weeks, so here's how I do mine. My bike is a 2018 SV650, but the process is basically the same on any chain-drive bike. **Check your own manual for the numbers.** Slack specs and torque values vary a lot between models.

**What you need**
- Rear stand, or just the sidestand if that's what your manual specifies (the SV spec is taken on the sidestand)
- Metric ruler or tape
- Torque wrench plus the right socket for the axle nut
- Wrenches for the adjuster bolts and locknuts
- Chain cleaner, a soft brush, rags, and O-ring-safe chain lube

**1. Find the tight spot first**

This is the part most people skip. Chains don't wear evenly, so slack changes as you rotate the wheel. Put the bike in neutral, then roll the rear wheel and check slack at 4–5 points around the chain. **Do the measurement and adjustment at the tightest spot.** Adjust at a loose spot and the chain will be over-tight at the tight one, which kills the countershaft bearing and the chain.

If there's a big difference between tight and loose spots (more than about 10 mm on mine), the chain is stretched unevenly and it's time for a new chain and sprockets. Adjusting won't fix that.

**2. Measure slack correctly**

Measure on the **lower run**, halfway between the sprockets. Push the chain up as far as it goes with light finger pressure, then pull it down, and read the total movement against the ruler. The SV spec is 20–30 mm. Plenty of bikes are in the 25–35 mm range and dirt bikes can be much more, so look yours up.

**3. Adjust**

1. Loosen the axle nut. You're only loosening it, don't take it off.
2. Loosen the locknuts on both adjusters.
3. Turn both adjuster bolts the **same amount**. I do 1/4 turns and count them. Keep checking the marks on the swingarm so the wheel stays aligned on both sides.
4. Re-measure at the tight spot. Repeat until you're in spec.
5. Sight down the chain from behind, or use a string line, to double-check alignment. Don't just trust the swingarm marks, because on some bikes they're off.

**4. Torque it down**

Snug the adjusters against the axle blocks, then torque the axle nut. **Mine is 100 N·m (74 lb-ft).** Do it with a torque wrench, not by feel. Then tighten the adjuster locknuts and fit a new cotter pin if your bike uses one. Check slack one more time, because torquing the axle can pull it tighter by a millimetre or two.

**5. Clean and lube**

I do this right after adjusting, ideally after a short ride so the chain is warm.

- Spray chain cleaner (or use kerosene on a rag), scrub with a soft brush, and wipe dry. **No wire brushes, petrol, or harsh solvents.** They damage the O-rings/X-rings.
- Put the lube on the **inside of the lower run**, aimed at the rollers and between the side plates, while you rotate the wheel by hand. Centrifugal force carries it outward when you ride.
- Wipe off the excess and let it sit for at least 15–20 minutes, or overnight, before riding so it doesn't fling everywhere.

I check slack every ~800 km and clean/lube every ~500 km, or after any ride in the rain.
