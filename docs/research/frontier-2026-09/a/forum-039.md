**Q: What exactly is the windowpane test, and what am I looking for?**

---

**A:**

The windowpane test is a quick check of whether your dough has developed enough gluten to hold gas and support a good rise.

**How to do it**

Pinch off a piece of dough about the size of a golf ball. Lightly oil or wet your fingers so it doesn't stick. Then slowly stretch it between your thumbs and fingers, rotating as you go, the way you'd stretch a pizza base. Work gently and take 20–30 seconds over it. Don't yank.

**What developed dough looks like**

- It stretches into a **thin, translucent membrane**, thin enough that light shows through it like a frosted window.
- The film feels **elastic and smooth**, not sticky or shaggy.
- If it does tear, the hole has **smooth, rounded edges** and the tear doesn't spread across the sheet.

This shows the gluten network is organised into long, aligned strands that can stretch without breaking.

**What underdeveloped dough looks like**

- It **tears quickly** before it gets thin, often while it's still opaque.
- Tears are **jagged and ragged** and run across the piece.
- The surface looks rough or lumpy, and it may feel sticky.

**Caveats**

- **Enriched doughs** (brioche, challah) and **whole-wheat doughs** rarely give a perfect windowpane. Fat and bran both interfere with gluten. A partial windowpane is usually fine for these.
- Plenty of great breads, especially long-fermented or no-knead styles, never pass a strict windowpane at mixing time. Their gluten develops over time instead of through kneading.

**If it still tears after 10 minutes of kneading**

Don't just keep kneading harder. Try these, roughly in this order:

1. **Rest it.** Cover the dough and leave it for 10–20 minutes. Gluten keeps developing while it rests, and the tension relaxes, so the dough often passes when you retest. Tight, overworked gluten tears just like underdeveloped gluten does.
2. **Check hydration.** Stiff, dry dough is hard to develop by hand. If it feels firm, work in a tablespoon of water at a time.
3. **Check your flour.** All-purpose or low-protein flour (under ~11%) may never get to a strong windowpane. Bread flour makes a big difference.
4. **Use stretch-and-folds.** Replace the continuous kneading with a set of folds every 30 minutes over 2 hours. You'll get good development with far less work.
5. **Check the salt timing and amount.** Salt strengthens gluten, but it also slows how fast the flour hydrates. Adding it after a short autolyse can help.

As a rule, 10 minutes of solid hand kneading plus a rest should get most lean bread-flour doughs there.
