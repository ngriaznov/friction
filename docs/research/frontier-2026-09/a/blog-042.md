# Our First Raised Beds: A Small Backyard, a Lot of Soil, and Surprise Cucumbers

Our backyard is about 20 by 25 feet, mostly patchy grass over heavy clay. For years I said I'd grow vegetables "someday." This spring I finally built two raised beds. Here's what I chose, what I'd change, and what grew better than I expected.

## The beds

I went back and forth between wood, galvanized steel, and composite kits. In the end I built two 4×8-foot beds from untreated cedar, 2×10 boards stacked two high for about 18 inches of depth.

Why cedar:

- It resists rot without chemical treatment, which mattered to me for food crops. Older pressure-treated lumber is the thing people worry about. Modern treatments are generally considered safe, but I didn't want to think about it.
- It's easy to cut and assemble with basic tools. I used 4×4 corner posts and exterior screws.
- It looks nice, and in a small yard where the beds are always in view, that counts.

It wasn't cheap. The lumber for both beds came to about $320. Galvanized kits would have been similar and probably would have lasted longer. Cedar should give us eight to ten years.

Four feet wide was the right call. I can reach the middle from either side without stepping in the bed. Eighteen inches deep was probably more than I needed for most crops, but it meant I never had to worry about the clay underneath.

I laid hardware cloth across the bottom of each bed before filling it. We have voles, and I'd heard enough stories.

## The soil mix

Filling the beds was the part I underestimated. Two 4×8×1.5-foot beds hold about 3.5 cubic yards of material. That's a lot of bags, so I ordered in bulk from a local landscape supplier.

The mix I used, roughly by volume:

- **60% screened topsoil**
- **30% compost**, split between the supplier's municipal compost and bagged mushroom compost
- **10% coarse perlite**, for drainage

Some popular recipes skip topsoil entirely and use peat or coir, compost, and vermiculite. That would have been lighter and fluffier, but it's expensive at this volume. The topsoil-heavy mix settled about two inches over the season, and I'll top it up with compost in the fall.

## What did surprisingly well

**Cucumbers.** I planted two vines on a cattle-panel trellis along the back of one bed, mostly as an afterthought. We picked well over a hundred cucumbers. We gave them to neighbors, made pickles, and still couldn't keep up.

**Bush beans.** I planted a single short row of a bush variety and it produced for about six weeks. They need no support and almost no attention.

**Swiss chard.** It looked unimpressive at first, but it never bolted during the hot spell in July, kept regrowing after every harvest, and is still going now in late September.

**Cherry tomatoes.** I expected them to do well, but not this well. One Sungold plant took over a whole corner of the bed and needed staking twice.

## What didn't

Carrots came up stunted and forked. I think the new soil still had too many clumps. Broccoli bolted almost immediately in the heat. Zucchini got powdery mildew in August, which I've since learned happens to basically everyone.

## Next year

I'm adding a third bed, putting in drip irrigation on a timer, and planting fewer cucumbers. Probably.
