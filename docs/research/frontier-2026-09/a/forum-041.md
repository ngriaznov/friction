**Re: Fitting tenons without a shoulder plane?**

Absolutely doable. I went years without a shoulder plane and still don't reach for one much. The trick is to leave the tenon a bit fat off the saw, then pare down to the fit with a chisel and sneak up on it with repeated dry fits. Here's how I do it.

**Start fat on purpose**

When you saw the cheeks, split the line on the **waste side** so the tenon ends up maybe 1/64" to 1/32" thick. Getting it slightly oversize is the whole point. You can always take wood off, but you can't put it back. Make sure your shoulder cuts are clean and square, because the chisel work only touches the cheeks.

**Paring the cheeks**

Use the widest sharp chisel you have. I use a 1" bevel-edge. It has to be *sharp*. If it won't shave end grain cleanly, hone it before you start.

1. Clamp the piece flat on the bench with the cheek facing up.
2. Hold the chisel **bevel-up**, with the back flat on the cheek. The flat back acts like a plane sole and stops you digging in.
3. Pare across the grain in thin shavings, working **from the edges toward the middle**. If you go all the way across in one pass, you'll spelch (break out) the far edge. Do half from one side, flip, and do the other half.
4. Near the shoulder, choke up on the blade and pare with your guide hand on the chisel's back. Keep the chisel slightly skewed so you get a slicing cut.
5. Check for flatness often with a straightedge or the blade of a combination square. You want the cheek flat or very slightly *hollow* in the middle, never crowned. A crowned cheek rocks in the mortise and feels tight when it isn't.

Take the same amount off both cheeks so the tenon stays centred. If your mortise is offset or a little off, pare mostly from the side that needs it.

**Checking the fit incrementally**

This is where patience pays off.

- **Start the tenon in the mortise** by hand. If it won't go in at all, look at the leading edges and knock a tiny chamfer on the end of the tenon so it starts easily.
- Push it in as far as it goes by hand, then pull it out and look. The tight spots get **burnished shiny** by the mortise walls. Soft pencil scribbled on the cheeks before the fit makes the rubbed areas even easier to see.
- Pare *only* the shiny spots, with just one or two light passes.
- Repeat. You'll do this maybe 5–10 times. Each dry fit should go a bit deeper.

**Knowing when it's right**

A good fit goes in with **firm hand pressure** or a couple of light taps from a mallet, and it holds its own weight when you pick the assembly up by one piece. It shouldn't need heavy pounding. If you have to beat it in, the tenon is too thick and you risk splitting the mortise walls, especially near the end of a stile. Too loose and it'll wobble; glue won't fill that gap properly.

If you overshoot and it goes slack, glue a thin shaving or veneer on the cheek, let it dry, and pare it back to fit. Nobody will ever know.

Last tip: mark each tenon and its mortise with a matching number. When you're hand-fitting, every joint is slightly different.
