**8 weeks of HR zone training before my marathon – what happened**

Long-time lurker, first real post. Some of you asked in the "slow down to speed up?" thread how my HR block went, so here's the write-up. I'm 41 and this is my third marathon. Previous best is 3:52, and I've always come apart after 30 km.

**Estimating my zones**

I didn't use 220-minus-age. For me it's way off: it gives 179, and I've seen 186 on hill repeats. Instead I did the lactate threshold field test from Joe Friel's method:

- Warm up 15 min.
- Run 30 min solo, as hard as you can hold evenly (a flat loop, no racing anyone).
- Use your **average HR over the last 20 minutes** as your LTHR.

Mine came out at **168 bpm**. I built my zones from that using Friel's running percentages. Roughly:

- Z1: < 85% LTHR (< 143)
- **Z2: 85–89% (143–150)**
- Z3: 90–94% (151–158)
- Z4: 95–99% (159–166)
- Z5: 100%+ (167+)

I cross-checked by doing a few easy runs where I could comfortably talk in full sentences, and they sat at 145–150, so the numbers felt right. I used a chest strap the whole time. The wrist optical sensor on my watch read 10+ bpm high for the first 10 minutes of every run, which would have messed everything up.

**Why most of the mileage stayed in Zone 2**

Weekly mileage went from 55 km to 72 km over the block, and about **80% of it was Z2**. The rest was one quality session a week (tempo or marathon-pace intervals in Z3/low Z4) plus strides.

The reasoning, as I understand it:

- Z2 builds the aerobic engine (mitochondria, capillaries, fat oxidation) without piling up the fatigue that stops you hitting the next day's run.
- My old problem was running every "easy" run at 155–160. That's junk-miles territory: too hard to recover from, too easy to give much training effect.
- Keeping easy days properly easy meant I could actually hit my quality sessions hard, and I didn't miss a single run through niggles this time.

Honestly, the first two weeks were humbling. Staying under 150 meant walking up hills and getting passed by everyone on the canal path. Stick with it.

**How my easy pace changed**

This is the part that sold me. Average pace on flat Z2 runs, same loop, similar weather, HR held at 145–150:

| Week | Z2 pace (/km) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 6:48 |
| 2 | 6:45 |
| 3 | 6:36 |
| 4 | 6:30 |
| 5 | 6:21 |
| 6 | 6:15 |
| 7 | 6:09 |
| 8 | 6:02 |

So about **45 sec/km faster at the same heart rate** in eight weeks. Weeks 1–2 were basically flat. The improvement really kicked in from week 3. Some of the early gain is probably just getting used to running slower and more relaxed, but it kept improving steadily after that.

I also tracked cardiac drift on the long runs. By week 7, my HR over a 28 km long run only rose about 4 bpm across the whole run, compared with 10+ before.

**Caveats**

- One athlete, one block. Weather got cooler across the eight weeks, which helps.
- Heat, sleep, caffeine and stress all shift HR a lot. Some days I had to go really slow.
- Retest your LTHR every 6–8 weeks. Mine went up to 170 at the end.

Race is in three weeks. I'll report back whether I finally get past 30 km without the wheels coming off.
