# My First Raised-Bed Garden: What I Built, What I Filled It With, and What Surprised Me

Our backyard is about the size of a two-car garage, half of it patio. I'd assumed that meant no vegetable garden. This spring I built two raised beds anyway, and the results were good enough that I'm already planning a third.

## The beds

I went with cedar, 2x8 boards, two beds each 4 feet by 8 feet and 16 inches tall (two boards stacked). Cedar costs more than pine, but it resists rot without any treatment, and I didn't want to think about what pressure-treated lumber might leach into the soil. The corners are held with galvanised brackets, which made assembly a twenty-minute job with a drill and no cutting. I laid cardboard on the ground under each bed to smother the grass; it decomposed by midsummer.

I looked at galvanised metal kits, which are cheaper per square foot and last longer, but I wanted something I could modify. Sixteen inches turned out to be a good height: deep enough for root vegetables, low enough that I didn't need to fill it with a truck's worth of soil.

## The soil mix

This was the part I over-researched. I ended up with a rough thirds mix:

- One third topsoil, bought in bulk from a local landscape supplier
- One third compost, half from the municipal compost site and half bagged
- One third a blend of peat moss and perlite for drainage

Two beds at that size need roughly two cubic yards total. Buying it bagged would have cost nearly twice as much as having the topsoil and compost delivered loose. I mixed it in the beds with a garden fork, which was a workout, and topped it off after the first watering settled everything by about two inches.

I didn't test the soil the first year. I should have; I'll do it this fall.

## What did surprisingly well

**Bush beans.** I planted them because a neighbour had leftover seeds. They produced more than we could eat from about six square feet, and they kept going for two months. I'll plant a second sowing three weeks after the first next year to spread the harvest.

**Swiss chard.** I expected it to bolt in the heat. It didn't. We were still cutting leaves in October, and the more I cut, the more it grew. One packet of seeds was far too many.

**Cherry tomatoes.** Two plants, trellised on a cattle panel bent into an arch between the beds. They completely outperformed the full-size tomato plant, which produced four fruit and a lot of leaves. Next year it's cherry tomatoes only.

**Herbs along the edges.** Basil, parsley, and thyme planted around the perimeter of the beds used space I'd otherwise have wasted and kept the kitchen supplied all summer.

## What didn't

Zucchini, of all things. Powdery mildew took both plants by August. And the carrots came up stubby and forked, which I think means the mix was still lumpy where they were planted.

## What I'd tell a first-timer

Build one bed, not zero. The materials for one cedar bed and the soil to fill it ran me about $180, and the beans alone paid back a good part of that. The learning came from having it in the ground, not from reading about it, and there was a lot more to learn than I expected.
