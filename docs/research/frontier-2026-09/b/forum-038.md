**Re: Chain slack – how tight is too tight?**

Posted by **greaseandgears** » Tue Sep 22, 2026 8:14 pm

Seeing a few "my chain is noisy / my sprocket is hooked at 12k miles" threads lately, so here's the routine I use. It takes ten minutes and covers 90% of chain problems before they start.

**1. Find the tight spot first**

Chains don't wear evenly. Some links stretch more than others, so slack will vary as you rotate the wheel. Put the bike on a paddock stand (or side stand with the wheel just off the ground), spin the rear wheel slowly, and measure slack at several points around the chain. The **tightest** spot is the one you set to spec. If you adjust at a loose spot, the tight spot will end up over-tensioned, and that loads the countershaft bearing and output shaft seal. That's how you get a weeping seal at 20k.

Mark the tight link with a paint pen so you can find it next time.

**2. Measure it properly**

Slack is measured at the midpoint of the lower run, halfway between the front and rear sprockets. Push the chain up with a finger until it stops, note the position, let it drop, note the position. The difference is your slack. My bike calls for **25–35 mm** on the side stand; check your manual, because sport bikes with a lot of suspension travel can be 45+ mm. If you measure with the bike on a rear stand, the swingarm hangs lower, so be aware of which position the manual specifies.

**3. Adjust**

- Loosen the axle nut a turn or so. Don't remove it.
- Back off the adjuster locknuts, turn each adjuster the same amount. Count flats.
- Check the alignment marks on both sides of the swingarm match, then verify with a straight edge or a string along the chain run, because the stamped marks are often out by a millimetre or two.
- Snug the adjusters, re-check slack at the tight spot, then torque the axle nut.

**Axle nut torque on my 2019 Z900 is 108 Nm (80 ft-lb)**. Yours will differ – anywhere from 65 to 130 Nm is normal – so look it up. Use a real torque wrench. An under-torqued axle nut is a genuinely dangerous thing, and an over-torqued one crushes the spacers. Re-check slack after torquing, because tightening the nut can pull the adjusters slightly.

**4. Lube afterwards, not before**

Adjusting first, then lubing, means you're not smearing fresh lube everywhere while you work. Routine:

- Every 500–800 km, or after rain: wipe the chain down with a rag and a bit of kerosene, then apply lube to the **inside** of the lower run while spinning the wheel, so centrifugal force carries it outward onto the rollers and o-rings.
- Do it after a ride while the chain is warm; it penetrates better.
- Let it sit overnight before riding if you can. Otherwise, it flings onto the tyre and the rim.
- Every 3,000 km or so, a proper clean with a grunge brush and o-ring safe cleaner.

Do this and a decent chain and sprocket set should see 30k km. Neglect it and you'll be buying a kit every 15k.

Edit: forgot to say, clean the axle threads before torquing. Grit in there gives you a false reading.
