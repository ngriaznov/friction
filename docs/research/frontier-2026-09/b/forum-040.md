**8 weeks of Zone 2 before Chicago – what actually happened to my easy pace**

Posted by **slowtwitch_dan** » Mon Sep 21, 2026 7:02 pm

I've read a dozen threads here where someone asks "does Zone 2 training actually work or is it just a podcast thing", and the answers are usually theoretical. So here's one data point from a block I just finished. 41M, fourth marathon, previous best 3:38, and my training history is the classic "run every run at moderately hard" pattern.

**Estimating zones**

I didn't do a lab test. I used three things and cross-checked them:

1. A field max HR test: 3 x 3 minutes uphill, hard, after a long warm-up. Highest I saw was 186. The 220-minus-age formula would have given me 179, which would have put every zone about 4% too low.
2. Resting HR from my watch over a week: 52.
3. Karvonen (heart rate reserve) method: Zone 2 = 60–70% of HRR plus resting. That gave me **132–146 bpm**.

I also did a 30-minute time trial and took the last 20 minutes' average HR as a rough lactate threshold estimate (172). That lined up: my Z2 ceiling was about 85% of LTHR, which is where most of the Friel-style charts put it. Good enough. I set Z2 to 132–146 and used 146 as a hard cap for easy days.

**Why almost everything stayed in Zone 2**

Weekly volume went 55 → 58 → 62 → 50 (down week) → 65 → 68 → 72 → 55 km. Of that, roughly 80% by time was in Z2. One quality session a week (tempo or marathon-pace intervals), and one long run that was Z2 except for a few MP miles at the end in the last three weeks.

The reasoning was pretty simple. Every previous block I'd done ended with me tired, stagnating, and doing quality sessions at about 90% of what I should have because I was never recovered. Zone 2 was a forcing function: it made the easy days genuinely easy, so I could actually hit the hard days. The aerobic-base stuff (mitochondrial density, fat oxidation, capillary growth) is real, but honestly the biggest change was just that I stopped digging a hole on Tuesday and Thursday.

The first two weeks were humbling. To keep HR under 146 on a warm evening I was running 6:40–6:50/km, sometimes walking hills. Previous "easy" pace was 5:45. My ego took a hit and I got passed by a lot of people.

**How easy pace changed**

I logged the average pace of every flat Z2 run, same 10 km loop, similar conditions where possible:

- Weeks 1–2: 6:42/km average at 141 bpm
- Weeks 3–4: 6:28/km at 140 bpm
- Weeks 5–6: 6:11/km at 142 bpm
- Weeks 7–8: 5:58/km at 141 bpm

So about 44 seconds per km faster at the same heart rate over eight weeks. Some of that is cooler September weather, some is decoupling from initial fatigue, and I'd guess maybe half is genuine aerobic adaptation. Even so, that's a lot, and the more important thing is the quality sessions: my tempo pace went from 5:05 to 4:52/km at the same effort, and I finished the block feeling fresh rather than cooked.

Caveats: HR drifts with heat, caffeine, sleep and dehydration, so some days I ran by feel and ignored the watch. Optical wrist HR is garbage for intervals; I used a chest strap for everything. And n=1.

Race is in three weeks. Will report back.
