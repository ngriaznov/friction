## MoltenPath Firmware Troubleshooting Guide

Welcome to the MoltenPath troubleshooting guide! We've designed MoltenPath to be reliable and user-friendly, but even the best systems can encounter issues sometimes. This guide addresses three common problems: layer shifting, nozzle clogs, and bed adhesion failures.  Follow these steps for diagnosis and resolution. **Always turn off and unplug your printer before performing any physical adjustments or modifications.**

**1. Layer Shifting - The Wandering Print**

Layer shifting manifests as noticeable horizontal displacement of layers during a print. It’s often accompanied by a slight “stepping” sound.  This can be frustrating, leading to inaccurate dimensions and structural weakness in your prints.

**Possible Causes & Diagnostic Steps:**

* **Belt Tension:** Loose belts are the most frequent culprit. They allow the print head or bed to slip during movement.
    * **Diagnostic:** Gently push on each belt (X and Y). If they feel loose, floppy, or easily deflect, tensioning is needed. MoltenPath’s LCD display may have a "Belt Tension" diagnostic option – check your printer's specific configuration.
    * **Fix:**  Refer to your printer's manual for instructions on adjusting belt tension. Typically involves loosening the motor mount and tightening/loosening idler pulleys. Aim for tautness without being overly tight (a slight "twang" when plucked is a good indication).
* **Motor Current Issues:** Insufficient or excessive motor current can cause skipping steps.
    * **Diagnostic:** Check MoltenPath’s LCD menu under “Configuration” -> “Motors.”  Observe if the motors are unusually quiet, struggle to move, or make a clicking sound during printing – all signs of insufficient current. Too high current can lead to overheating and skipped steps as well.
    * **Fix:** Gradually increase/decrease motor currents (X and Y) in small increments (e.g., 0.10-0.20A).  Test with a small print after each adjustment until you find the optimal setting where motors move smoothly and powerfully without clicking or overheating. *Caution: Increasing current too much can damage motors.*
* **Resonance/Vibration:** Certain speeds and accelerations can cause vibrations that lead to layer shifts.
    * **Diagnostic:** Observe if shifting occurs at specific print speeds, particularly with fast movements. Listen for a rattling or buzzing sound during those moments.
    * **Fix:** Try reducing acceleration and jerk settings in MoltenPath's "Configuration" -> “Movement.”  Experiment incrementally; small changes can make a big difference. Consider dampening vibrations by adding foam padding under the printer frame.
* **Communication Errors:** Less common, but sometimes firmware or USB connection issues can cause errors impacting movement commands.
    * **Diagnostic:** Try a different USB cable and/or computer to rule out communication problems.  A full firmware reflash (see MoltenPath documentation) might be necessary in extreme cases.

**2. Nozzle Clogs - The Filament Freeze-Up**

Nozzle clogs prevent filament from extruding, leading to paused prints or a “bird’s nest” of tangled filament.

**Possible Causes & Diagnostic Steps:**

* **Heat Tower Issues:** Insufficient nozzle temperature or inadequate cooling can cause the filament to partially melt and clog.
    * **Diagnostic:**  Verify your print settings in MoltenPath (Slicer Profile) are using appropriate temperatures for your filament type. Check the hotend fan is working correctly – it should be blowing air *into* the hotend, not away from it. A thermistor malfunction can also cause incorrect temperature readings; consider testing with a spare.
    * **Fix:** Increase nozzle temperature in small increments (5-10°C). Clean the nozzle manually using a needle or cleaning wire while hot (be extremely careful!). Ensure proper cooling of the printed part and surrounding area.
* **Filament Quality/Moisture:**  Wet filament is notoriously prone to clogging.
    * **Diagnostic:** Examine the filament for signs of moisture absorption – it might look dull or feel brittle.
    * **Fix:** Dry your filament using a dedicated filament dryer, oven (at low temperature!), or even by exposing it to warm air from a hairdryer.  Store filament in an airtight container with desiccant packs.
* **Partial Jams:** Small debris or inconsistent filament diameter can cause partial clogs.
    * **Diagnostic:** Look for inconsistent extrusion during printing.
    * **Fix:** Cold pulls (cooling the nozzle, then pulling out the filament) can often remove small blockages.  Consider using a PTFE tube liner inside your hotend to improve filament flow and reduce clogging (check MoltenPath documentation for compatibility).

**3. Bed Adhesion Failures - The Print That Won't Stick**

This problem occurs when the first layer doesn’t adhere properly, leading to warping, curling, or the print detaching from the bed entirely.

**Possible Causes & Diagnostic Steps:**

* **Bed Leveling:** An uneven bed is a primary cause of adhesion issues.
    * **Diagnostic:** Use MoltenPath's auto-bed leveling (ABL) feature if available. If not, manually level the bed using a piece of paper as a feeler gauge at various points across the build plate.  The nozzle should be just barely touching the paper with slight resistance.
    * **Fix:** Repeat ABL or manual leveling until consistent nozzle height is achieved across the entire bed. MoltenPath's Z-offset adjustment can also fine-tune the initial layer height.
* **Bed Surface Cleanliness:** Oils and residue on the bed prevent proper adhesion.
    * **Diagnostic:** Visually inspect the bed for any visible dirt or grease.
    * **Fix:** Thoroughly clean the bed with isopropyl alcohol (IPA) *before every print*.  If using a specialized adhesive like glue stick or hairspray, apply it evenly and sparingly.
* **First Layer Print Settings:** Incorrect settings can negatively impact adhesion.
    * **Diagnostic:** Check MoltenPath's slicer profile for first layer height, width, speed, and temperature.
    * **Fix:** Increase first layer height slightly (0.2-0.3mm).  Widen the first layer line width by 10-20%. Reduce first layer print speed to around 20-30mm/s. Increase bed temperature by 5-10°C for PLA, and potentially higher for other materials.
