Subject: Important Update: Migrating Our Internal Wiki to MkDocs

Hi Team,

I’m writing to announce an exciting change regarding our internal documentation platform. We'll be migrating from Confluence to a self-hosted MkDocs setup over the next six weeks. This is a significant shift, and I want to clearly explain why we're making this move, what it means for you, and how we’ll approach the transition.

**Why are we moving away from Confluence?**

We've been using Confluence for quite some time now, and while it's served us well, recent developments have led us to re-evaluate our documentation strategy. Primarily, the escalating licensing costs associated with Confluence have become a significant concern. We’re committed to being fiscally responsible and exploring alternatives that deliver comparable value at a more sustainable price point.

Beyond cost, we’ve also identified areas where MkDocs offers demonstrable improvements:

*   **Improved Search Quality:** Many of you have expressed frustration with the search capabilities within Confluence. MkDocs, leveraging technologies like Algolia (to be integrated), promises significantly faster and more accurate search results.
*   **Git-Based Workflow & Review Process:** MkDocs utilizes Markdown files stored directly in our Git repository. This enables a streamlined review process mirroring our standard code workflows – pull requests, code reviews, and clear version history for all documentation changes.  This fosters collaboration and ensures higher quality content.
*   **Greater Customization & Control:** A self-hosted MkDocs setup provides us with more control over the platform’s appearance, functionality, and security.

**Migration Timeline & What to Expect**

The migration will be rolled out in phases over the next six weeks:

*   **Phase 1 (Week 1 - Next Week):** Initial Setup and Infrastructure – The MkDocs environment is being built and configured. You'll receive an invitation to access the new platform shortly.
*   **Phase 2 (Weeks 2-4):** Content Migration & Training – We’ll begin migrating a subset of content, prioritizing critical documentation first.  We will also schedule short training sessions on using MkDocs and Markdown. Details about these sessions will be shared next week.
*   **Phase 3 (Weeks 5-6):** Full Content Migration & Confluence Sunset – The remaining content will be migrated. We’ll formally sunset Confluence access at the end of Week 6, giving you sufficient time to adjust.

**What You Need To Do**

Over the next few weeks, we ask that all contributors take a moment to:

*   **Review Your Existing Pages:** Briefly review the Confluence pages you've created or significantly contributed to.  Consider whether they are still relevant and if they need updating.
*   **Prepare for Markdown:** Familiarize yourself with basic Markdown syntax. It’s straightforward, and we’ll provide resources and training. (Links to helpful guides will be shared shortly).
*   **Be Ready for Migration Notifications:** You'll receive notifications as your pages are migrated to MkDocs. Please review them upon arrival in the new platform.

**Questions & Support**

We understand this change may raise questions or concerns.  For any queries regarding the migration process, MkDocs usage, or anything related to the documentation platform, please contact [Documentation Team Email Address] or reach out directly to [Lead Engineer/Point of Contact Name]. We're here to help!

Thank you for your understanding and cooperation during this transition. We believe that moving to MkDocs will ultimately improve our internal knowledge sharing and collaboration.

Best regards,

[Your Name]
Engineering Manager

**Notes & Explanation of Choices:**

*   **Positive Framing:** The email starts with "exciting change" to soften the impact and focus on the benefits.
*   **Clear Reasoning:**  The 'Why' section is crucial. Directly addressing licensing costs and search quality concerns shows transparency and explains *why* this change is happening.
*   **Specificity:** Calling out Git-based workflow highlights a key benefit for engineers.
*   **Phased Timeline:** Breaking the migration into phases makes it less daunting and allows for adjustments along the way.  Specific weeks give people anchors to plan around.
*   **Actionable Steps:** The "What You Need To Do" section gives clear instructions, even if they’re relatively simple.
*   **Support Contact:** Providing a dedicated email address (and a point of contact) shows commitment to supporting the team through this change.  Consider setting up a Slack channel for quicker questions as well.
*   **Markdown Introduction**: Mentioning Markdown is important, and offering resources will ease adoption.
*   **Sunset Date:** Giving a firm sunset date for Confluence helps drive the transition.
*   **Tone:** The tone aims to be professional but approachable.  Avoid overly technical jargon where possible.
* **Consider a Demo/Preview**: If feasible, consider setting up a small preview instance of MkDocs with a few sample pages so people can get a feel for it before the full migration starts.
