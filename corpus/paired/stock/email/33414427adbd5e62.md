Subject: New Build Reveal! Walnut Dining Table - Lessons Learned & Lots of Grain!

Hey everyone,

Just wanted to share my latest project – a walnut dining table I finally finished up! It’s been a bit of a journey, and I thought you all might enjoy hearing about it, especially with some of the challenges (and triumphs!) along the way.

I've been wanting to build a solid wood dining table for ages, and figured now was the time. The biggest hurdle, as always, was sourcing the lumber. I spent weeks scouring local sawmills and lumber yards searching for something that really spoke to me.  I wanted something with beautiful grain patterns but also good stability – walnut seemed like the perfect fit. 

Eventually, I found a small supplier who had some gorgeous black walnut slabs harvested from a downed tree nearby. They weren't perfectly clear (a few knots and minor imperfections), which actually added character in my opinion!  I ended up selecting four pieces for the legs and apron, and a slightly wider slab for the tabletop itself. The color variation within the walnut – ranging from deep chocolate to lighter browns with warm undertones – is just stunning, and I really tried to showcase that in the design.

The table is fairly simple in its construction: mortise-and-tenon joinery for the legs and apron, with a breadboard end detail on the tabletop. This was my first time incorporating breadboards, and while it added some complexity, I’m incredibly happy with how they turned out – they really accentuate the beauty of the top.

Now, no build is without its hiccups, right?  I definitely had one memorable moment during glue-up that tested my patience (and a fair amount of sandpaper). When assembling the legs and apron, I wasn't paying close enough attention to grain direction on one of the legs, and ended up with a slight warp after clamping. It was heartbreaking to see it, but thankfully I caught it relatively early.  The fix? Well, let’s just say I got reacquainted with my jointer and hand planer! A few passes later, and the leg was perfectly straight again – albeit a little thinner than originally planned. Lesson learned: always double-check grain direction *before* you commit to glue! It's amazing how much you can learn from those moments of frustration.

For the finish, I opted for a combination oil/wax blend.  I used three coats of Rubio Monocoat Pure Oil Plus 2K, followed by two coats of Renaissance Wax. The Rubio is incredibly easy to apply and provides a really durable matte finish that highlights the natural beauty of the wood without any glossy sheen. The Renaissance wax adds an extra layer of protection and gives it a subtle feel. I was aiming for something low-maintenance that would age gracefully – hopefully, this combination will do the trick!

Overall, the entire build took about 45 hours spread out over three weeks. That includes time spent sourcing lumber, milling it down to size, cutting joinery, assembly, sanding (so much sanding!), finishing, and of course, all the little tweaks and adjustments along the way.  It felt like a marathon!

I’ve attached a few photos so you can see the finished table. I'd love to hear your thoughts – especially if anyone has experience with breadboard ends or Rubio Monocoat finishes. Always looking for tips and tricks from fellow makers!

Happy building, everyone!
