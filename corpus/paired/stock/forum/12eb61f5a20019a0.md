Hey everyone,

Just wanted to share my recent home lab NAS build. I've been lurking here for a while, absorbing all the collective wisdom, and figured it’s time to contribute back now that I'm finally done (and learned a *lot* in the process!).

My goal was a reliable, high-capacity storage solution for backups of VMs, media files (movies/music), and general file sharing around the house.  I wasn’t aiming for absolute bleeding-edge performance - data integrity and uptime were priority one.

**The RAID Choice: Why RAIDZ2 over Mirroring?**

Initially, I considered mirrored vdevs (RAID1). It seemed straightforward enough – simple recovery, good read performance. However, after really digging into the math, especially considering my budget and desired capacity, RAIDZ2 just made more sense.

With mirroring, you essentially lose half your raw drive space to redundancy. With eight drives in a mirrored setup, I'd only get 4TB of usable storage – which felt limiting for what I wanted.  RAIDZ2 with eight drives gives me around 16TB usable (assuming 18TB drives), and the double parity offers significantly better fault tolerance than RAID1. Losing *two* drives in a mirrored setup leaves your data completely vulnerable, while RAIDZ2 can handle two drive failures gracefully.

The performance hit on writes isn’t huge with Z2, especially if you're not constantly hammering it.  Read performance is generally very good as well. The increased usable space and better redundancy outweighed the slight write penalty for my needs. I also felt comfortable with the complexity of RAIDZ2 after reading a bunch of guides here!

**Ashift - The Painful Lesson**

Now, let’s talk about Ashift.  This was where I made a *significant* blunder initially. My drives are WD Red Pro 18TB (the newer ones). I assumed, based on some older documentation I found online (mistake #1!), that the standard default Ashift value of 6 would be fine.

The first few days were… weird.  I’d get random, inconsistent performance, and noticed a lot of retries in the SMART data. It wasn't catastrophic, but it was unnerving. I started digging deeper into WD's documentation (mistake #2 - should have done this *before* building!), specifically for the Red Pro line.

Turns out, these drives require an Ashift value of **9**.  Seriously! A difference of just three sectors made a noticeable impact on performance and introduced unnecessary stress on the system. I feel incredibly foolish for not verifying this properly beforehand. Switching to Ashift 9 resolved all those issues - performance normalized, retries disappeared.

I learned my lesson: ALWAYS check the manufacturer's documentation for your specific drive model regarding Ashift! Don’t rely on generic guides or forum posts – they might be outdated or inaccurate.  The impact of getting this wrong can be subtle but damaging over time.

**Hardware Details:**

*   **Drives:** 8 x WD Red Pro 18TB
*   **Controller:** Microchip RockPro64 (running OpenMediaVault) - I wanted a low-power, flexible solution.
*   **RAM:** 8GB
*   **Case:** Fractal Design Define R5 (modified for better airflow)

**If I Had Hindsight…**

Looking back, here's what I would do differently:

1.  **Thoroughly Research Ashift *Before* Building:** This is the big one. It cost me a few days of troubleshooting and unnecessary stress.
2. **Test Drive Compatibility:** Ideally, I’d have tested a smaller RAIDZ2 array with just 2-3 drives initially to confirm compatibility and performance *before* committing to the full eight-drive build.  A bit more time upfront could have saved me headaches later.
3. **Consider Power Consumption:** These Red Pros aren't exactly power sippers. I’ll be monitoring power consumption closely and might consider switching to some lower-power drives in the future if needed.

**Conclusion**

Overall, I’m happy with my NAS build. It’s serving its purpose well, providing reliable storage for my home lab needs.  Hopefully, sharing my experience – especially the Ashift debacle – will help someone else avoid a similar pitfall.  Feel free to ask any questions!
