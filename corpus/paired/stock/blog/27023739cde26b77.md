## From Ouch to Okay: How We Slashed Our AWS Bill by 50% with Right-Sizing & Spot Instances

Running a small business means constantly juggling priorities – building features, keeping customers happy, and… staring nervously at your AWS bill.  We felt that pain acutely here at [Your Company Name]. For months, our monthly cloud spend felt like an unpredictable beast, consistently hovering around $1800-$2000. It wasn't crippling, but it was a significant chunk of our budget and felt unsustainable as we grew.

Then we decided to tackle it head-on.  The good news? We weren’t doing anything *wrong*, just inefficiently. Through careful analysis and some strategic adjustments, we managed to cut that monthly bill roughly in half – saving us around $900-$1000 every month! This post details how we did it, so you can potentially replicate the process for your own AWS environment.

**The Problem: Over-Provisioned & Unaware**

Our initial setup was pretty typical for a startup: we launched EC2 instances with sizes that felt "big enough" to handle anticipated load.  We figured it's better to have headroom than to risk performance issues, especially early on. We also had several batch processing jobs – things like report generation, data imports, and image resizing – running regularly. These were typically thrown at standard EC2 instances because we didn’t really think about alternatives.

The problem? Those "big enough" instances were often idling.  And those batch jobs were consuming resources during peak times unnecessarily. We weren't actively monitoring utilization or optimizing our resource allocation.  We were essentially paying for power we weren’t using.

**Phase 1: Right-Sizing EC2 Instances – A Deep Dive**

The first and most impactful step was rightsizing our core application servers. This involved a combination of careful monitoring and some educated guesses, followed by iterative adjustments.

* **Monitoring is Key:** We started using CloudWatch metrics to track CPU utilization, memory usage, disk I/O, and network throughput for *every* EC2 instance.  We looked at averages over various timeframes (1 hour, 5 minutes, 1 day) to get a comprehensive picture of their behavior.
* **Identifying the Candidates:** We identified several instances consistently running below 30% CPU utilization during peak hours. These were prime candidates for downsizing.
* **The Downsizing Process:**  We didn't just shrink them randomly! For each instance, we researched alternative EC2 instance types with similar performance characteristics but a lower cost (e.g., moving from `m5.large` to `t3.medium`). We then spun up a test environment and meticulously tested the new instance size under load *before* making any changes to production.  This is crucial!
* **Example:** Our primary web server was running on an `m5.large`. After monitoring, we discovered it could comfortably operate on a `t3.medium` without impacting performance. This alone saved us roughly $60/month per instance (a significant difference!).

**Phase 2: Embracing Spot Instances for Batch Jobs – A Risky but Rewarding Move**

Our batch processing jobs were the next target.  These weren't mission-critical and could tolerate interruptions, making them perfect candidates for Spot Instances.

* **Understanding Spot Instances:** Spot Instances allow you to bid on unused EC2 capacity at a significant discount (often 70-90% cheaper than On-Demand). The catch? Amazon can terminate these instances with short notice if the spot price exceeds your bid.
* **Job Design for Resilience:** To mitigate the risk of termination, we redesigned our batch jobs to be:
    * **Checkpointed Regularly:**  We implemented a system that periodically saved progress so that interrupted jobs could resume from where they left off.
    * **Idempotent:** Designed to handle repeated executions without causing unintended side effects.
    * **Distributed:** We split large tasks into smaller, independent sub-tasks that can be executed on multiple Spot Instances concurrently.
* **Spot Fleet Automation:**  We used AWS Auto Scaling and Spot Fleets to manage our Spot Instance pools. This allowed us to automatically request instances based on pricing and availability, ensuring jobs continued running even if some instances were terminated.
