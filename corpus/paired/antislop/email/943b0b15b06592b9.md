**Subject: Important Announcement: Transitioning from Confluence to MkDocs for Internal Documentation**

Hi Team,

This announcement details an upcoming change that will impact how we manage our internal documentation. We’ll be migrating away from our current platform, Atlassian Confluence, and adopting a self-hosted MkDocs setup over the next six weeks.

We've been evaluating several options to improve our documentation system, focusing on cost efficiency, performance, and collaboration processes. While Confluence has served us well in the past, its ongoing licensing costs have become increasingly significant. More importantly, we’ve identified challenges with search quality and limitations regarding version control for consistent review of changes.

Our decision to move to MkDocs is driven by these factors:

*   **Cost Savings:** Self-hosting MkDocs significantly reduces our operational expenses associated with software licenses. These savings can be reinvested in other key areas of development.
*   **Improved Searchability:** We believe the underlying architecture and indexing capabilities of MkDocs will allow for a noticeably faster and more precise search experience when looking for critical information.
*   **Version Control & Collaboration:** MkDocs uses Markdown files stored directly within our Git repositories. This allows us to leverage existing workflows – code review, branching, merging – to manage changes, facilitate collaboration, and maintain high quality documentation just like we do with our application codebase. Every edit is tracked, easily reviewed, and auditable.
*   **Customization & Control:** Hosting the system ourselves provides greater flexibility for customization and integration with other internal tools as needed.

**Migration Timeline & What to Expect**

The migration will happen in phases over the next six weeks:

*   **Week 1 (Next Week):** Introduction of the new MkDocs instance, accessible at [Insert URL Here]. We'll publish a basic framework and provide introductory guides for contributing.
*   **Weeks 2-4:** Focused content migration. Key areas will be prioritized based on usage frequency and importance.  We’ll have weekly sync sessions (details below) to monitor progress and address challenges.
*   **Weeks 5-6:** Final data cleanup, refinement of the MkDocs theme/structure, and sunsetting of the Confluence instance.

If you are a current contributor to our internal documentation in Confluence:

1.  **Familiarize Yourself:** Please take some time this week to explore the new MkDocs environment at [Insert URL Here] and review the introductory guide: [Link to Introductory Guide - very important!].

3.  **Submit via Pull Request:** Once exported, submit these Markdown files (along with any necessary accompanying assets) through a new pull request against the `[Documentation Repo Name]` repository on GitHub. We’ll provide templates and guidelines for creating effective documentation entries.
4. **Don't directly edit in Confluence during migration**: To avoid conflicts, please refrain from making updates to existing pages within Confluence after [Date].

**Support & Questions**

We understand this is a shift in process, and you may have questions.  Here are the resources available:

*   **Weekly Sync Sessions:** Every [Day] at [Time], we'll hold brief team syncs dedicated to addressing migration-related queries and reviewing progress. Link will be shared closer to the time.

**Explanation of Considerations:**

*   **Positive Framing:** Focused on the benefits (cost savings, better features) rather than just "we're getting rid of Confluence."
*   **Specificity:**  Filling in bracketed sections with concrete details is crucial. The intro guide and slack channel are vital to ease adoption.
*   **Actionable Items:** Clearly outlines what contributors need to *do*.
*   **Timeline Clarity:** Gives a phased approach so it's less overwhelming.
*   **Support Channels:** Provides multiple avenues for questions. Naming specific contacts is more helpful than just a generic email address.
*   **Respectful Tone:** Acknowledges that change can be disruptive and offers support.
*  **Freezing Confluence**: Setting a firm deadline to prevent edits in the old system will help avoid conflicts when importing.
