**Subject: Fresh off the Saw – Walnut Dining Table Build!**

Hi Everyone,

Just wanted to share something I’ve been pouring a lot of time and effort into recently – a walnut dining table! It’s finally finished and in place, and I thought you all would appreciate seeing it. I know we're always inspiring each other with our projects, so hopefully this can provide some ideas or just offer a bit of maker motivation.

The whole thing started about two months ago when I decided to tackle a larger piece than I usually do. A dining table has been on my "to-build" list for ages, and I figured now was the time. The biggest hurdle was sourcing the walnut. It took quite a few trips to different local sawmills and lumber suppliers before I found what I considered suitable.  Honestly, it’s amazing how much variation you see even in “same species” wood! 

I ended up settling on six planks of black walnut, each about 8' long and roughly 1" thick. The figuring was really interesting - a nice combination of straight grain with ribbons of tiger stripe – I aimed to lay them out in a pattern that would show this off nicely across the tabletop. Choosing those six boards felt less like buying lumber and more like playing matchmaker! They needed to feel *right* together, both visually and regarding how they would move as one panel.

The design is fairly simple: a live-edge slab tabletop attached to four turned legs connected by stretcher rails.  I wanted it to have that “honest” handmade quality – classic but without unnecessary flourishes. The dimensions ended up being about 6’ long, 36" wide, and 30" high.

The glue-up process for the tabletop was definitely a lesson in humility (and patience!). I had planned out my board layout meticulously, making sure to match grain patterns and account for potential movement… but somewhere along the line, I miscalculated a critical clamping pressure distribution. The result? One of the legs separated slightly from the table apron during glue-up.  It was a really frustrating moment – seeing hours of work potentially compromised.

Thankfully, it wasn't catastrophic. After letting everything dry completely and assessing the damage, I ended up having to remove that leg, flatten its surface with my jointer (and then re-plane it a bit) before reclamping. It added an extra day or two to the project and definitely set me back a little in my timeline but thankfully no serious issues resulted from this. 

For the finish, I opted for a combination of Rubio Monocoat Pure Oil Plus 2C. It’s been on my list of things to try out because it gives a beautiful matte finish that really showcases the natural color and grain of the walnut. The application process was pretty straightforward - three coats with adequate drying time in between.  I really appreciate how easy it is to maintain – just a simple soap-and-water cleaning keeps it looking great, without needing any harsh chemicals or reapplications.

The whole project, from sourcing the lumber to the final finishing touches, took about 75 hours distributed across several weekends. That includes planning, milling (I did rough cut all the lumber myself at home), joinery, assembly, and finishing. It’s a significant time commitment, but honestly, it was really rewarding seeing it come together.

I've attached some pictures – I hope you enjoy them!  Would love to hear your thoughts or if any of you have tackled similar builds recently. Always looking for tips and tricks from the community. 

Happy making!
