We’re currently awaiting legal approval for our third-party analytics Software Development Kit (SDK). *  **Communication with Apple Developer Support:** Should significant delays emerge, we will engage directly with their support team to inquire on status and identify possible expediting options.

**Explanation of Choices & Considerations:**

*   **Tone:** The overall tone is positive and reassuring, focusing on accomplishments and proactive management of challenges.  It avoids overly technical jargon.
*   **“Analytics SDK” Explanation:** I’ve used a simplified explanation because “SDK” can be meaningless to non-technical people.  Highlighting *why* it's needed (measuring performance) is key.
*   **"What Shipped":** Leading with success builds confidence and demonstrates momentum. The attached video demonstration allows stakeholders to quickly see the change.
*   **“Blocked” – Transparency:** It’s important to be upfront about blockers, but framing it positively ("pending approval") and providing an estimated timeline helps manage expectations.  Explaining *why* something is blocked (legal compliance) provides context.
*   **"Next Week":** Concise list of priorities allows for easy tracking.
*   **Risk Section:** Acknowledging the risk of App Store delays demonstrates awareness and preparedness. Offering mitigation strategies shows that you're taking steps to minimize impact. Using a realistic timeframe ("typically 3-5 business days") rather than overly optimistic predictions builds trust.
* **Metrics & KPIs**: It’s good, without boring them with technical detail, to mention key metrics that will be tracked after the redesign is launched.



**To further tailor this email:**

*   **Know Your Audience's Technical Literacy:** Adjust the level of explanation accordingly. If your stakeholders are *completely* non-technical, you might need even simpler explanations.
*   **Specific KPIs:** Replace “key performance indicators” with more concrete examples relevant to their interests (e.g., "increase in account creation rate").
*  **Severity/Impact Classification**: You may consider adding a simple risk classification system (Low/Medium/High) to the risk section for ease of understanding.
