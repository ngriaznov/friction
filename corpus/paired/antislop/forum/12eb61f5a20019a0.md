Hey everyone,

Been lurking here for ages getting ideas and soaking up the wisdom. Finally taking the plunge and launching my own little homelab NAS – been dreaming about this for a while! I figured I'd document it as I go along and hopefully share some useful thoughts/experiences in case anyone else is considering something similar.  This post focuses specifically on why I decided to use RAIDZ2 with 8 drives, what drove the shift value choice, and honestly – where I might have gone wrong.

**The Storage Challenge:**

My primary needs were (and will continue to be) a combination of media storage (movies/music), software backups (think Docker containers & VMs), and a place for various project archives.  Capacity is important, but *availability* and *data protection* are the highest priorities. I'm comfortable with loss situations – losing files can often be recovered from somewhere - it’s catastrophic system failure that worries me most.

**Why RAIDZ2 8x4TB? The RAID Calculus:**

I seriously considered mirrored vdevs (RAID1) for a while, especially because of the relatively simple recovery process and performance advantages during rebuilds. But after crunching numbers, I realized they fall short in overall capacity efficiency for my budget & future expansion goals. With 8 drives in a mirrored setup, you’re only getting two times the capacity of your smallest drive.  That becomes very expensive *fast*.

RAIDZ2 offers significantly better capacity density – 75% usable space with this configuration. It provides double parity protection which is what really sold me. RAID1 can tolerate one failure *per vdev*, a RAIDZ2 can handle two simultaneous failures in the array.  While redundancy is great, I'm more comfortable dealing with the (lower) probability of 2 independent drive failures than being completely crippled by just one disk going south.

The performance trade-off? Yes, rebuilds are slower and potentially impact throughput during that period. However:

*   My workload isn’t heavily transactional - mostly streaming and archival storage.
*  I'm using SATA drives, I won’t be looking at crazy high IOPS anyway. 
*  The redundancy gains outweigh the performance penalty for *my* needs.

**Ashift Values – Getting It Right (Eventually):**

Choosing the correct `ashift` value was a bit of a journey! My chosen drives are Seagate IronWolf 4TB models. Most modern drives use a 4KB sector size, but they’re still using Advanced Format (AF) which necessitated setting an appropriate `ashift` for optimal performance in ZFS.

I started with the "safe" default – 6. I monitored drive performance closely after the initial array creation. Performance was acceptable but… *off*.  Through a combination of monitoring reports via `zpool iostat`, and researching online discussions relating to known issues with Seagate drives on certain controllers, I discovered that these specific IronWolf models perform much better (higher sequential read/write speeds) when set to `ashift=12`.

There's no easy way than empirical testing to determine the best `ashift` value. It really involves understanding how your controller interacts with the drive’s firmware in combination.  I was lucky enough that I had a spare system and time to test different settings (very crucial).

**What Would I Do Differently?**

If I were doing this over, here are few things I would revisit:

*   **Controller Choice:** My motherboard has an older SATA3 controller. Now knowing the sensitivity of these Seagate drives to sector alignment, a dedicated hardware RAID/SAS controller might have given me even better baseline performance and reliability in dealing with those edge-cases.
*  **Monitoring Tools**: While `zpool status` is essential, expanding my monitoring beyond that – incorporating things like SMART data collection for more granular reporting - would be a smart next step.
* **Testing Methodology:** My testing involved just throwing a bunch of files at the system. A formal performance test suite (e.g., FIO) to establish a true baseline and easily track changes with `ashift` modifications would have been much better.

**Current Setup Details:**

*   Hardware: Old motherboard, using built-in SATA3 controller
*   Array Type: RAIDZ2
*   Drives: 8x Seagate IronWolf 4TB (ST4000VN000)
*   `ashift`: 12
*   Filesystem: ZFS
* Operating System: Debian 11

I’m still in the early stages, but so far, I am really happy with my setup. I'd love to hear your opinions and experiences regarding this or similar setups!

Cheers!
