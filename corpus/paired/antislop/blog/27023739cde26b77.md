## From Sticker Shock to Savings: How We Cut Our AWS Bill in Half (and You Can Too)

We've all been there. That monthly AWS bill arrives, and you experience that sinking feeling – a moment of sticker shock followed by a review process filled with anxiety. For our team at [Your Company/Team Name], we reached this point several months ago. Our growth had led to increased usage, but it also meant a significant jump in costs. We needed to take action.

This post details how a small engineering team (around 6 people) systematically reviewed and optimized our AWS infrastructure, ultimately cutting our monthly bill roughly in half – moving from approximately $10,000/month to just over $5,000/month. This wasn't about massive architecture changes; it was focused effort on identifying inefficiencies at a component level.

**The Problem: Steady Growth & Unchecked Costs**

As our application gained popularity (and load!), we relied heavily on EC2 instances for both core services and batch processing jobs. Initially, we’d launched reasonably-sized machines, but as usage climbed, we scaled *up* – often adding more powerful (and expensive) instances without truly evaluating if they were necessary.  Our monitoring showed decent utilization during peak hours, which was used to justify the larger sizes, but this neglected a significant portion of our monthly uptime where resources sat relatively idle.

**The Strategy: Focused Optimization in Two Main Areas**

We broke down our optimization strategy into two key areas: **EC2 Rightsizing** and **Spot Instance Migration for Batch Jobs**.  Here’s how we tackled each one, followed by the overall results.

**1. EC2 Rightsizing - Finding the 'Just Right' Size:**

This was the biggest lever for immediate savings. We used a combination of tools and investigation:

*   **AWS Compute Optimizer:** This built-in AWS service analyzes your EC2 usage patterns and provides recommendations for optimal instance types and sizes. It flagged several instances that were significantly oversized.
*   **CloudWatch Monitoring:**  We closely monitored CPU utilization, memory consumption, network I/O, and disk I/O across all our production instances *over a longer period* (a couple of weeks) to identify true peak demands and average loads.
*   **Load Testing:** For critical services, we conducted controlled load tests using tools like ApacheBench or Locust to precisely measure performance at different instance sizes. This helped confirm if smaller instances could handle realistic workloads without degradation.
*   **Iterative Adjustment & Monitoring:**  We *didn’t* make wholesale changes all at once. We reduced the size of a few representative instances first, monitored their performance intensely for several days, and only moved on when confident in their stability.

**What we changed (examples):**

*   Several `m5.xlarge` instances were downgraded to `m5.medium`, retaining acceptable performance.
*   One particularly oversized database server (`c5.4xlarge`) was successfully transitioned to a `db.r6g.large` managed RDS instance, which provided better cost/performance characteristics while handling our workload efficiently. (Note: This also reduced operational overhead).
*   We identified non-critical development environments that could be scaled down or even stopped entirely during off-peak hours.

**2. Spot Instance Migration for Batch Jobs - Embracing Intermittent Availability:**

Our batch processing jobs – things like daily reporting, image resizing, and occasional data migrations – were running on standard `t3.medium` instances. These jobs are generally resilient to interruption (designed to be retriable) and *don’t* need to run at specific times.  Spot Instances offered a huge opportunity here.

*   **Understanding Spot Pricing:** We educated ourselves thoroughly about Spot Instance pricing, bid strategies (capacity optimized vs. price prioritized), and the risks of interruption.
*   **Job Design for Interruption Tolerance:** Our engineers modified our batch jobs to be idempotent – meaning they could safely restart from where they left off without causing corruption or data loss. This is *critical*. We used a combination of check-pointing, queueing (SQS) and task orchestration (AWS Step Functions).
*   **Spot Instance Strategy:**  We utilized AWS Batch to manage our spot requests, letting the system automatically handle scaling up/down based on price and availability. The “capacity optimized” bidding strategy worked extremely well for us because it prioritized getting access to available spot capacity instead of chasing a specific low price.

**Lessons Learned & Key Takeaways**

*   **Monitoring is Paramount:** You can't optimize what you don’t measure. Detailed monitoring of your entire AWS environment is vital.
*   **Rightsize, Regularly:** Don’t assume instances are correctly sized because they “worked” when launched. Regularly review and adjust based on actual utilization.
*   **Spot Instances Require Thoughtful Design:**  Batch jobs are prime candidates for Spot Instances, but make sure your processes are interruption-tolerant.
*   **Automation Helps:** Investing in infrastructure-as-code (IaC) using tools like Terraform or CloudFormation makes rightsizing and spot instance management much more manageable at scale.
* **Continuous Optimization:** This isn't a one-time project.  Regularly audit your AWS spending and processes to identify new optimization opportunities.
