You asked specifically whether the waker handling is correct, so let me focus there rather than relitigating the whole executor design.

Short answer: mostly, but there's a lost-wakeup race. Your `Task::poll` path clears the "notified" flag before calling `Future::poll`, which is the right order in principle (clear-then-poll, not poll-then-clear, avoids missing a wakeup that arrives during the poll itself). The problem is that the flag clear and the mio registration aren't tied together atomically — if the waker fires between when you clear the flag and when the underlying future actually calls `Context::waker().wake_by_ref()` inside `poll` to re-register interest with epoll, and that race window lands wrong, you can end up with a task that's neither in the ready queue nor still registered for the event that would have woken it, and it just stalls forever. This is the classic reason `Waker` implementations for hand-rolled executors need the "polling" tri-state (idle/polling/repoll) rather than a plain bool — when a wake comes in while the task is already being polled, you need to remember to requeue it immediately after the poll returns rather than trusting the in-flight poll to have seen it. Worth looking at how `futures-util`'s `AtomicWaker` or async-task's internal state machine handle this; it's a genuinely fiddly bit of code that's easy to get subtly wrong, and I'd trust neither of us to eyeball-verify it without a stress test that runs many tasks with artificially delayed wakes.

Separately: `Waker::wake` is implemented by cloning an `Arc<Task>` and pushing it onto the ready queue, which allocates on every wake. That's not wrong, but under load with many small futures waking frequently it'll show up in profiles; an intrusive linked-list node embedded in the task (avoiding the queue's own allocation) is the usual next step once this is otherwise correct.

I'd get the lost-wakeup case fixed first — that's a correctness bug, not a nice-to-have — before worrying about the allocation.
