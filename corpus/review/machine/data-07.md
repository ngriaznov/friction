Good progress on getting this training loop working end to end for the image classifier, but there are a few correctness and reproducibility issues I'd want addressed before treating any results from this script as meaningful.

Biggest one: I don't see a seed being set anywhere — no `torch.manual_seed`, no `numpy.random.seed`, and the `DataLoader` for training uses `shuffle=True` with no generator passed. That means two runs of this exact script on identical data can produce meaningfully different final accuracy just from initialization and batch ordering, which makes it impossible to tell whether a change you make later actually helped or you just got a lucky seed. Pin the seed (and note that full determinism on GPU also needs `torch.backends.cudnn.deterministic = True`, with the understanding that costs you some speed).

Second, the train/validation split: `train_data, val_data = random_split(dataset, [0.8, 0.2])` is applied after the dataset already has your augmentation transforms attached in `__init__`. That means validation images are going through the same random crops/flips as training images, which adds noise to your validation metric and makes it a less reliable signal for early stopping decisions. Construct two dataset instances — one with train-time augmentation, one with only the deterministic eval-time transform (resize/normalize) — and split the underlying indices, not the already-transformed dataset.

Third, the checkpoint logic saves the model at the end of every epoch unconditionally (`torch.save(model.state_dict(), f"checkpoint_epoch{epoch}.pt")`), which is going to fill disk fast over a long training run and also means you're not actually tracking which checkpoint was best — you'd have to go back and check validation logs to figure out which epoch to load for inference. Track `best_val_loss` across epochs and only overwrite a single `best_model.pt` when the current epoch improves on it; keep the periodic per-epoch saves only if you specifically want resumability, and even then consider keeping just the last one or two rather than all of them.

Smaller: the loss is being printed but not logged anywhere persistent (no TensorBoard, no CSV, no experiment tracker), so once the terminal session closes that training history is gone. Worth at least writing epoch/loss/val_metric to a CSV even if you're not ready to wire up a full tracking tool.

The model architecture and optimizer choice look fine, and the overall loop structure (forward, loss, backward, step, zero_grad) is written correctly with no obvious ordering bugs. It's the reproducibility and validation contamination issues that need fixing first.
