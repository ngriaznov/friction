The author's code sends outbound webhooks to customer-configured URLs when an internal event fires (order shipped), using `requests.post` in a Celery task with `retry` on any non-2xx response, and asked for a review before rolling it out more broadly.

The retry-on-failure behavior is a reasonable instinct but as implemented it will cause real problems for your customers' systems. There's no idempotency key included in the payload or headers — every retry sends what looks, from the receiving end, like a brand new event. If a customer's endpoint actually processed the first delivery but was slow to respond (timeout on your side, success on theirs) or returned a 200 after already having taken effect, your retry causes them to double-process "order shipped," which for something like triggering a shipping notification email or updating an external inventory count is a real, customer-visible bug, not just wasted work. Add a stable event ID (a UUID generated once when the event is first created, not regenerated per retry) to the payload — most webhook consumers key their own dedupe logic off exactly this, and it's a pretty firm expectation for anyone who's built webhook receivers before (Stripe, GitHub, etc. all do this).

Second, the retry policy itself: `self.retry(exc=exc, countdown=5)` with a fixed 5-second delay and, from what's shown, `max_retries` either unset (infinite) or a flat count with no backoff. Fixed-interval retries against a downstream that's struggling (rather than just having one blip) turns your retry traffic into a steady hammering pattern instead of giving it room to recover. Switch to exponential backoff with jitter — Celery supports this natively via `retry_backoff=True, retry_backoff_max=600, retry_jitter=True` on the task decorator — and cap `max_retries` at something bounded (Stripe uses roughly 3 days of exponential backoff as a reference point, though your product's tolerance may differ) rather than retrying forever.

Third: you're retrying on any non-2xx, including 4xx responses. A 400 or 404 from the customer's endpoint almost always means the request itself is malformed or the endpoint is gone — retrying it with the same payload will fail identically every time and just burns worker time. Only retry on 5xx, timeouts, and connection errors; treat 4xx as a terminal failure and surface it (dashboard, email, whatever your customer-facing signal is) rather than silently retrying into a dead end.

Smaller items: no explicit `timeout` argument on `requests.post`, so a hung customer endpoint can tie up a worker indefinitely — set a connect/read timeout pair. And consider signing the payload (HMAC with a per-customer secret, sent as a header) if you don't already, since right now anyone who guesses or intercepts a customer's webhook URL can forge events, and there's no way for a receiver to verify authenticity.

Given the idempotency gap and the missing 4xx/5xx distinction, I'd hold this back from broader rollout until those are addressed — they're the two most likely to cause customer-visible incidents.
