The main thing I'd change is the `Optional<String> middleName` field on the `Customer` entity itself. `Optional` was designed as a return type to signal "this method might not have a value," not as a field type — it's not `Serializable`, so if anything about this entity ever needs to serialize (caching, session replication, even certain JPA providers doing dirty-checking tricks under the hood), you'll hit friction. It also doesn't buy you anything a plain nullable `String` doesn't, since JPA has to persist and rehydrate it as a plain column either way — you'd end up unwrapping it right back to nullable at the persistence boundary regardless.

Keep the field as a plain `String`, and if you want the `Optional` ergonomics, expose them at the getter: `getMiddleName()` returning `Optional.ofNullable(middleName)` is the idiomatic spot for it, and it's exactly what callers like `formatFullName()` further down are already doing manually with a null check — that method can simplify to `.map()`/`.orElse()` once the getter returns `Optional`.

Separately, `getPreferredContact().get()` in `NotificationDispatcher` will throw `NoSuchElementException` with a useless message if that Optional is empty, which based on `CustomerService.create()` looks entirely possible for customers created without a contact preference. Either guard it with `.orElseThrow(() -> new IllegalStateException("..."))` for a clearer failure, or better, `.ifPresentOrElse(...)` if there's a sane fallback behavior (default to email, say) rather than failing at all.
