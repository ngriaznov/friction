Reviewed the new `fct_revenue_recognition` fact table and its supporting dbt model. The general approach — spreading subscription revenue across the service period rather than recognizing it all on the invoice date — is the right accounting treatment, and the `generate_series`-based approach to expanding each subscription into one row per recognized period is a reasonable implementation pattern.

I do think there's a correctness issue worth resolving before this ships to finance, though, since revenue recognition numbers tend to get scrutinized closely. The proration logic in the CTE `monthly_recognized_amount` divides `total_contract_value` evenly by `contract_length_months`, but I don't see handling for subscriptions that start or end mid-month. A subscription that starts on the 20th of the month is currently getting a full month's worth of revenue recognized for that partial first month, which either overstates revenue in the start month or, depending on how the tail end is handled, could mean the last period is silently dropped or truncated in a way that doesn't sum back to the original contract value. Worth confirming with finance what the expected treatment is (daily proration within partial months is the common approach) and adjusting the CTE accordingly, plus adding a test that asserts `SUM(monthly_recognized_amount)` per subscription equals `total_contract_value` exactly — that invariant should hold for every subscription regardless of start date, and it's a cheap way to catch this class of bug going forward.

Also flagging: mid-term upgrades and downgrades aren't addressed anywhere in this model as far as I can see. If a customer upgrades plans mid-contract, does this model currently treat that as a new subscription record, or does it silently continue recognizing the old contract value? If it's out of scope for this PR that's fine, but it should be called out explicitly as a known gap rather than left implicit, since someone reconciling against Stripe or the billing system later will otherwise assume it's handled.

Requesting changes on the partial-period proration specifically — that's the piece I'd consider a correctness bug rather than a nice-to-have. Happy to re-review quickly once that's addressed.
