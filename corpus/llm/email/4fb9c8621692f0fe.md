## Current blocker: carrier API rate limiting

This is the reason for the date change, so I want to explain it properly.

Our outbound shipping integration calls Tessaract Logistics' rating and label API once per parcel. Our contracted tier allows **40 requests per second, burst to 100**. That was sized in the original design against average daily volume, which it comfortably covers.

It does not cover our actual traffic shape. Dunmore and Ashford both release waves — roughly 900 parcels drop into the queue within a 90-second window, four to six times a shift. During those bursts we need something closer to 250 requests per second sustained. In load testing on 3 July we started receiving HTTP 429s eleven seconds into a simulated wave, and the retry logic then made it worse by stacking a backoff storm on top of an already-throttled endpoint.

Three things are happening in parallel:

1. **Commercial.** Ravi has opened a tier upgrade discussion with Tessaract. Their enterprise tier is 500 rps sustained. Indicative uplift is £4,100/month; we're pushing for it to be absorbed into the existing contract renewal in November. Verbal indication is positive, paper is not signed. Expected by 8 August.
2. **Engineering — request shaping.** Regardless of the tier we land on, calling the API once per parcel at wave release is the wrong architecture. We're moving to a queue-and-drain model with a token-bucket limiter, so label generation smooths across the wave rather than spiking at the front of it. Labels are needed at the pack bench, not at wave release, which buys us a genuine 6-8 minute window. This work is in progress and is the critical path item.
3. **Engineering — batch endpoint.** Tessaract has a batch rating endpoint that accepts 50 parcels per call. It is poorly documented and their sandbox returns a different response envelope than production, which cost us four days. We now have it working against production for rating; label generation remains single-parcel on their side.

I want to be clear that this is a design miss on our part, not vendor bad faith. We sized against averages when we should have sized against peak burst. I've added a "traffic shape, not traffic volume" check to the integration design review template.

## The revised date, and why

The original 15 September date assumed shipping integration would be closed out in June. Instead we spent July diagnosing and re-architecting it, and the fix isn't load-tested yet.

I considered holding 15 September and compressing the parallel-run window from two weeks to one. I'm recommending against it. The parallel run is where we catch financial variance between the two systems, and a one-week window over a single month-end gives us one data point on inventory valuation. Two weeks gives us a month-end plus the following week's activity. Getting inventory valuation wrong at go-live is the failure mode that costs us an audit finding, and it is not worth four weeks of schedule.

The new date also moves cutover away from the pre-peak freeze that Distribution asked us to respect, and lands go-live with three clear weeks before volumes start climbing in late October. Bram, if that window looks wrong from your side, tell me this week rather than in September.

Three weeks of the four-week slip is genuine work. One week is schedule buffer I have deliberately added and will report against honestly rather than quietly consuming.
