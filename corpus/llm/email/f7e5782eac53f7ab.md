## IPA tasting recap

We had fourteen entries, which was more than the scoring sheets accounted for, so apologies for the improvised photocopying. Results by popular vote:

1. **Marcus Idowu — "Second Shift"**, a West Coast IPA at 6.8%. Simcoe and Centennial, single hop stand at 170°F, no whirlpool addition. Bitterness that finished clean instead of lingering, which several people commented on. Marcus credits a water adjustment he's been experimenting with — more on that below.
2. **Priya Raman — hazy, 6.2%.** Citra and Nelson Sauvin. Held its clarity profile better than most hazies do after three weeks in the keg.
3. **Tom Beckett — an English-style IPA at 5.4%**, which was the only entry in that direction and got a lot of "oh, right, this is also IPA" reactions. East Kent Goldings, a proper malt backbone, served slightly warm on purpose.

Marcus has agreed to write up his water chemistry for next month's newsletter. The short version is that he's been targeting a sulfate-to-chloride ratio around 2.5:1 for the West Coast styles and has stopped using the mineral additions that came with his kit. Several people asked him about this at the tasting and he was getting tired of repeating it, hence the writeup.

## Member writeup: rescuing a stuck fermentation

Dana Whitfield sent this in and it's worth reading in full — I'm summarizing here and the complete version is on the club site.

Dana pitched a 1.084 imperial stout in early September using a single vial of liquid yeast without a starter. Fermentation took off vigorously for about three days, then stopped dead at 1.038 with roughly half the sugar unconsumed. Airlock silent, no krausen, gravity readings identical over four days.

Her first instinct was to blame the yeast, but before pitching more she checked her temperature log and found the fermentation chamber had dropped to 61°F overnight when the ambient temperature fell — the heater in her setup was sized for a much milder swing than we got in that cold snap. The yeast had gone dormant and flocculated out rather than dying.

What worked: she moved the fermenter to a warmer room over about six hours to avoid shocking it further, brought it up to 68°F, and gently roused the yeast back into suspension by swirling the carboy. Fermentation restarted within a day and finished at 1.022 over the following week.

Her takeaways, which I'll repeat because they're the useful part:

- Underpitching a high-gravity wort leaves you no margin. A 1.084 beer wants roughly twice the cell count of a standard-gravity batch. Make a starter.
- Check temperature before you check anything else. A stuck ferment is a cold ferment far more often than it's a dead yeast cake.
- Rousing is free and non-destructive. Try it before you pitch expensive replacement yeast.
