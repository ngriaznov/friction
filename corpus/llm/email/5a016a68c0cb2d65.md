**Saturday's long run**

We ran the 16-mile loop out to the reservoir and back. Conditions were about as rough as we've had: 74°F at the 6:30 start, 81°F and humid by the time the last group finished a little after 9:15.

The pace groups mostly held together, with one exception — the 9:00 group split around mile 10 and the back half came in closer to 9:40s. That's fine. That's the correct response to heat, and I'd rather see it than see people grinding out goal pace in conditions that don't warrant it. A useful rule for the rest of the summer: on days above 70°F with high humidity, add 20 to 45 seconds per mile to your long-run target and don't think twice about it. You get the same aerobic benefit at the higher heart rate. Forcing the pace just means you finish the run more wrecked than the training stimulus was worth.

The water stop at the reservoir turnaround worked well. Thanks to Priya and Marcus for hauling the coolers out at 5:45 in the morning so it was there when we arrived.

**Hydration in hot weather**

We spent about twenty minutes on this afterward and it's worth putting in writing, because a few people are running into real trouble on the long runs.

Two things you're losing when you sweat: water and sodium. Most people are pretty good about the water and pretty careless about the sodium, and the symptoms of getting the balance wrong — headache, sloshy stomach, fingers swelling, feeling foggy late in a run — look a lot like dehydration, which leads people to drink *more* plain water and make it worse.

Practical version:

- **Start hydrated.** Sixteen to twenty ounces in the hour or two before you head out, not chugged at the trailhead.
- **On the run, aim for roughly 16 to 24 oz per hour** in this weather, sipped regularly rather than gulped at stops. Your number will vary; see the sweat-rate test below.
- **Get sodium in with it.** Roughly 300–600 mg per hour for most people, more if you're a heavy or salty sweater — if you finish a run with white crust on your face or your hat, that's you. Electrolyte tabs, a sports drink, salt capsules, whatever you'll actually take. Plain water alone for three hours is how you end up in trouble.
- **Practice with what you'll use on race day.** Do not discover on race morning that the on-course drink upsets your stomach.

If you want a real number instead of a guess, do a sweat-rate test: weigh yourself naked before an hour-long run, weigh yourself naked after (towel off, note how much you drank during). Each pound lost is about 16 oz of fluid. Add back what you drank, and you have your hourly sweat rate. It's worth doing once. It changes with the weather, so a spring number won't tell you much in August.

A caution I want to state plainly: if anyone ever feels confused, stops sweating, gets chills in the heat, or seems disoriented on a run, that's not a "push through it" situation. Stop, get them in shade, get help. We had a near-miss two summers ago and I don't want a repeat.
