Subject: Updated Parental Leave Policy — Effective Next Quarter

Dear all,

I'm writing to share an update to our parental leave policy, effective for all qualifying leaves beginning next quarter. This change reflects feedback we've heard directly from employees over the past year, and I want to make sure everyone understands both what's changing and how it affects you.

**What's changing**

We are increasing paid parental leave from 12 weeks to 16 weeks for all eligible employees, regardless of gender or which parent is the primary caregiver. This applies to birth, adoption, and foster placement. The additional four weeks can be taken continuously immediately following the original 12-week period, or split and used within the first 12 months after the qualifying event — for example, some employees may want to use part of the extra time later in the year rather than all at once, and that flexibility is now built into the policy.

**Eligibility**

The eligibility rules themselves are not changing: any employee who has completed 90 days of continuous employment at the time of the qualifying event is eligible for the full 16 weeks. Employees who have not yet reached the 90-day mark remain eligible for our existing baseline of 6 weeks, consistent with the prior policy.

**How this interacts with short-term disability**

For birth parents, this policy continues to run concurrently with short-term disability (STD) benefits rather than stacking on top of them. In practice, this means: your STD benefit (typically covering 6–8 weeks depending on your specific circumstances and physician documentation) will cover the initial portion of your leave, and the company will supplement your pay during that period so that your total compensation matches 100% of your normal salary, not just the STD-covered percentage. Once your STD period ends, the remainder of your 16 weeks continues as company-paid parental leave at full salary. Non-birth parents, who are not eligible for STD in connection with the birth itself, receive the full 16 weeks as company-paid leave from day one.

**A few other notes**

- This policy applies retroactively to any employee currently on leave who has not yet exhausted their original 12 weeks — you'll automatically receive the additional 4 weeks; no action needed on your part.
- Leave can still be taken intermittently with manager approval, for example returning part-time before resuming full-time work.
- Job protection and benefits continuation terms are unchanged from the existing policy.

We're glad to be able to make this change, and we hope it makes a real difference for growing families on our team.
