Subject: The walnut table is finally done

Hey all,

The walnut dining table is finished, and I wanted to write up the build while it's still fresh, mistakes and all.

I started shopping for wood back in the spring, and finding the walnut took longer than I expected. I went to three different hardwood dealers before landing on a supplier about ninety minutes north who had a stack of 8/4 black walnut with genuinely good color consistency — a lot of what I'd seen elsewhere had that washed-out sapwood edge running through half the board, which I didn't want fighting the grain on a piece this size. I ended up buying more than I needed, about 60 board feet for what became roughly 45 board feet of finished table, because I wanted enough to bookmatch the top and still have offcuts for the apron and legs from the same tree if possible. Worth the extra cost, in hindsight — the color match across the top is close to seamless.

The glue-up is where things went sideways. I was joining the top in three wide slabs, biscuit-jointed and clamped with six pipe clamps alternating top and bottom to fight bowing. I got impatient on the second slab and didn't check my clamp pressure evenly before it started to set — by the time I noticed, one seam had crept slightly out of flush, maybe a sixteenth of an inch, not enough to see standing up but very much enough to feel running a hand across it. That wouldn't have been the end of the world on its own, but when I went to true up the legs afterward, I discovered I'd cut one leg from a section of walnut with a bit more internal tension than the rest, and it had bowed just enough during the wait that it no longer sat flush against the apron. I ended up having to take that leg back to the jointer and re-plane it down almost an eighth of an inch on one face to get everything sitting square again, which meant re-fitting the mortise and tenon joint on that corner from scratch. Lesson relearned: let glue-ups cure fully before moving on to joinery, and don't trust a board just because the rest of the batch was straight.

For finish, I went with three coats of hardwax oil rather than a film finish. I wanted something that would show the walnut's natural depth rather than sitting on top of it with a plastic-y sheen, and I like that hardwax oil is easy to spot-repair down the road since this table is absolutely going to take some abuse from my kids. Each coat got a light hand-buff with 320 grit between applications, and the final result has a low, warm sheen that photographs a lot better than it sounds on paper.

All told, from rough lumber to the last coat curing, this was about 65 hours of shop time spread over five weekends, plus another week of cure time before it could go under real use. Longer than I budgeted, mostly thanks to the leg re-plane, but I'm genuinely happy with how it came out. Happy to answer questions about the joinery or finish if anyone's planning something similar — and if anyone wants to see it in person, you all know where to find me.

Talk soon,
Ray
