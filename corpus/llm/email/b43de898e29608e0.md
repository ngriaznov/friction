Subject: We're moving the wiki off Confluence — here's the plan

Hi everyone,

Starting next week, we're beginning a migration of our internal wiki from Confluence to a self-hosted MkDocs setup. I want to walk through why we're doing this, what the timeline looks like, and what you'll need to do with your existing pages.

**Why we're making this change**

Three reasons drove this decision. First, cost: our Confluence licensing bill has grown roughly 40% over the past two years as the team has scaled, and that trend isn't slowing down. Second, search quality: I know I'm not the only one who's given up on Confluence search and just asked in Slack instead. Docs-as-code tools with proper static site indexing consistently perform better here. Third, and honestly the biggest driver for me, is that MkDocs content lives in git. That means changes go through pull requests, get reviewed like code, and we get a real history of who changed what and why — instead of a wiki page silently drifting out of date with no accountability.

**Timeline**

We're giving ourselves six weeks:

- Week 1 (starting Monday): Platform team stands up the MkDocs instance and CI pipeline. Read-only mirror of Confluence stays live throughout.
- Weeks 2–4: Team-by-team migration window. Each team lead will get a checklist and a suggested folder structure. This is the bulk of the work — please don't wait until week 4 to start.
- Week 5: Cross-linking cleanup, redirect setup, and a full-team review pass to catch broken links or missing pages.
- Week 6: Confluence goes read-only, then is archived. MkDocs becomes the source of truth.

**What you need to do**

Every team is responsible for migrating its own pages. Practically, that means:

1. Audit your team's Confluence space and flag anything stale for deletion rather than migration — this is a good excuse to clean house.
2. Convert pages you're keeping to Markdown (there's a conversion script in the #wiki-migration channel pinned post that handles most formatting automatically).
3. Open a PR against the new docs repo, get it reviewed by a teammate, and merge.
4. Update any bookmarks or links in your own docs, tickets, or onboarding materials.

Team leads will hear from me individually this week with your migration slots so we don't have everyone hitting the new repo at once.

**Questions**

For anything about the migration process or timeline, reach out to me directly. For technical issues with the MkDocs setup itself — build failures, theme problems, plugin requests — ping the platform team in #wiki-migration.

I know any tool change is disruptive, but I think this one pays off quickly. Thanks in advance for your patience over the next six weeks.

Best,
Sam
