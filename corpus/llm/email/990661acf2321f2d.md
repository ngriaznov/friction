## The dash

Since roughly February I've had a note in my task list that just said "movement feels flat." I knew it was true and I had no idea what to do about it. Hollowreach's whole pitch is that you're a small, fast thing in enormous rooms, and the small fast thing was moving like a shopping cart.

So this month I built a dash. Not a novel idea — you've played fifty games with a dash — but the specifics took three weeks to get right, and the specifics are the whole thing.

Version one was what you'd expect: press the button, get a burst of horizontal velocity, cooldown of half a second. It was fine. Fine is the worst possible result, because there's nothing obviously wrong to fix. I sat with it for a week feeling annoyed.

What finally worked was making the dash *stop* properly. The original version blended back into normal movement, so you'd dash and then sort of drift out of it. Now the dash ends hard — velocity snaps to zero for exactly four frames before your normal input takes over again. Four frames is about 66 milliseconds. You do not consciously perceive it. But it turns the dash into a punctuation mark instead of a smear, and suddenly the whole game feels like it has a rhythm.

There's also a small thing I stole shamelessly from every good platformer: if you dash into the ground at an angle, you keep your horizontal speed on landing instead of losing it to friction. That one line of code is responsible for about half the joy in the current build. Players who find it start chaining dashes across whole rooms without ever really deciding to.

Two related changes came out of this:

- **Dash cancels your air-jump refresh.** Without this you could dash-jump-dash-jump upward forever, which I discovered when a playtester climbed out of the third area entirely and into a room I hadn't built yet.
- **The cooldown is now measured from the *end* of the dash**, not the start. Same number, feels twice as responsive. I've stopped being surprised by this kind of thing.

Levels one through three have been retuned around the new movement. Everything past that is still built for the old speed and currently plays like wading, so if you're wondering why the demo stops where it stops — that's why.

## 5,000 wishlists

We crossed 5,000 Steam wishlists on the 14th. When I put the page up last August I told myself that 1,000 by the end of the year would mean the game had a pulse. So: thank you, genuinely. A large chunk of that came from the trailer that went around in early March, and a surprising amount came from a single forum post someone made that I didn't know about until a week later.

Some honesty about the number, because I think this newsletter is more useful if I don't just report good things: wishlists are a soft signal. The conversion rate from wishlist to purchase at launch is usually somewhere between two and twenty percent depending on who you ask, which is a range wide enough to be almost useless for planning. What 5,000 does tell me is that the game is legible to people from a trailer — that they can look at thirty seconds of it and understand what it is. That's the part I was actually unsure about.

The next real milestone is 10,000, which is roughly where Steam's visibility features start doing meaningful work on your behalf. If you've wishlisted and enjoyed the demo, telling one person about it does more than anything I can do with a marketing budget of zero.

## What's next, and when

Here's the rough shape of the next few months. Dates are intentions, not promises.

**April–May:** Rebuild areas four and five around the new movement. This is mostly level design, not code, and level design is the part I'm slowest at. Expect me to be quiet.

**Early June:** New demo build. This is the one I actually care about — it'll include the dash, the retuned early areas, at least one of the rebuilt later areas, and the new save system so you can stop and come back. The current demo's lack of saving is the single most common complaint and it has been fixed for a while; it just isn't in a public build yet.

**Late June:** A short window where I'll be looking for maybe twenty people to play the June build and talk to me about it properly — a call, not a survey. If that sounds interesting, just reply to this email and I'll keep a list.

**After that:** I'll say something about launch timing when I have something real to say. Right now anything I told you would be a guess dressed up as a plan, and I'd rather not.
