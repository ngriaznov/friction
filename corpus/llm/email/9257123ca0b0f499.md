**Subject:** New department cluster (Thornwood) arriving over winter break — accounts, downtime, and the Halstead migration deadline

Dear colleagues and graduate students,

I'm glad to announce that the College has approved funding for a replacement compute cluster, which will be installed in the Marchetti Hall machine room over winter break. The new system is named **Thornwood**. It replaces Halstead, which has been in service since 2018 and is now well past the point where we can get replacement parts without going to the secondary market.

**What's in it**

Thornwood has 44 compute nodes. Forty are standard CPU nodes with dual 64-core AMD EPYC processors and 512 GB of memory each, and four are GPU nodes carrying four NVIDIA H200 cards apiece with 1 TB of host memory. Everything is connected with 200 Gb InfiniBand, which is a substantial step up from Halstead's 40 Gb fabric and should matter a great deal to those of you running tightly-coupled MPI jobs.

Storage is 1.2 PB of usable parallel filesystem, plus 40 TB of NVMe scratch. Home directories will be quota'd at 200 GB per user, up from 50 GB on Halstead. Project space beyond that is available on request through your faculty advisor — we've deliberately kept the request process light, but we do need to know what's growing and why.

The scheduler is Slurm, same as Halstead, so most of your batch scripts will carry over with minor changes to partition names and module loads.

**Requesting an account**

Accounts are not migrated automatically. Everyone needs to request one, including current Halstead users.

Fill out the form at **research-computing.our.edu/thornwood-account**. Faculty and postdocs are approved directly; graduate students need their advisor listed on the form, and we'll email the advisor for a one-click confirmation. Please don't ask your advisor to fill the form out on your behalf — the account belongs to you, and the SSH key you upload should be yours.

Requests submitted before **December 12** will be provisioned in the first batch and ready when the system opens. After that we'll process weekly.

**Schedule and maintenance windows**

- **December 15 – January 6** — installation and burn-in. Halstead remains available throughout, though I'd expect some brief network interruptions in Marchetti as the fabric is cut over. We'll announce those on the rc-announce list with at least 24 hours' notice.
- **January 7 – February 14** — Thornwood open in friendly-user mode. No allocation limits, no guarantees. Expect reboots, and expect some of them to be unannounced. This is the period to port your workflows and tell us what's broken.
- **February 17** — full production. Standard allocation policy applies from this date.
- **Recurring maintenance** — the second Tuesday of each month, 07:00–13:00. Jobs that would run past the window start will be held by the scheduler rather than killed, so submit with accurate walltimes and you won't lose work.

**Halstead retirement — please read this part**

**Halstead goes read-only on March 6 and is powered off on March 27.**

Between those dates you can copy data off, but you cannot run jobs or write. After March 27 the disks are wiped and returned to the vendor as part of the trade-in credit that helped pay for Thornwood, and nothing on them is recoverable. There is no archival copy. I want to be blunt about this because the last time we retired a system, two groups discovered in the final week that they had years of raw data on scratch and nowhere else, and the resulting scramble was not pleasant for anyone.

Research Computing will run four migration clinics in February — dates to follow — where you can bring a laptop and a list of directories and get help moving things. If you have more than about 5 TB to move, please contact them early rather than starting a transfer in the last week; the copy itself takes real time.

**Questions**

Rachel Okonkwo (rc-help@our.edu) is the technical contact and will answer anything about accounts, software modules, or transfers far better than I can. Questions about allocation policy or whether your group's needs are being met should come to me directly.
