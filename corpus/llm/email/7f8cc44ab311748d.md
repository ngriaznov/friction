I'd like to request a new read replica for the reporting workload on the orders database. Wanted to give you the full context before you scope the work.

**Why we need this**

Over the past month the primary has been showing sustained CPU load in the 70–80% range during business hours, up from a baseline closer to 40% a quarter ago. When I profiled it last week, a good chunk of that is coming from the reporting queries the finance and ops teams run against `orders` and `order_line_items` — several of these are large aggregations that scan wide date ranges and just aren't friendly to run against the same instance handling live transaction traffic. We've had two incidents in the last three weeks where a reporting query correlated with elevated write latency on checkout, which is the part that worries me most.

**What we're asking for**

A dedicated read replica that the reporting service can point at exclusively, separate from any existing replicas used for failover.

- **Query patterns**: Mostly large aggregate queries (SUM, COUNT, GROUP BY) over `orders`, `order_line_items`, and `payments`, typically scoped to date ranges from a few days up to 18 months for quarterly reports. Query volume is bursty — heaviest Monday mornings and the first three business days of each month — rather than steady throughout the day.
- **Data volume**: Current combined size of the tables in scope is approximately 800GB, growing at roughly 15GB/month.
- **Region**: Same region as the primary (us-east-1) to keep replication lag minimal — the reporting team needs same-day data, not eventually-consistent-over-hours data.
- **Instance sizing**: I'd defer to your judgment on instance class, but given the aggregation-heavy workload I'd guess something with more memory relative to the primary would help, since a lot of these queries are memory-bound during sort/aggregate steps.

**Proposed cutover**

I'd like to target two weeks from now for cutover, which gives us a week for provisioning and replication catch-up, plus a few days to redirect the reporting service's connection string and validate query performance before we call it done. I'm happy to be flexible if that timeline doesn't work on your end — just let me know what's realistic.
