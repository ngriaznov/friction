## Project sharing

**Marguerite's cabled pullover.** This was, by unanimous and unprompted agreement, the thing everybody came away talking about. Fourteen months on the needles, an aran-weight wool in a deep oatmeal, with a central panel of interlocking cables flanked by moss stitch and a saddle shoulder. She passed it around and it went all the way down one side of the table and back before anyone would let go of it.

A few details worth writing down, because several people asked and I know I'd forget them by January:

- She swatched the cable panel three times before starting. Her first swatch was on the needle size the pattern called for, and the cables looked slack. Going down a full millimetre gave her the definition she wanted, and she recalculated the body stitch count from the new gauge rather than following the printed numbers.
- The saddle shoulder was worked flat and grafted, not picked up. She said the grafting was the only part she'd do differently — not because it went wrong, but because she did it at eleven at night and had to redo half of it in the morning.
- She blocked it hard on foam mats, pinned to measurement, and let it dry for two full days. Someone asked whether blocking flattened the cables. It doesn't, if you don't stretch across the cable panel — pin the edges and let the middle sit.

Marguerite has offered to bring the swatches to the next meeting so people can see the difference the needle change made. If you're on the fence about swatching, come look at them.

**Other things on the table:** Ivan's first pair of colourwork mittens, worked in the round with a steek he was visibly nervous about and which held perfectly. Priya's baby blanket in a garter-stitch chevron, finished a comfortable three weeks before the baby is due, which is a record for this group. Nell brought a half-finished vest specifically to ask about armhole shaping, and got four different opinions, which is exactly what she was after.

## Yarn substitution for the Hollow Ash shawl

A good chunk of the evening went to this, since at least six people are either knitting the Hollow Ash or planning to, and the yarn the pattern specifies is both expensive and hard to get here.

The pattern calls for a fingering-weight single-ply in a wool-silk blend, 400 metres to 100 grams, and the design leans heavily on drape — long garter sections and a wide lace edge that's meant to hang rather than stand out from the shoulders.

The consensus, roughly:

**Match the metres per gram first, and the fibre second.** A 400 m/100 g substitute will get you the right fabric weight even if the fibre differs. Getting the yardage wrong is much harder to recover from than getting the fibre wrong.

**Singles versus plied matters here more than usual.** A round three-ply at the same weight will give you crisper stitch definition but noticeably less drape, and this shawl wants drape. If you only have plied yarn available, several people suggested going up a needle size and accepting a slightly more open fabric.

**Superwash behaves differently after blocking.** Dorothy's warning, from experience: superwash wool grows when blocked and keeps growing under its own weight. If you substitute a superwash, block your swatch, hang it overnight, and measure again before you trust the gauge.

**Watch the yardage total.** The pattern gives a total in metres, not skeins, and a few of the suggested substitutes come in 50 g skeins rather than 100 g. Two people have already had to order an extra skein mid-project at a different dye lot. Buy the extra now.

Ivan is putting together a short list of specific substitutes people in the group have actually used, with photos of the finished fabric, and I'll circulate it once he's done. If you've knit the Hollow Ash or anything similar in a substitute yarn, send him a note.

## Beginner's sock workshop — Saturday 6 December

We're running a beginner's sock-knitting workshop next month, and this one is genuinely aimed at people who have never turned a heel. If you have knit socks before, you're welcome to come and help rather than attend.

**When:** Saturday 6 December, 10:00 to 15:00, with a break for lunch around 12:30.
**Where:** Grange Hall, back room as usual.
**Cost:** £8 to cover the room and materials.
**Led by:** Dorothy, with Priya and me helping on the floor.

We'll work a cuff-down sock on double-pointed needles, top to toe, on a small child's size so it's finishable in a day. The heel turn gets a full hour of its own, worked slowly and out loud, because that's the part that makes people put socks down and never pick them up again.

**Bring:** a set of five 2.5 mm double-pointed needles and 50 g of sock-weight yarn in a light, solid colour. Please, not a dark yarn and not a variegated one — you need to be able to see your stitches while you're learning what they're doing. A stitch marker or two and a darning needle if you have them. If you don't own DPNs, tell me by the 29th and we'll lend you a set.

**Lunch:** bring your own. There's a kettle.

Places are limited to twelve. Reply to this email to claim one — first come, first served, and I'll confirm each one individually so you're not left guessing.
