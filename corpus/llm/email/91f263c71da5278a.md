Subject: Riverside Community Food Bank — Quarterly Update for Our Volunteers

Dear Riverside Community Food Bank volunteers,

Thank you for another quarter of showing up — early mornings sorting produce, long afternoons at distribution sites, and everything in between. We wanted to take a moment to share what your time made possible, tell you about a new route that's already changing how we reach people, and invite you to an event where we can say thank you in person.

## The Numbers Behind the Work

This past quarter, Riverside Community Food Bank distributed 214,000 meals across our service area, up from 198,000 the quarter before. That increase reflects both rising need in our community and the growing capacity you've helped us build. We served just over 3,100 individual households, including roughly 1,200 children and 480 seniors. None of that happens without the roughly 6,800 volunteer hours logged this quarter alone — sorting donations, packing boxes, driving routes, and staffing distribution tables in weather that was not always cooperative.

We also want to highlight a quieter number that matters just as much: our food waste diversion. Through partnerships with three local grocers and a regional produce distributor, we recovered and redistributed over 38,000 pounds of food that would otherwise have gone to landfill this quarter. That's food that fed families instead of sitting in a dumpster, and it's a direct result of the relationships our volunteer coordinators have built with those partners over the past year.

## Spotlight: The New Eastside Mobile Route

For years, residents in the Eastside neighborhoods — particularly around the Milldale and Corbin Heights areas — have had to travel 25 minutes or more to reach our nearest fixed distribution site. Many don't have reliable transportation, and for seniors especially, that distance has meant going without.

This quarter, we launched a new mobile distribution route that brings a fully stocked van directly to three stops in those neighborhoods every Tuesday and Friday. In its first six weeks, the Eastside route has already served 340 households, more than half of whom told our intake volunteers they hadn't been able to access food bank services before. Longtime volunteer Marcus Yee, who has driven the route since it started, put it simply: "You see people who've been going without, and now they don't have to wait for a ride that might not come." We're incredibly proud of this expansion, and it exists because enough of you were willing to learn a new route, load a van before sunrise, and be patient while we worked out the kinks in the first few weeks.

If you're interested in joining the Eastside route rotation, reply to this email or reach out to our volunteer coordinator, and we'll get you scheduled for an orientation ride-along.

## A Request as the Holidays Approach

As we move into the busiest stretch of our year, we're asking for your help in a different way: a can drive. Shelf-stable protein — canned beans, tuna, chicken, and peanut butter in particular — tends to run low precisely when demand is highest, and we'd rather build up a buffer now than scramble in December. If you're able to bring a few cans to your next shift, or better yet, organize a small collection among coworkers, neighbors, or your kids' school, it makes a real difference. Drop-off bins are at the Delancy Street warehouse and both fixed distribution sites, seven days a week.

If cash donations are easier for you, those go just as far — often further, since we're able to purchase in bulk at prices below retail through our regional food bank network. Either way, know that whatever you're able to give gets turned around and back out the door within days, not months.
