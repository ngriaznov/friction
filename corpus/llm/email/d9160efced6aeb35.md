**Subject:** New children's room, longer weekend hours, and Marisol Cheng visits us in October

Dear friends of the Ashgrove Branch,

I have three pieces of news this month, and for once all three are good. The children's section reopens on Saturday the 20th after eleven weeks of construction, our weekend hours are expanding starting the first of October, and the novelist Marisol Cheng is coming to Ashgrove for an evening event that we have been trying to arrange for the better part of two years.

Details on all three below. If you only read one section, make it the last one — registration for the author event opens Monday and I expect it to fill.

## The children's room reopens Saturday the 20th

Those of you who have been navigating the temporary picture-book shelving in the reference alcove have my sincere thanks for your patience. The alcove was never meant to hold four thousand board books and a story-time rug, and I think everyone involved knew it.

The renovated space is nearly double what it was. We took the old staff workroom behind the east wall — a windowless room that had become, over three decades, mostly a place where broken chairs went to be forgotten — and folded it into the children's area. The wall came down in June. What we gained was not just floor space but the two large north-facing windows that had been sealed behind it since the 1990s.

What's new:

**A dedicated story-time room.** Story time has been held in the open stacks since I started here, which meant that twenty enthusiastic four-year-olds and anyone trying to quietly browse local history were sharing acoustics. The new room has a door. It also has proper sound insulation, tiered soft seating for about thirty-five children plus caregivers, and a projector for the picture-book-on-the-wall sessions that Ms. Adeyemi has been running out of a laptop propped on a milk crate.

**Lower shelving throughout.** The tallest shelf in the picture book area is now 42 inches. Children can reach the books that are meant for them without an adult retrieving things from overhead, which sounds like a small thing and is not.

**A quiet corner for older readers.** Middle-grade and young-adult have been pulled out of the general fiction stacks and given their own alcove with four armchairs and a window seat. Our teen advisory group chose the furniture, and they were considerably more decisive about it than the adults on the planning committee.

**Accessible early-literacy stations.** Four tablet stations at wheelchair-accessible height, loaded with the early-literacy software we've had on the old desktop machines, plus tactile and large-print materials that were previously stored on request only.

**A family restroom with a changing table.** Long overdue. It is directly off the children's area rather than down the main corridor.

The reopening on Saturday the 20th runs from 10:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. There will be a ribbon cutting at 10:15, a double-length story time at 11:00, a craft table, and — because the Friends of the Ashgrove Library insisted, and they paid for the window seat, so they get to insist — cake.

I want to name the Friends group specifically. The renovation was funded by the branch capital allocation, but the furnishings, the tablets, and the story-time projector came from three years of book sales, bake sales, and the annual quiz night. That is roughly £31,000 raised in £2 and £5 increments by volunteers. If you have ever bought a paperback from the trolley by the entrance, some part of that room is yours.

## Extended weekend hours from October 1st

Beginning Saturday, October 1st, the branch will be open:

| | Current | From October 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Saturday | 10:00 – 4:00 | 9:00 – 6:00 |
| Sunday | Closed | 11:00 – 4:00 |

Weekday hours are unchanged: 9:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. Monday through Thursday, 9:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. Friday.

Sunday opening is the change I have wanted most since taking this post. Roughly a third of the survey responses we collected in the spring mentioned weekend access as the single biggest barrier to using the branch, and a great many of those came from people working weekday shifts who simply could not get here.

Full services will be available during all extended hours: circulation, holds pickup, reference help, the public computers, and the study rooms. The only exception is that the local history room will remain by appointment on Sundays, as it requires a specialist member of staff.

Two people made this possible: Daniel Okonjo and Priya Raghunathan, who join us as part-time library assistants in September. Please say hello when you see them.

## An evening with Marisol Cheng — Thursday, October 13th

Marisol Cheng will be at the Ashgrove Branch on **Thursday, October 13th at 7:00 p.m.** to discuss her new novel, *The Salt Ledger*, in conversation with our own Ms. Adeyemi, followed by audience questions and a signing.

For anyone who hasn't encountered her work: Cheng writes quiet, sharply observed fiction about migration and inheritance. Her second novel, *Winterfold*, spent much of last year on our holds list with a queue of forty-plus, which is the closest thing a library has to a bestseller list.

**Registration details:**

- Free, but registration is required. The event room holds 120.
- Registration opens **Monday, September 12th at 9:00 a.m.** at the circulation desk, by phone on 0117 496 2280, or through the events page on the branch website.
- Two tickets maximum per library card.
- A waiting list will open once we reach capacity, and we do release no-show seats at 6:55 p.m. on the night.
- Copies of *The Salt Ledger* will be available for purchase from Harrow & Quill, our local independent bookshop, who are handling sales for the evening. You are welcome to bring your own copy, including a library copy, for signing.
- The event will be BSL interpreted. If you need any other accommodation, please tell us at registration and we will arrange it.

Doors open at 6:30. There will be tea.
