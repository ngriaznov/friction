**Subject:** Incident summary — credential-stuffing attack on customer login (INC-2291, resolved)

Team,

Writing up Tuesday's login incident now that traffic has returned to baseline and the temporary mitigations have been in place for 48 hours without issue.

**What happened**

Starting around 02:10 UTC on Tuesday, failed logins on the customer sign-in page climbed from a normal ~180/hour to just over 40,000/hour and stayed there for roughly five hours. This was not an outage — the login service handled the load — but the pattern was clearly automated.

**How we found it**

The rate-limiter's per-endpoint alert fired at 02:23, thirteen minutes in, when the failure ratio on `POST /session` crossed 85%. On-call picked it up immediately. The tell that this was credential stuffing rather than a broken client integration: each source IP tried a small number of distinct email addresses, most of the attempted addresses had never existed in our system, and the user-agent string was identical across tens of thousands of requests. Traffic came from roughly 3,400 residential IPs across a wide geographic spread, consistent with a rented proxy pool.

**What we did**

At 02:50 we enabled the CAPTCHA challenge on the login form for any session exceeding three failures, and tightened per-IP throttling from 60 requests/minute to 10. Attack volume dropped by about 95% within twenty minutes and tapered off entirely by 07:30. Both mitigations are still on; we plan to keep the CAPTCHA threshold in place permanently and relax the IP throttle back to 30/minute next week.

**Scope of impact**

Of approximately 190,000 login attempts, 214 succeeded against real accounts. All 214 have had sessions revoked and passwords reset, and the affected customers were notified Wednesday morning. We found no evidence of data export or settings changes on any of those accounts. No credentials were leaked from our side — the address/password pairs were almost certainly sourced from a third-party breach and replayed against us.

**What's next**

Three things are being scoped for this quarter: checking new and changed passwords against a breached-credential corpus, offering (and eventually defaulting on) TOTP for customer accounts, and adding a device-recognition signal so logins from unfamiliar devices get an extra check. I'll bring proposals to the security review on the 14th.
