**Subject:** 8-inch f/6 progress, one ugly scratch, and a testing question

Evening all,

Long-overdue update on the 8-inch f/6 I started back in the autumn, plus a problem I could use opinions on.

**Where things stand**

Grinding went fine and I won't bore anyone with it. Fine grinding through 25 micron took three sessions and the sphere came out clean. Polishing on pitch took considerably longer than the books suggested — about eleven hours across two weeks with a full-size lap, W-strokes, working in a cool garage where the pitch behaved differently morning to evening. I ended up pressing the lap for twenty minutes before every session rather than the five I'd been getting away with.

Figuring has been slower and more interesting. Starting from the polished sphere I've been working with a sub-diameter lap, roughly 60% diameter, using chordal strokes to deepen the centre. Foucault readings after each session, five zones, three sets of readings averaged. Current state is a paraboloid that's about 1/6 wave at the edge with a slight turned-down zone in the outer 10mm and a shallow ring depression around the 70% zone. Neither is dreadful. The TDE is the one bothering me — I've been feathering the strokes to keep the lap from overhanging, but it creeps back.

**The setback**

Two sessions ago I found a scratch. About 18mm long, running roughly radially, starting maybe 25mm in from the edge. Fine but definitely visible under a bright light at an angle, and I can just barely feel it with a fingernail — which is the part I don't like.

I think I know what happened. I'd been keeping the polishing bucket next to the workbench, and there's a shelf above the bench holding tins of the old carborundum grits. I'd moved a tin the day before. It's entirely possible a few grains found their way onto the lap or the mirror face. Entirely my own fault for a workshop layout I'd been meaning to fix for a year.

The question is what to do about it. My instinct was to go back to polishing and try to work it out, but a scratch that catches a fingernail is a fair way down and polishing it out means removing a lot of glass across the whole surface to keep the figure — which more or less means starting the figure over. I've done about six hours of figuring work I'd be throwing away.

The alternative is to accept it. A single 18mm scratch at that radius is a vanishingly small fraction of the aperture. Scattered light, sure, but I suspect it's below what I'd notice against ordinary sky glow in my location. I've read plenty of people saying exactly this, but I've also never had to actually decide.

Anyone here made this call themselves? Did you regret it either way?

**The testing question**

Assuming I press on and finish the figure, I'd like a second opinion on the surface before I send it off for coating, since coating is the point of no return for cheap fixes.

Foucault is what I've got and what I trust, but I know its limitations at the edge, and the edge is exactly where I'm least confident. I've read about the Ronchi test as a quick sanity check and about the Bath interferometer as the serious option. The Bath looks buildable — I've seen a few designs using a cube beamsplitter and a diode laser — but I don't want to sink three weekends into building a tester if a careful Ronchi grating and a star test would tell me the same thing for a mirror at this focal ratio.

So: for an 8-inch f/6, what would you consider sufficient before coating? Is a star test on a real star enough of a final check, or do people here treat it as confirmation rather than measurement?

Happy to post my zonal readings if anyone wants to look at the numbers.

Clear skies,
Martin
