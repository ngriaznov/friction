**Subject:** We closed the Series B — $42M, and what happens next

Team,

The wire cleared this morning. We've closed a $42 million Series B led by Northaven Partners, with participation from Ridgeline Capital and both of our seed investors coming back in. The round values us at $310 million post-money. Papers were signed Tuesday; I wanted to wait until the money was actually in the account before saying anything, because I've been on the other side of an announcement that had to be walked back and it's not an experience I'd repeat.

I'll be direct about what this means and what it doesn't.

**What we raised on**

Northaven did not invest because of a deck. They invested because they talked to eleven of our customers and nine of them independently used the phrase "we'd have to rebuild this ourselves if you disappeared." Kavita on the partner side told me that call sequence was the shortest diligence process she'd run in three years. That reputation is not something the fundraising team manufactured over six weeks — it's the accumulated result of every support ticket that got answered properly, every migration that didn't lose data, every time somebody stayed late to make a customer's edge case work.

Net revenue retention landed at 141% for the trailing twelve months. That number is the whole argument. It says customers who join us buy more from us over time, and there is no way to fake it.

**How we'll spend it over the next 18 months**

The plan we committed to the board breaks down roughly as follows.

About half goes to engineering and product. The single biggest item is the multi-region work — we've been losing deals in Europe on data residency, and we've turned down two eight-figure opportunities in the last year for that reason alone. That project is now funded end to end rather than squeezed in around everything else.

Roughly a third goes to go-to-market. We've been running a sales team of six against a market that our own inbound volume says can support three times that. We're also building out a real solutions engineering function, because right now our AEs are pulling backend engineers into calls, which is bad for the AEs and worse for the engineers.

The remainder covers infrastructure, security certifications (SOC 2 Type II is done, ISO 27001 starts in Q1), and a genuine operating buffer. We are planning to reach default-alive on this round. That is the actual goal — not a Series C, not a bigger round, but a business that doesn't need one.

**Hiring**

We're going from 58 people to approximately 105 over the next four quarters. Three teams grow meaningfully:

- **Platform engineering** — 12 roles, weighted toward distributed systems and data infrastructure. This is the multi-region team plus the reliability investment we've been deferring.
- **Sales and solutions engineering** — 16 roles across AE, SE, and two sales managers. First hires are for the EMEA territory, starting with a lead in Q1.
- **Customer success and support** — 9 roles. We currently have one CSM for every 34 accounts. That ratio is not sustainable and it's the thing most likely to break our retention number.

Every open role goes up on the careers page next Monday. Referral bonus is doubling to $6,000 for the duration of this hiring push, and it pays out at the new hire's 90-day mark rather than at 6 months.

**What isn't changing**

We are not moving to a bigger office. We are not adding management layers between you and the work. We are not going to start optimizing for a growth number that our customers wouldn't recognize as good service. If you see us drifting toward any of those, say so loudly.

Compensation review is unchanged and happens on the normal March cycle. The option pool was refreshed as part of this round, which means new grants and refreshers have room — your manager will have specifics after the March cycle, not before, so please don't chase them for numbers this month.

**Thank you**

I've had a strange few weeks. Fundraising pulls a founder out of the company for a stretch, and I came back to find that essentially everything had continued fine without me — releases shipped, the November incident was handled well and written up honestly, and two of our largest renewals closed early. That is not normal, and I don't take it for granted.

We'll do a proper all-hands Thursday at 10:00 with time for questions, and I'll bring Kavita along for the second half so you can ask her things directly. Bring the hard questions.
