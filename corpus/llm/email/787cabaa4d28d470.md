Subject: Pixelroot Drift — dash mechanic, 5k wishlists, and a demo timeline

Hey everyone,

It's been a busy month on Pixelroot Drift, and I wanted to get a proper update out before things get even busier heading toward the demo build. Thanks for sticking around on this list — every one of you signing up still feels a little unreal to me.

**The dash mechanic is in.** This has been on the design doc since the very first prototype and I kept pushing it back because I didn't trust myself to get the feel right on the first pass. It's a short-range horizontal dash with a brief invincibility window, on a cooldown that's currently tuned at 0.6 seconds — long enough that you can't just spam through hazards, short enough that it feels responsive when you need it. It also cancels the recovery frames on your basic attack, which opened up a couple of combo routes I hadn't planned for and honestly enjoyed discovering more than designing on purpose. There's a rough clip on the devlog if you want to see it in motion; it's still using placeholder dash-trail art, but the timing is close to final.

The trickiest part wasn't the dash itself, it was making sure it interacted sensibly with the existing wall-jump and the spike traps in the mining-tunnel biome. Early on you could dash through a spike and take zero damage regardless of timing, which trivialized about six rooms I'd already built. I ended up narrowing the invincibility window specifically against instant-death hazards while keeping it generous against contact damage from enemies, which took longer to tune than I expected but feels fair now in playtesting.

I also spent a few days on something less visible but arguably more important: input buffering around the dash. Early testers (three friends who tolerate my Discord messages asking them to try builds) kept reporting that the dash "didn't come out" during fast platforming sections, and it took embarrassingly long to realize it wasn't a bug, it was players pressing the dash button one or two frames before the cooldown actually cleared. Adding a six-frame input buffer so an early press queues up and fires the instant it's legal fixed almost all of the complaints, and it's a good reminder that a lot of "this feels unresponsive" feedback is really about input timing forgiveness rather than the mechanic itself.

**Wishlist milestone: we passed 5,000 on Steam this month.** I know that number means different things to different people, but for a solo project with basically no marketing budget, it's the first time the number has felt real rather than aspirational. A decent chunk of the jump came from a gif of the dash mechanic that did better than expected when I posted it, which is a good reminder that showing moving gameplay beats screenshots every time. If you've wishlisted already, thank you — it genuinely helps with Steam's algorithm and with my own motivation on the harder weeks.

For context on where 5,000 sits: it's roughly triple where I was after the first trailer six months ago, and the growth curve has been steadier than spiky, which I take as a decent sign that word of mouth is doing more than any single post. The one exception was the dash gif, which alone accounted for what looked like a few hundred wishlists in about three days — enough that I'm now planning to post short gameplay clips on a more regular cadence instead of saving them up for big announcements.

**Demo timeline.** I'm targeting a public demo build in about ten weeks, covering the first two biomes (the surface ruins and the mining tunnels) and roughly 25 minutes of content if you're exploring for collectibles rather than speedrunning straight through. Before that goes out I still need to: finish enemy variety in the tunnels (currently three enemy types, want at least five), add a proper save/checkpoint system instead of the debug-only save state I've been using myself, and do a full controller-input pass since so far I've only tested on keyboard.

The save system is the piece I'm least looking forward to, honestly. Right now progress only persists via a debug hotkey I use myself, which is fine for me but obviously unshippable — I need real checkpoint triggers placed throughout the levels, a proper file format that won't corrupt if the game crashes mid-write, and some UI around choosing and deleting save slots. None of that is glamorous work and none of it will show up in a devlog gif, but it's the kind of infrastructure that turns a tech demo into something that respects a player's time, so it's getting priority over any more content this month.

If anyone on this list has a controller and is willing to bang on a very rough build in a few weeks, reply and let me know — I'll send a build around to a small group before it goes any wider. I'm especially interested in hearing from anyone with an Xbox controller specifically, since that's the one I don't own and can't test against myself, which makes me nervous about button-prompt mismatches slipping through unnoticed.

Thanks again for reading this far, and for wishlisting, sharing clips, and generally making a solo project feel a lot less solo.

— The Pixelroot Drift dev
