**Subject:** Walnut dining table — finished, finally

Hi all,

The table is done. Six months, which is embarrassing for what is fundamentally four legs and a top, but it's out of the shop and in the dining room and my wife has stopped asking about it, so I'm calling it a win.

**The wood**

I went to Hardwood Row in Bellingham back in February looking for cherry and came home with walnut, which is how these things go. They had a pallet of 8/4 black walnut, kiln dried, from a lot that had been sitting since the previous fall. Most of it was ordinary. But there were four boards near the bottom, all roughly 11 inches wide and 9 feet long, that had come off the same log — consecutive, still stacked in order, with a color run from a chocolate heart out to a pale sapwood edge that lined up board to board.

I bought all four plus a fifth for the legs and aprons. It was more than I'd budgeted by a wide margin and I have not fully accounted for this at home.

Top is those four boards, book-matched at the center seam and then just sequenced outward. Final dimensions 42" x 84". I left the sapwood in on the two outer edges rather than ripping it off. It reads as a deliberate pale border and I'd do it again.

**The mistake**

The glue-up. Of course it was the glue-up.

I did the top in two halves and that went fine. The base is a trestle with through-tenons, and I glued the two leg assemblies on the same afternoon because I was feeling confident. I had one leg clamped, checked it for square, and moved on. When I came back the next morning it had drifted about 3/32" out of square along its length — a clamp had pulled it, and I'd checked the wrong diagonal.

3/32" doesn't sound like anything. On a 29" leg it's enough that the table rocks.

I couldn't take the joint apart; it's Titebond III and it was a day old. So I planed the foot. Took the whole leg assembly to the bench, shimmed it until the top of the leg sat plumb, scribed a line on the foot, and hand-planed down to it. Took maybe forty minutes and a lot of checking. The foot is now very slightly out of parallel with the floor plane in a way nobody will ever see, and the table sits flat.

Lesson, if there is one: check both diagonals, and don't glue two subassemblies in the same session when you're tired.

**Finish**

Osmo Polyx, satin, three coats. Applied thin with a white pad, wiped back hard after ten minutes, 24 hours between coats, light 400-grit scuff before the third. The walnut went from flat brown to something with real depth in the first coat — that moment is the whole reason to work with this wood.

It's a dining table with children involved, so I wanted something repairable rather than a film finish that chips and has to be stripped. Osmo means I can scuff and recoat a damaged area in an afternoon.

**Time**

Roughly 95 hours logged, spread over six months of evenings and about eight Saturdays. Milling was 20 of that. Sanding was more than I want to admit.

Happy to share the trestle drawings if anyone wants them. Photos attached — the last one is the leg I had to fix, in case anyone wants to feel better about their own week.
