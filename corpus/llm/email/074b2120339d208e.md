**Subject:** Mobile redesign — weekly update, week of 14 April

Hi all,

Short version: onboarding shipped to the beta group on Wednesday and the early numbers look good. One item is stuck waiting on legal, and there's a timing risk in May I want on your radar now rather than later.

**Shipped this week**

The new onboarding flow is live for everyone in the TestFlight beta — about 900 people. It replaces the old six-screen setup with three screens, and it no longer asks for a phone number before showing the app.

Early results from the first four days: 71% of new beta users finished setup, against 52% on the current App Store version. That's a small sample over a short window and the beta group skews toward engaged users, so please treat it as encouraging rather than final. We'll have a cleaner read once it reaches general release.

The two screens that tested badly in March (permissions and notification opt-in) were reworked and no longer appear before the user has seen the main screen at all.

**Blocked**

We're waiting on legal sign-off for the third-party analytics SDK we planned to use for funnel measurement. Legal's question is about where the vendor stores data and whether our current privacy notice covers it. Priya sent over the vendor's data processing agreement on 9 April and we're expecting a response early next week.

Practical impact: we can still ship the redesign without it. What we lose is granular drop-off data — we'd know how many people finished onboarding but not exactly which step lost them. If the review runs past 25 April we'll ship with our existing basic event logging and add the SDK in a later release rather than hold the launch.

**Planned for next week**

- Settings and account screens move into design review (Tuesday).
- Accessibility pass on the onboarding flow — contrast, text scaling, VoiceOver labels.
- Second round of usability sessions, six participants, Thursday and Friday.
- Begin assembling the App Store submission materials: screenshots, description copy, and the privacy nutrition label.

**Risk: App Store review timeline**

Our target general-release date is 20 May. App Store review typically takes one to three days, but we've had a submission held for six days before, and rejections for privacy-label mismatches are common enough that I'd rather assume one.

To absorb that, we're planning to submit on 8 May, which gives roughly ten working days of slack. That means the build has to be feature-complete and tested by 6 May. If the analytics SDK is still unresolved at that point, we cut it — I'd rather ship on schedule with less measurement than slip the date.
