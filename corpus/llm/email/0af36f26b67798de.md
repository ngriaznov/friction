**Subject:** Saltmarsh Point recap — a peregrine, 47 species, and next month's trip

Hello all,

Nineteen of us made it out to Saltmarsh Point on Saturday, which is the best turnout we've had for a coastal outing in two years. The weather cooperated more than the forecast suggested — overcast and cold at dawn, clearing by nine, wind under ten knots the whole morning.

We finished with 47 species. Highlights included a good showing of dunlin and black-bellied plover on the outer flats, four northern harriers quartering the marsh, and a single American bittern that Ruth spotted frozen in the reeds near the second boardwalk. Several people got their first-ever look at it, which is exactly the sort of morning we hope for.

The bird of the day, though, was the peregrine falcon that came through around 10:15. It put up the entire shorebird flock from the flats, made two passes, and perched briefly on the channel marker before heading south. Only our fourth club record for the site. Dev got photographs and they're good enough to confirm the identification without argument — full report submitted to the county records committee.

**Telling the raptors apart**

A few people asked, reasonably, how we called it so quickly. A few notes for next time:

*Peregrine vs. merlin* — size is the giveaway when you have something to compare against, but shape is more reliable at distance. Peregrines have broad-based, sharply pointed wings and a heavy chest; merlins look smaller-headed and rangier, with a flatter, more rowing wingbeat. The peregrine's dark malar stripe, that thick "helmet" below the eye, is diagnostic when you can see the face.

*Peregrine vs. northern harrier* — harriers hold their wings in a shallow V and rock side to side as they hunt low over the marsh. They also show an obvious white rump patch. Peregrines fly straight, fast, and level. If it's teetering, it isn't a peregrine.

*Peregrine vs. Cooper's hawk* — Cooper's have short rounded wings and a long banded tail, and they flap-flap-glide. A peregrine's silhouette is all wing.
