Subject: Riverside Food Bank — Quarterly Volunteer Newsletter

Dear volunteers,

Thank you for another quarter of showing up for this community — literally, in many cases, before sunrise on distribution days. Here's a look back at what your time made possible, plus what's coming up.

**Meals Distributed**

This past quarter, we distributed 214,600 meals across our network of pantries and mobile routes, up from 198,300 the quarter before. That increase matters — it reflects both growing need in the community and, frankly, growing capacity thanks to volunteer hours. We logged just over 4,100 volunteer hours this quarter across sorting, packing, driving, and client-facing distribution shifts. If you were one of the people who showed up on a Tuesday morning to sort a pallet of donated produce, that work is directly why those numbers moved in the right direction.

**Spotlight: The New Westbrook Mobile Route**

One of the developments we're proudest of this quarter is the launch of our new mobile distribution route serving the Westbrook and Cedar Grove neighborhoods. This area has been a persistent gap in our coverage — the nearest fixed pantry location is a 45-minute round trip by bus for many residents there, which is a real barrier when you're also juggling work schedules and childcare. The mobile route runs every other Thursday afternoon, currently stopping at three sites: the Westbrook Community Center, Cedar Grove Elementary (during pickup hours, with school permission), and the senior housing complex on Birch Street.

In its first six weeks, the route has served an average of 85 households per run, and that number has been climbing as word spreads. Big thanks to the small crew of regular route volunteers — Marcus, Denise, and Paul in particular — who've driven nearly every single run since launch and have gotten to know a lot of the families by name. If you're interested in joining the rotation, even occasionally, reach out to our volunteer coordinator, Grace, at the contact info below. We could use two or three more regular drivers as the route continues to grow.

Getting this route off the ground took longer than we initially hoped — we spent the better part of the spring working through insurance requirements for the van, coordinating site agreements with the school and senior housing complex, and building a distribution list of eligible households in a way that respected people's privacy while still letting us plan for volume. None of that shows up in a headline number, but it's worth naming, because a lot of you asked patiently for updates over those months and we appreciate that patience. We're already scouting a possible second mobile stop for early next year, likely somewhere along the north side of the county where we've heard from several families that transportation is the main barrier to accessing services.

**A Word From a Route Volunteer**

Denise, one of our regular Thursday drivers, shared this reflection after a recent run: "The thing that's stuck with me is how many people at the Birch Street stop tell us this is the first fresh produce they've had access to in weeks. It's easy to think of a food bank route as just moving boxes from one place to another, but standing there handing someone a bag of apples and watching their face change — that's not something you get from behind a table at the main pantry in quite the same way. I look forward to Thursdays now in a way I didn't expect when I signed up."

**Volunteer Appreciation Event**

Mark your calendars: our annual Volunteer Appreciation Dinner is happening on the evening of the 19th at the community center's main hall. This is our chance to say thank you properly — with actual sitting down and eating a meal together, rather than the usual quick wave across a folding table. There will be food (naturally), a short program recognizing some outstanding volunteer contributions from this past year, and honestly just a chance to catch up with people you've been working alongside without the pressure of a distribution line moving behind you. RSVPs are open now through our usual sign-up link; please let us know if you're bringing a guest, as we're finalizing catering numbers by the end of next week.

We'll also be presenting our Volunteer of the Year award at this event. If you'd like to nominate someone who's gone above and beyond this year, send a short note to Grace explaining why — nominations close five days before the dinner.

**Call for Donations: Canned Goods Ahead of the Holidays**

As we head into the holiday season, we're seeing our usual seasonal spike in need, and our canned goods inventory is running lower than we'd like heading into it. We're specifically asking for:

- Canned vegetables (low-sodium preferred)
- Canned beans and legumes
- Canned soups and stews
- Shelf-stable proteins (canned tuna, chicken, peanut butter)

Donations can be dropped off at either of our two pantry locations during regular hours, or at any distribution event — several of you have mentioned bringing a bag along on your regular volunteer shift, which is exactly the kind of thing that adds up fast across a whole volunteer base. If you'd prefer to organize a collection drive through your workplace, school, or place of worship, reach out to us and we'll provide flyers and collection bins.

**A note of thanks**

None of the numbers above happen without the people behind them. Whether you sorted one shift this quarter or showed up every single week, thank you. We see it, and more importantly, so do the families we serve.

Warmly,
The Riverside Food Bank Team
