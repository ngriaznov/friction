**Subject:** Fall Quarter Update: 214,000 meals, a new route, and an invitation

Hi everyone,

The leaves are finally turning and the warehouse is finally cool again, which means it's time for the quarterly update. As always, none of the numbers below happened because of the staff. They happened because roughly 340 of you showed up, sorted, drove, lifted, and stayed late. Thank you.

---

## By the numbers

Between July and September, Riverbend Community Food Bank distributed **214,600 meals** across our service area. That's up about 11% from the same quarter last year, and it's the highest third-quarter total in our fifteen years.

A few figures behind that number:

- **68 partner agencies** received regular deliveries, including 22 school-based pantries that came back online in late August.
- **412,000 pounds of food** moved through the Warren Street warehouse. About 31% of that was fresh produce, which is the highest share we've ever managed in a summer quarter.
- **9,180 volunteer hours** were logged. The single busiest day was August 14th, when 61 of you processed a surprise 14-pallet donation from a regional distributor in about six hours.
- **Average cost per meal** held at $0.61, down two cents from last quarter, largely because of the produce rescue partnerships described below.

The 11% increase is worth sitting with for a moment. It is genuinely good news that we were able to meet that demand. It is not good news that the demand exists. Our intake team reports that first-time households are up sharply — nearly a third of the families we served in September had never used a food assistance program before. Rent and utility costs come up in almost every conversation.

---

## Spotlight: the Eastvale mobile route

In June we quietly launched a mobile distribution route serving Eastvale, Kirkwood Heights, and the trailer communities along Route 9. Three months in, it's working, and it deserves more than a line item.

The problem it solves is simple and had been obvious to us for years. Our main pantry is on the west side of the river, and there is no bus line that reaches it from Eastvale in under ninety minutes with two transfers. Families were telling our intake volunteers that they could get food or they could keep their shift at work, but not both.

The route runs every other Thursday out of a refrigerated box truck, with three stops: the Eastvale Community Center parking lot, the lot behind Kirkwood Heights Elementary, and a pull-off on Route 9 near the Maple Grove entrance. Each stop is open for ninety minutes. There's no appointment, no proof of address, and no form beyond a household size count that we use only for planning.

Through the end of September the route has served **1,940 households**, about 60% of whom had never been to any Riverbend site before. The Kirkwood Heights stop has been the busiest by a wide margin, and we're evaluating whether to split it into a separate weekly run in the spring.

Enormous thanks to Marisol Reyes and Dan Whitaker, who drove nearly every run this quarter, and to the eight volunteers who rotate through the loading and distribution shifts. If you have a clean driving record and are comfortable with a 16-foot box truck, we would love to add two more drivers to the rotation — reply to this email and I'll get you connected with Marisol for a ride-along.

## Canned goods drive: now through December 12

Here is the part where I ask you for something.

Holiday demand starts climbing in early November and peaks in the week before Christmas. Our fresh produce supply is in good shape thanks to the rescue partnerships, but shelf-stable protein and canned goods are where we run thin every single year.

**Most needed, in rough priority order:**

1. Canned tuna, chicken, and salmon
2. Peanut butter (plastic jars only, please — glass breaks in transit)
3. Canned beans of any kind
4. Canned soup and stew, low-sodium if possible
5. Canned vegetables and fruit packed in juice rather than syrup
6. Rice and dried pasta in sealed bags

**Please don't send:** anything past its date, anything in glass, homemade or home-canned items, or opened packages. We have to discard all of these and it costs us volunteer hours to sort them out.

Drop-off is at the Warren Street loading dock, Monday through Friday 8 a.m.–4 p.m. and Saturdays 9 a.m.–noon. If you'd like to run a collection at your workplace, church, or building, email Priya at pnair@riverbendfood.org and she'll send you a bin, a flyer, and a pickup date.

If canned goods aren't practical for you, a cash donation goes further — our purchasing power means a dollar buys roughly three meals, which is better than almost anyone can do at retail.
