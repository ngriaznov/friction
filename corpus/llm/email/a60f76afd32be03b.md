## What happened

Between roughly 02:15 on Monday, November 3 and 16:40 on Thursday, November 6, the Merchant Insights dashboard displayed figures that were up to three days out of date. The dashboard did not error, did not show a stale-data warning, and did not show gaps. It showed Sunday's numbers, confidently, with Thursday's date in the header.

Fifty-one merchant accounts opened the dashboard during the affected window. We do not believe anyone made a materially wrong business decision as a result, but at least one merchant spent time reconciling a discrepancy that did not exist, and that is a real cost we imposed on a customer.

Nobody on our side noticed for three days. That is the part of this incident I want us to sit with.

## How it was found

At 15:20 on Thursday, a support engineer escalated a ticket from a mid-size merchant who had written in to ask why their weekly payout total in the dashboard did not match the total in their settlement statement. Their assumption — reasonably — was that the settlement statement was wrong.

Support did the right thing and pulled the underlying settlement records before answering. The records were correct and current. The dashboard was not. They escalated to data-platform at 15:34. We had the cause identified by 16:05 and a corrected dashboard by 16:40.

## Root cause

Two things went wrong, and only the second one is interesting.

**The proximate cause** was a schema change upstream. On Friday, October 31, the payments team shipped a migration that renamed `settlement.net_amount_cents` to `settlement.net_amount_minor`. This was a good change — it was part of standardizing multi-currency handling — and it was announced in the #payments-changes channel with a week of notice. Our extraction query in `load_settlement_facts` still referenced the old column name, so it began failing with a column-not-found error on the first run after the migration landed.

We were not subscribed to that channel. The announcement was made; it did not reach us. I do not want to frame this as the payments team's failure, because the mechanism we were relying on to hear about breaking upstream changes was "somebody happens to be in the right Slack channel," and that mechanism does not work.

**The actual cause of the three-day duration** was that our alerting was configured on the wrong object. We alert on *DAG-level* SLA misses — specifically, whether `merchant_rollup` completes within its SLA window. But `load_settlement_facts` failing causes its downstream tasks to be *skipped*, and a DAG run whose tasks are skipped rather than hung does not miss an SLA. It finishes quickly and unsuccessfully. Our SLA check looked at duration, saw a run that finished well inside the window, and reported healthy.

We also had no freshness check on the output tables themselves. Every monitor we had was watching the pipeline, and none was watching the data. When the pipeline failed in a way our monitors did not model, we had nothing left.

## Customer impact

- 51 merchant accounts viewed stale figures. We have the list from access logs.
- No incorrect payouts. Settlements run on a separate pipeline that was unaffected; only the reporting view was stale.
- One merchant spent an estimated 90 minutes reconciling. Their account manager has reached out and offered a credit.
- No data was lost. The backfill recovered the full window from source.

Support is sending a short factual notice to all 51 accounts today. Draft is with Maya for review; if you want to see it before it goes, ping her by 11:00 tomorrow.

## What we are changing

**1. Table-level freshness monitors on all published marts.** Every table backing a customer-facing dashboard gets a max-timestamp check that alerts if the newest row is older than 1.5× its expected refresh interval. This catches the whole class of failure regardless of what happened upstream. — *Owner: Dan Okafor. Due: Nov 14.*

**2. Task-level failure alerting, not just DAG-level.** Any task in a production DAG that ends in `failed` or `upstream_failed` pages the data-platform rotation. We will be noisy for a few weeks and will tune from there. — *Owner: Priya Raghunathan. Due: Nov 12.*

**3. Stale-data banner in the dashboard UI.** If the backing table's watermark is more than 26 hours old, the dashboard shows a banner saying so and displays the data's actual as-of timestamp. The dashboard should never be able to lie about how current it is. — *Owner: Ines Haddad, with Frontend. Due: Nov 21.*

**4. Contract tests against upstream schemas.** A lightweight CI job that runs each extraction query's column list against the current source schema in staging and fails the build on drift. This would have caught the migration on Oct 31, before it reached production. — *Owner: Dan Okafor. Due: Dec 5.*

**5. Formal notification path for schema changes.** Working with the payments team to route migrations through a subscribed channel with an explicit consumer list, rather than a general announcement channel. — *Owner: Priya Raghunathan and Tom Berger. Due: Nov 28.*

## One reflection

The uncomfortable thing here is not that a query broke. Queries break. It is that a customer was our monitoring system for three days, and that we had four separate dashboards showing green while the thing they existed to protect was wrong.

Every monitor we had was a proxy for correctness rather than a measure of it. Items 1 and 3 above are the ones I actually care about, because they check the property we want — the data is fresh — instead of checking something we hope implies it.
