**To:** engineering-all@, platform-leads@

All,

Here is the write-up for Tuesday's build system outage. No customer-facing service was affected, but every deployment across the company was blocked for just over five hours, and two teams missed a planned release window. Nothing in here is aimed at any individual — the failure was in our monitoring, not in anyone's judgment.

**Impact**

Between 08:41 and 13:45 UTC on November 12, the shared CI runner fleet was unable to complete builds. Jobs either failed within seconds with `no space left on device` or hung in the queue waiting for a runner that would never pick them up. In total term 611 jobs failed and 140 were queued at the peak. Deployments to production and staging were blocked for the whole window. The mobile team's 11:00 release cut was pushed to the following morning, and the billing team deferred a scheduled migration by a day.

**Timeline (UTC)**

- **08:41** — First job failures on runners 04 and 09. Both had filled their build volume.
- **08:44** — Failures spread as the scheduler shifted load to the remaining runners, which then filled in turn.
- **09:02** — First report in #eng-help ("is CI broken for anyone else?"). No alert had fired.
- **09:20** — Platform on-call picks up the thread and confirms disk exhaustion across the fleet.
- **09:35** — Incident opened at Sev-2.
- **09:50–11:30** — Manual cleanup begins. Docker layer caches and cargo/npm caches are cleared runner by runner. Each cleanup frees space, then refills within roughly twenty minutes as queued jobs resume.
- **11:30** — We stop the scheduler, drain the queue, and clear all sixteen runners before restarting, rather than fighting the refill.
- **12:10** — Fleet restarted with the queue paused. Volumes at 12–18% used.
- **12:15–13:45** — Queue released in batches to avoid another simultaneous fill. Backlog cleared.
- **13:45** — Incident closed.

**What actually caused it**

Two things combined.

The immediate trigger was a change merged on November 6 that added a multi-stage Docker build to the `payments-api` pipeline. Each run produced roughly 3.2 GB of intermediate layers, and our prune job only removed images untagged for more than seven days. Intermediate layers from a multi-stage build are untagged from birth, so they accumulated for six days and then aged into a window where the prune job would eventually have caught them — about a day too late.

The underlying cause is that we had no alerting on runner disk usage at all. We monitor CPU, memory, job duration, and queue depth on the fleet. Disk was never added, apparently because the runners were originally provisioned with volumes considered large enough that filling them was not a realistic scenario. That assumption stopped being true some time in the last two years and nobody revisited it. Had an alert fired at 80%, we would have had roughly eleven hours of warning.

The 21-minute gap between first failure and first human report is a secondary problem: we found out from a developer in a chat channel, not from a page.

**What we are changing**

1. **Disk-space alerting on the runner fleet.** Warning at 75%, page at 88%. Owner: Priya Raghavan. Target: November 19.
2. **Prune job rewritten** to remove dangling and intermediate layers on every run rather than on a seven-day age threshold, and to run hourly instead of nightly. Owner: me. Target: November 15. *(Done as of this morning — this is the highest-value fix and it is already live.)*
3. **Per-job disk budget.** The scheduler will refuse to place a job on a runner below 15 GB free, so a partially full fleet degrades in throughput instead of failing every job. Owner: Tom Feld. Target: December 3.
4. **Capacity review of the runner volumes**, including whether 200 GB is still the right size given how build images have grown. Owner: Priya. Target: end of quarter.
5. **Audit of the rest of the fleet dashboards** for other resources we monitor nowhere. Disk is unlikely to be the only one. Owner: me. Target: November 26.

I will post an update in #eng-platform when items 1 and 3 land. If your team lost work or has a release that still needs rescheduling, reply here or grab me directly and we will sort it out.
