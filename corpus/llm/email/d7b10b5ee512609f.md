**The blight**

Most of you on the east side already know, but for anyone who hasn't been down that row in a few weeks: I lost about two thirds of my tomatoes to early blight this season, and Marta and the Halloran plot took similar hits. It showed up around the third week of June, which is earlier than I've ever seen it here.

For anyone who hasn't dealt with it and wants to know what to look for: it starts on the oldest leaves, the ones nearest the soil. Small brown spots, maybe a quarter inch, with faint concentric rings inside them like a tiny target. A yellow halo around each spot. Then the whole leaf yellows and drops, and it walks up the plant one tier at a time. The fruit itself keeps setting for a while, which lulls you into thinking it isn't serious, but a plant with no lower canopy cooks its own fruit in July sun and you get sunscald on everything.

The thing I got wrong, and I'll own it, is that I saw the first spotted leaves and told myself it was just the usual bottom-leaf yellowing that happens to every tomato in a wet spring. I lost ten days to that. If you see targets with rings, it isn't nitrogen, it's blight, and ten days matters enormously.

**What actually worked**

I want to be careful here because I only have one season and no control plot, so this is one gardener's experience, not a study.

First and most important: sanitation. I stripped every affected leaf and everything below the lowest healthy branch, bagged it, and put it in the household trash rather than our compost. Do not compost blighted foliage. Our pile does not get hot enough to kill the spores and we will just be redistributing it next April. I went back through twice a week for the rest of the season and kept pulling anything spotted the day I saw it.

Second: mulch, heavily. Early blight overwinters in soil and gets onto plants by rain splash off the ground. I put down three inches of straw around each plant, right up to the stem, and that seemed to slow the spread noticeably on the plants I did first. In hindsight I should have mulched in May before any of this started, and I will next year.

Third: I switched to watering at the base with a wand, in the morning only, and stopped using the overhead sprinkler on that bed entirely. Wet leaves overnight are how this thing travels.

Fourth, and the only actual treatment: copper octanoide, the OMRI-listed one, sprayed every seven to ten days and reapplied after rain. I started it too late for the worst plants, but on the four Cherokee Purples I caught early, it held. Those four gave me fruit into September. If you use it, spray in the evening — I burned some foliage spraying midday in that heat wave, which was an unforced error on my part.

I also pruned hard for airflow. Anything touching a neighboring plant got cut. My spacing was too tight to begin with; I had them at about 18 inches and they should have been at 30 or more with cages.

What did not seem to help: the milk-and-water spray several people recommended, and the baking soda mix. I tried both on separate plants for three weeks and honestly couldn't see a difference against the untreated ones next to them. I'm not saying they do nothing, just that I couldn't see it.

For next year I'm rotating the tomatoes to the north end of my plot, which hasn't had nightshades in four seasons, and I've ordered Iron Lady and Mountain Magic seed, both of which are supposed to have real early blight resistance. Happy to split a packet with anyone — they come with far more seed than one plot needs.

**Fall planting reminder**

Quick reminder of the dates Jonah posted last month, since a few people asked me to repeat them:

- **Garlic:** cloves in the ground between October 15 and November 10. Earlier than that and they'll put up too much top growth before frost.
- **Spinach and mache:** sow by September 20 for a fall cut, or early October if you're overwintering under a low tunnel.
- **Fava beans:** late October, and give them something to lean on.
- **Cover crop:** if you're leaving a bed empty, crimson clover or winter rye needs to go in by the first week of October to establish.

The shed key rotation also changes on the 1st — Denise has it in September.
