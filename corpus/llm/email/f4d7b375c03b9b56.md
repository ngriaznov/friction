**The endgame from Okonjo–Halloran**

I want to spend a few paragraphs on the last-round game, because it's the most instructive thing that came out of the whole event and Marguerite has kindly agreed to let me pick it apart in public.

They reached a rook-and-pawn ending on move 41. Marguerite had rook and four pawns on the kingside; Devin had rook and four pawns, also kingside, but with his king cut off along the sixth rank and one pawn on a half-open file. Nominally dead level. Every engine you own will tell you it's a draw, and it is a draw, and Devin lost it anyway.

What went wrong is a thing I've watched strong club players do for twenty years: he activated his rook one move too late. On move 44 he had the choice between defending his weak pawn passively with the rook behind it, or abandoning the pawn and swinging the rook to the seventh rank. He defended. It's the human move — you don't want to just hand over material — but it condemned the rook to babysitting for the rest of the game, and once your rook is passive in a rook ending you are usually just lost, however equal the material count looks.

The counting exercise is worth doing at home. If he gives up the pawn and goes active, Marguerite queens two moves faster in the resulting race, but his rook gets behind her passed pawn and picks up two pawns of her own on the way back. It's a draw by about one tempo. That's the whole difference: one tempo, available for free on move 44, thrown away for the sake of not losing a pawn.

Marguerite, for her part, did the thing that wins rook endings, which is nothing dramatic at all. She improved her king for six consecutive moves, made no committal pawn pushes, and waited for the position to get better on its own. It took until move 63.

If you want to look at it properly, I've put the PGN of all 45 games on the club site under Tournaments → Autumn Round-Robin. Okonjo–Halloran is game 41.

**Simultaneous exhibition — Saturday 14 December**

Now the fun part. IM Katalin Varga has agreed to come and give a simul at the hall on Saturday 14 December. Some of you will know her from the county league; she played board one for Ashcombe for six seasons and has been doing coaching work since she stepped back from competitive play.

Details:

- **When:** Saturday 14 December. Boards set up from 13:00, first moves at 13:30. Expect to finish around 17:30, possibly later if a few games grind on.
- **Where:** the main hall, not the back room. We'll have 24 boards in a horseshoe.
- **Entry:** £8 for members, £12 for non-members, payable on the day or by transfer. This covers Katalin's fee and travel; anything left over goes to the junior section's clock fund.
- **Format:** she'll take White on all boards, standard simul rules — you move when she arrives at your board, and you may pass once per game but no more. No clocks.
- **Bring:** a set and board if you have a spare. We're about six short.

Twenty-four places, and I expect them to go. Reply to this email to claim one and I'll confirm in order of receipt. If we're oversubscribed I'll start a reserve list, and I'd ask that anyone who's played in a simul with us in the last two years give way to someone who hasn't.

Katalin has also offered to stay for half an hour afterwards to go over any game anyone wants to show her. Worth staying for even if you don't get a board.

Spectators are welcome and free. Bring family — a simul is one of the few chess events that's actually watchable.

Ordinary club nights continue as usual on Thursdays throughout. The winter Swiss pairings go out on 3 January.
