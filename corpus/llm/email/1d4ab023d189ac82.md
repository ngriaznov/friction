**Subject:** Incident summary — Nov 14 unintended send to full customer list (INC-2291)

**To:** engineering-all, marketing-all, support-leads, legal, exec-staff
**From:** Priya Raghunathan, Director of Lifecycle Marketing
**Severity:** SEV-2 (customer-facing, no data exposure)

Team,

Here is the full write-up of Tuesday's send. Short version: a campaign intended for 4,100 trial accounts went to all 218,000 addresses in our marketing database, including active paying customers, churned accounts, and every address that had ever entered a webinar form. No personal data was exposed to anyone other than the address owner, and no account was modified. What we sent was wrong-audience, not wrong-content-with-someone-else's-data. That distinction matters for our obligations but not for how annoyed people are, and a lot of people are annoyed.

**What happened**

The campaign was "Trial ending in 3 days — here's what you'd lose," a fairly aggressive upgrade nudge with a countdown block and a discount code. It was scheduled to send at 09:00 PT on Tuesday to the segment `trial_expiring_72h`.

At 09:04 our SMTP provider's dashboard showed a queued volume two orders of magnitude above forecast. Marketing ops noticed at 09:06 and hit the platform's Stop Campaign control at 09:07. By then roughly 141,000 messages had already been handed to the provider and were undeliverable-to-cancel. The remaining ~77,000 were successfully halted in queue.

So: about 141,000 people received an email telling them their trial was ending in three days. Many of them have been paying us for years. Several hundred have been churned long enough that the email was their first contact from us in over a year.

**Immediate response**

- 09:07 — send halted.
- 09:15 — incident channel opened, support notified ahead of the queue spike.
- 09:40 — all scheduled campaigns for the week paused platform-wide pending review.
- 11:20 — correction email approved by legal and sent to the affected 141,000. Plain text, no branding beyond the wordmark, no CTA, no discount code. It said we sent it in error, that their account and billing were unaffected, and apologized without hedging.
- 11:20 onward — every unsubscribe generated by either email honored immediately rather than on the usual nightly batch. We processed 3,847 unsubscribes across Tuesday and Wednesday, against a normal two-day baseline of about 190.

Support took 1,190 tickets and 74 phone calls related to the send. The dominant theme was not anger about the email itself but confusion about billing — a meaningful number of long-term customers genuinely believed something had gone wrong with their subscription. Support's macro was live by 09:55 and I want to call out that turnaround specifically.

**Root cause**

The segment `trial_expiring_72h` was defined as a filter chain: account status equals `trial`, AND trial end date within 72 hours, AND marketing consent equals true.

Ten days earlier, as part of the lifecycle-stage cleanup, the `status` field was migrated from a free-text property to a controlled enum. The migration mapped the old value `trial` to the new enum member `TRIAL`. The segment's filter still compared against the lowercase string.

The platform does not error on a comparison against a value that no longer exists in the field's allowed set. It evaluates the condition as *unmatched* and — this is the part that turned a bug into an incident — its default behavior for a filter step that matches zero records is to pass the full upstream population through to the next step rather than return empty. The two remaining conditions were date-based and consent-based; the date condition had been widened to "any" during a test in October and never reverted, and consent is true for nearly everyone in the database. The chain collapsed to "everyone who consented to marketing."

The preview count in the campaign builder showed 4,102 recipients when the campaign was scheduled on Friday. Segment membership is recomputed at send time. Nobody looked again on Tuesday, and the platform gives no diff between scheduled-preview count and send-time count.

Three failures, in order of how much I care about them: the passthrough-on-empty default, which is genuinely dangerous and which we did not know about; the stale filter left behind by a schema migration that had no consumer audit; and the absence of any check between what we approved and what we sent.

**What we're changing**

1. **Send-time volume gate.** Any campaign whose send-time recipient count exceeds its scheduled-preview count by more than 20% halts and pages marketing ops. This is built and in staging; live by Nov 25.
2. **No implicit passthrough.** All segments migrated to explicit inclusion logic, with a lint check that fails any filter chain containing a comparison against a value not present in the target field's schema. Owner: Dana Whitfield, target Dec 5.
3. **Migration consumer audit.** Any schema change to a field used in segmentation now requires enumerating and updating dependent segments before merge. Added to the data platform change template.
4. **Second approver for sends above 25,000.** Process-only, effective immediately.
5. **Standing kill-switch drill,** quarterly, so we know our actual stop latency rather than assuming it.

I approved this campaign and I own the outcome. Questions to me directly; the timeline and provider logs are in the INC-2291 doc.
