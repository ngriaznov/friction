**Subject:** Moving the internal wiki off Confluence (six-week plan, and what we need from you)

Team,

Starting next Monday we're migrating our internal documentation from Confluence to a self-hosted MkDocs site. I want to lay out why, what the schedule looks like, and the one thing each of you will need to do.

**Why we're doing this**

Three reasons, in the order they mattered.

Licensing. We're paying per seat for Confluence, and the last renewal quote came in about 40% above what we budgeted. That number kept growing as we hired. MkDocs is free, and the hosting cost lands somewhere around $30 a month on infra we already run.

Search. This is the complaint I hear most. Confluence search returns the 2021 version of the runbook before the current one, and there's no reliable way to fix that beyond archiving pages one at a time. MkDocs indexes a flat directory of Markdown, so what's in the repo is what's searchable, and stale docs get deleted rather than buried.

Review. Docs will live in the `handbook` repo. Changes go through pull requests, which means the same review flow we already use for code, plus history that actually tells you who changed what and why. Several of you have asked for this specifically when updating on-call procedures.

**Timeline**

- **Weeks 1–2 (Mar 3–14):** Platform stands up the MkDocs site, auth via our existing SSO, and a search index. No action needed from anyone.
- **Weeks 3–4 (Mar 17–28):** Bulk conversion. We're running an automated Confluence export to Markdown. It will handle text, headings, tables, and images reasonably well. It will mangle Confluence macros, embedded Jira queries, and anything with nested layouts.
- **Week 5 (Mar 31–Apr 4):** Cleanup window. This is the part that needs you.
- **Week 6 (Apr 7–11):** Confluence goes read-only Apr 7, and we cancel the subscription Apr 30.

**What you need to do**

During week 5, go through the pages you own and either fix them or delete them. You'll get an automated list of pages where you're the last editor — expect it Mar 28. For each one, pick one of three outcomes: keep it and clean up the conversion damage, merge it into an existing page, or delete it. Deleting is a legitimate and often correct answer. We currently have 2,400 pages and I would be surprised if 800 of them are still true.

If you've never opened a PR against a docs repo, Priya is running two 30-minute walkthroughs that week — Tuesday 10am and Thursday 2pm, same content, pick either.

Content decisions, or "does this page still need to exist": me.
Anything blocking you on the week-5 cleanup: flag it early rather than at the deadline. If the timeline doesn't work for your team, tell me now and we'll adjust.
