Subject: The walnut dining table is finally done

Hey all,

The walnut dining table is finished and moved into the house, so I figured I'd send the build rundown I promised a few of you back in the spring.

I started the wood selection at the same yard I always use, and this time I actually went twice — once to pick through the walnut stack cold, and once a week later after I'd slept on it, because the first visit I kept gravitating toward the flashiest grain and had to remind myself that four boards need to actually read as one tabletop, not four auditions. I ended up with five 8/4 walnut boards, bookmatched two of them for the center of the top, and set aside a sixth board that had some sapwood along one edge for the apron, where it wouldn't show. Walnut isn't cheap, so I probably spent more time standing in that yard doing mental math than I did on any single step of the actual build.

The glue-up is where things went sideways. I was gluing up the legs — laminated from three strips each, rather than solid 8/4 stock, mostly to control movement — and got impatient on leg three. I didn't check that all three strips were fully seated against the cauls before I walked away to mix more glue, and by the time I came back one strip had crept just enough that the leg cured slightly out of square. Nothing dramatic, maybe a sixteenth of an inch, but enough that it would have thrown off the whole base once assembled. I ended up re-planing that entire leg by hand rather than trying to shim it later, which cost me the better part of an evening and taught me to dry-clamp every glue-up now, no exceptions, even the ones that feel routine.

For finish I went with a wipe-on oil-varnish blend as the base — three coats, sanding lightly between each — followed by two coats of a hard wax oil on top for a bit more water resistance around a dining table's actual life. No film finish; I wanted the grain to still feel like wood under your hand, not like it's under glass. It's not the most bulletproof choice for a family that eats every meal there, but it's easy to spot-repair down the road, which mattered more to me.

All told, from milling the rough boards to the last coat of wax curing, it came to right around 70 hours spread over six weekends. That's slower than I'd planned, almost entirely because of the leg re-do, but I'm glad I didn't rush past it.

Happy to talk grain matching or glue-up clamping over the group chat if anyone's mid-project. Pictures are in the usual shared folder.

Take care,
Dan
