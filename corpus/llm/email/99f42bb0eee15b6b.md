**Subject:** Updated parental leave policy, effective 1 September

Hi everyone,

We're making a change to our parental leave policy that takes effect on 1 September. The short version: paid leave is going from 8 weeks to 16 weeks, it now applies equally regardless of whether you gave birth, and the one-year service requirement is gone. Details below.

**What's changing**

Starting 1 September, all new parents receive **16 weeks of fully paid parental leave**. This is a single number that applies to birth parents, non-birth parents, adoptive parents, and parents through surrogacy or foster-to-adopt placement. Previously we offered 8 weeks to birth parents and 4 weeks to everyone else, which was a distinction we no longer think we can defend.

Leave can be taken **in up to three blocks** within the first 18 months after birth or placement. Each block must be at least two consecutive weeks. This is new — the old policy required leave to be taken continuously, which meant a lot of people came back before they were ready in order to bank time they then couldn't use.

**Who's eligible**

Every regular employee, full-time or part-time, from your first day. The previous 12-month service requirement has been removed entirely. Part-time employees receive the full 16 weeks at their normal pay rate; we do not prorate parental leave by scheduled hours.

Contractors and agency staff are not covered, as their arrangements are set by their own employers.

**How this works with short-term disability**

For birth parents, short-term disability (STD) still covers the medical recovery period — typically 6 weeks for an uncomplicated delivery, 8 for a caesarean, longer if your physician certifies it. STD and parental leave now run **sequentially, not concurrently**.

In practice: you file the STD claim as usual through Meridian Benefits, receive your STD benefit during the recovery period, and your 16 weeks of parental leave begins when the STD period ends. Under the old policy the two overlapped, which quietly reduced the effective leave to roughly 8 weeks total. That's fixed.

Because STD pays at 70% of base salary, we top up the difference so your take-home is unchanged for the full duration. You don't need to request the top-up; payroll applies it automatically once the STD claim is approved.

**If your leave is already in progress or scheduled**

If you're currently on leave, or have leave scheduled to begin before 1 September, you'll be moved to the new terms automatically. Nobody has to reapply, and nobody loses time. If you took leave earlier this year and returned, and your child was born or placed on or after 1 January, you're eligible for the difference between what you took and the new 16 weeks — please contact us and we'll work out the schedule with you and your manager.

**What you need to do**

Nothing right now. When you're ready to plan leave, notify your manager and submit the request in Workday at least 30 days in advance where that's possible. We understand that births and placements don't always give you 30 days.
