Subject: Ridgeview Astronomy Club Newsletter — This Month's Skies, Star Party Recap, and Upcoming Lecture

Hi everyone,

Hope the clear nights lately have found you out under the stars. Here's what's been happening with the club and what's coming up this month.

**Last Month's Star Party**

Our star party at Hollow Creek Overlook drew 47 attendees, our best turnout since the club started keeping count three years ago. A big thanks to everyone who brought scopes for public viewing — we counted at least nine setups on the field, ranging from Dave's 10-inch Dobsonian to a handful of small refractors that were perfect for letting kids get their first look at Saturn's rings. Conditions were about as good as we get locally: seeing was steady, transparency was solid until the humidity crept up around 11 PM, and we had a good two-hour window on Jupiter before it dropped too low in the west. Several of you mentioned it was the first time you'd seen the Great Red Spot clearly, which is always a nice milestone to watch happen in real time.

Thanks also to the three members who ran informal mini-talks for newcomers — explaining what they were looking at, how to star-hop, and answering the inevitable "is that a satellite" questions. That kind of volunteer teaching is exactly what keeps these events welcoming for people who've never looked through a scope before.

**Member Spotlight: Imaging the Orion Nebula**

This month's spotlight goes to Priya Ramaswamy, who recently upgraded to a new CMOS camera and used it to capture a striking image of the Orion Nebula from her backyard in town — no small feat given our local light pollution. Priya wrote up a short report on her process for anyone interested in astrophotography: she stacked roughly 90 exposures of 30 seconds each using a dual narrowband filter to cut through sky glow, then processed the stack in PixInsight. The resulting image resolves nebulosity structure in the wings that's usually washed out in single-shot exposures from urban skies. Priya's put together a short writeup with her full capture settings and processing steps, and she's offered to walk through it at a future meeting if there's interest — reply to this email and let us know if you'd want that as a future talk topic.

**Upcoming Guest Lecture: Exoplanet Detection**

We're excited to announce a guest lecture from Dr. Adaeze Nwosu, an exoplanet researcher, who will be speaking at our regular meeting on the transit method and radial velocity detection — how astronomers actually find planets around other stars, what current instruments can and can't detect yet, and what's coming with next-generation telescopes. This is a great one to bring a curious friend or family member to, even if they're not a regular club attendee. Details on time and location are on the club calendar; RSVPs help us plan seating, so please sign up if you're able to attend.
