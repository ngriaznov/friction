**Subject:** Firewall change request — egress from payments-router to Meridian gateway (prod + staging)

Hi Network Ops,

I'd like to request an egress firewall rule change to let a new service reach an external payment gateway. I've written this up in more detail than a ticket usually gets because there's a compliance angle and I'd rather you have the full picture before you start scoping it. Happy to move the details into your intake form once you've had a look — let me know which you'd prefer.

**What the service is**

We're standing up a service called `payments-router`. It sits between our order service and our payment providers, and its job is to pick a provider per transaction and normalise the responses. Right now all card traffic goes to our incumbent provider through a rule you set up in 2022. We're adding a second provider, Meridian, initially for European card transactions only, and the routing decision is what `payments-router` exists to make.

The service is already running in both environments. It just can't talk to Meridian yet, which is the entire ask here.

**Source**

- Kubernetes namespace `payments-prod`, workloads labelled `app=payments-router`.
- Egress leaves via the payments egress NAT gateway in eu-west-1 — the same one the incumbent provider rule already uses, so the source address as seen from outside is unchanged.
- Staging equivalent is namespace `payments-stage`, behind the staging egress NAT in the same region.

I've deliberately not listed pod addresses because they're ephemeral. If your tooling needs something more concrete than a namespace, the NAT gateway addresses are stable and I can send those over separately — I'd rather not put them in a mail thread.

**Destination**

- Meridian's production API endpoint and their sandbox endpoint, both hostnames they publish in their integration docs.
- HTTPS on 443 only. No other ports, no plaintext fallback.
- Meridian resolve to an address range they publish and commit to in their integration agreement. Their docs say the range is stable and changes are announced 30 days ahead. I can forward the relevant section of their documentation if it helps you decide between a hostname-based rule and a range-based one — you'll have a better view than me on which is less painful to maintain.

**Direction and scope**

Outbound only. Meridian never initiates a connection to us; result notifications come back on the same connection, and their asynchronous webhooks land on our existing public ingress, which already has a rule and doesn't need touching.

I'd like the rule scoped to `payments-router` specifically rather than the whole `payments-prod` namespace. There are three other workloads in that namespace and none of them have any business reaching a payment gateway. If a namespace-level rule is significantly easier for you to implement and maintain, say so and I'll take it, but the tighter scope is my preference and I think it'll read better at audit.

**Business justification**

Two things are driving this.

The first is cost. Meridian's rates for European card transactions are materially better than what we pay the incumbent, and finance's estimate is a meaningful annual saving on our current European volume. That's the number that got this funded.

The second is resilience, and honestly it's the one I care about more. We had a 40-minute payment outage in March when our single provider had a regional incident, and we had no route around it. Every card transaction failed for the duration. A second provider means `payments-router` can fail over rather than fail. That capability doesn't exist until this rule does.

There's a compliance dimension too: our PCI scope review this year flagged single-provider dependency as a concern. This work is the response to that finding, and our assessor is expecting to see progress at the next checkpoint.

**Timeline**

Our release freeze starts on the 14th and runs through the end of the month. We need this in place well before that, because the change is worthless to us if we can't exercise it.

What I'd like:

- **Staging rule by the 30th.** This unblocks integration testing against Meridian's sandbox. We have a two-week test plan that needs to start on the 1st to finish before freeze, so the 30th is the real constraint on the whole sequence.
- **Production rule by the 7th.** That gives us a week of production traffic at low volume — we'll route about 1% of European transactions through Meridian behind a feature flag — before the freeze locks things down.

If a standard change window works for both, that's fine; nothing here needs an emergency process. If the staging date is tight for you, tell me now rather than later and I'll move our test plan rather than compress it.

Meridian's integration documentation, their published address ranges, the architecture diagram showing the egress path, and the internal ticket with the finance and compliance justification attached. Say the word and I'll send whichever of those is useful.
