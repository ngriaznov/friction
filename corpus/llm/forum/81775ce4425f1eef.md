**Re: When is blocking actually worth it in Ticket to Ride?**

Posted by **narrowgauge_ned** — Sat 14 Mar, 21:47

Our group argues about this constantly, so here's how I think about it after far too many plays.

The honest starting position: blocking is usually *not* worth it. A route you don't need is points you'd have scored anyway plus trains you've spent for zero destination progress. If you claim a 4-length grey route purely to deny someone, you got 7 points and lost four trains of build capacity. That's a fine trade only if you actually cost them something bigger.

So the real question is what the block is worth to them, and there are three things I check before pulling the trigger.

**1. Is it a genuine chokepoint or just inconvenient?**

Look at the map, not at their face. Denying a route with two parallel alternatives is theatre — they reroute for one extra train and you've wasted four. The blocks that matter are the ones with no cheap detour: the double routes where you're taking the second half of a pair on a 2-3 player game, the single entry into a corner of the board, the crossings that everything funnels through.

On the US map, Duluth and Pittsburgh come up over and over for exactly this reason. Seattle–Vancouver in a 4+ player game where both halves are live is a much weaker block than it looks.

**2. How far along are they?**

Early blocking is mostly guessing. You don't know their tickets and neither does anyone else, so you're paying real trains for a coin flip. Mid-to-late is where it pays — by then their built network has shape, you can see the two ends they're trying to join, and the gap between them is often one or two obvious segments.

Late is also when the block actually sticks, because they no longer have the turns to build around it.

**3. What's in my own hand?**

This is the bit people skip. If I'm holding three destination tickets and I'm short on trains, blocking is a luxury I can't afford — every train spent off-plan is a ticket I might eat at the end. If I've completed everything and I'm sitting on spare trains looking for somewhere to dump them, blocking is nearly free and I'll happily do it.

Rough test I use: if failing my own remaining tickets would cost me more than the block plausibly costs them, don't block. Score the downside, not the satisfaction.

**The bit nobody mentions**

Blocking the leader helps everyone at the table, including the third player who did nothing. If you're second and you spend four trains kneecapping first, you may well have handed the game to third. Worth a moment's thought before you feel clever about it.
