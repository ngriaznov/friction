**Subject: Homelab NAS build — why I went RAIDZ2 (8x drives) instead of mirrors, and ashift notes**

Finally got the new NAS fully populated and thought I'd write up the reasoning in case it saves someone else a few nights of forum-diving like I did. Long post, sorry in advance.

**The setup**

8x 16TB drives (Seagate Exos X18, model ST16000NM000J), single vdev, RAIDZ2, running on TrueNAS SCALE. This box replaces an aging 4-bay unit that was creaking under the weight of a growing media library plus a handful of VMs and a Postgres instance for a few self-hosted apps.

**Why RAIDZ2 over mirrored vdevs**

I went back and forth on this for a couple of weeks. The mirrored vdev crowd makes a good case — better random IOPS, faster resilver times, and the ability to grow capacity incrementally by adding another mirror pair whenever you feel like it instead of committing to a full vdev's worth of drives upfront. For a lot of homelab use cases, especially ones running several VMs off the pool, mirrors are probably the better call.

For me it came down to three things. First, usable capacity: 8 drives in RAIDZ2 gives me 6 drives' worth of usable space (75%), versus 4 drives' worth in a 4-pair mirror setup (50%). At 16TB a drive that's the difference between roughly 96TB usable and 64TB usable, and for a media-heavy pool where sequential throughput matters a lot more than IOPS, that gap was too big to ignore. Second, my workload is overwhelmingly sequential reads and writes — media streaming, occasional large file copies, nightly backup jobs — not random small-block I/O from databases or VM disks. RAIDZ2's weaker IOPS story matters far less when you're rarely doing random access in the first place. Third, and this is the one that actually tipped it for me: I wanted two-drive fault tolerance across the whole pool, not two-drive tolerance only if the failures happen to land in different mirror pairs. With 8 drives in a single RAIDZ2 vdev, any two drives can die and I'm still fine. With four mirror pairs, losing both drives in the same pair is fatal, and losing one from each of two different pairs is fine — so the actual protection is less uniform than "two-drive tolerance" makes it sound at a glance.

I did think seriously about splitting into two 4-drive RAIDZ1 vdevs striped together, which some people prefer for the extra IOPS from having two vdevs instead of one. I ruled it out because RAIDZ1 only tolerates a single drive failure per vdev, and with drives this large, resilver times are long enough that I wanted the extra margin RAIDZ2 provides during that vulnerable window.

**Picking ashift**

This one took more digging than I expected. `ashift` sets the minimum block size ZFS uses on the underlying vdev, expressed as a power of two — ashift=12 means 4K sectors, ashift=9 means 512-byte sectors. Get it wrong and you either waste space (ashift too high) or take a serious performance hit (ashift too low, forcing read-modify-write cycles on drives that are natively 4K).

The Exos X18 reports 512-byte logical sectors but is physically a 4Kn-capable drive family depending on SKU, and in my case `smartctl -a` confirmed 4096-byte physical sectors under logical 512-byte emulation (512e). That's the classic case where trusting the reported logical sector size would silently be wrong. I set `ashift=12` manually at pool creation rather than letting auto-detection decide, since I'd read enough horror stories of auto-detection picking ashift=9 on drives like this and locking in a performance penalty that isn't fixable without rebuilding the pool from scratch. Worth noting: ashift is set once per vdev at creation time and can't be changed later, so this is not a place to guess and fix it after the fact.

**What I'd do differently with hindsight**

Two things. First, I'd have bought two extra drives upfront and gone with 10 drives instead of 8 — same RAIDZ2 tolerance, better ratio of usable-to-total capacity, and I'm already eyeing an expansion a year in, which means buying more of the same drive at a worse price later instead of just doing it once. Second, I underestimated how long the initial resilver test (I intentionally failed a drive to see recovery behavior before putting real data on the pool) would take on drives this size — north of 14 hours — and I'd plan around that more deliberately if I were staging this again, rather than assuming it'd be closer to the few hours my old 4TB drives took.

Happy to answer questions if anyone's weighing a similar build.
