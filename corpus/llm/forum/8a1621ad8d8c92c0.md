**Re: Three-node mesh still has dead spots — finally solved it**

Posting a follow-up since a few of you helped me troubleshoot this back in the spring and I never came back with the answer. Short version: it was the backhaul, and I made it worse before I made it better.

Setup is a 1920s two-story, plaster and lath, about 2,100 sq ft. Fiber comes in at the front of the house on the ground floor. Three-node mesh kit, one node at the modem in the front room, one in the upstairs hallway, one in the back of the house near the kitchen.

The dead zone was the back bedroom upstairs — right above the kitchen node and about 25 feet from the hallway node. Phones would show two or three bars and then just stall. Speedtest would sit at "connecting" and time out.

Things I tried that did nothing useful:

- Moved the hallway node three different times. Best case gained me maybe 5 Mbps in the bedroom.
- Bought a fourth node. This actually made things *worse*, which is what finally pointed me at the real problem.
- Swapped channels manually, disabled band steering, re-enabled band steering, factory reset twice.

The fourth node making it worse was the clue. If adding capacity degrades the network, you're not short on coverage, you're short on backhaul.

Dug into the node status page and found that both the kitchen node and the hallway node were backhauling on **2.4 GHz**, not 5. The kit is supposed to prefer 5 GHz for node-to-node and only fall back when the link is too weak. My plaster walls have metal lath in them, which chews up 5 GHz badly, so both nodes had quietly decided 2.4 was the better link — and then every client in the back of the house was sharing that same congested 2.4 band with the backhaul traffic. Adding a fourth node just added another hop to the same choked pipe.

The fix was ethernet backhaul to the upstairs node. I already had a coax run to the upstairs hallway from the old cable setup, so I put a pair of MoCA adapters on it — 2 minutes of work, no drilling. Upstairs node now has a wired uplink, kitchen node meshes off it over a short clean 5 GHz hop.

Back bedroom went from ~8 Mbps and unusable to 340 Mbps. Removed the fourth node entirely, three is plenty.

If your mesh is underperforming, check what band your nodes are actually backhauling on before you buy more hardware. I wasted about $180 not doing that.
