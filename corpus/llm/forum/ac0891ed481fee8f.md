Start from the two components that actually matter, add headroom for spikes, then treat efficiency as a separate question from wattage.

**Add up the real draw.** For a mid-range CPU like a Ryzen 7 7700X, package power under an all-core load lands around 140 W. A high-end GPU — say a 4080-class card — sits around 320 W sustained. Everything else in the box is small: figure 10–15 W for a couple of NVMe drives and RAM, 5 W per case fan, a few watts for the board itself. Call it 40 W of miscellaneous and you're at roughly 500 W of steady-state load with both the CPU and GPU pinned, which is already a worse case than gaming produces (games rarely max both at once).

**Now handle transients.** This is the part that catches people. Modern GPUs draw in short bursts far above their rated board power — spikes of 2x the sustained figure lasting a millisecond or less are normal, and a 320 W card can momentarily pull 600 W or more. High-current CPUs do the same thing on a smaller scale. A power supply's rating is a *continuous* rating; whether it survives a 1 ms spike depends on its over-current protection design and its hold-up capacitance, not on the number on the box.

The practical rule that comes out of this: size the PSU at roughly 1.5x to 1.6x your calculated sustained load. For the 500 W example, that's a 750–850 W unit. You are not buying that capacity because you'll use it; you're buying it so the OCP threshold sits comfortably above the spikes. Undersizing here produces the classic symptom — the machine shuts off instantly under load with no BSOD and no thermal warning — which is protection working correctly, not a defect.

Check the GPU vendor's own recommendation too. It's usually conservative, but it's derived from their measured transient behavior on that specific board.

**Why efficiency rating isn't just about your electricity bill.** The 80 Plus tiers (Bronze, Gold, Platinum) certify how much input power becomes output power at 20%, 50%, and 100% load. Two things follow. First, waste heat: an 850 W load at 87% efficiency dumps about 125 W into your room and case; at 92% it's about 74 W. That's real, and it affects fan noise. Second — and this is the bigger point — efficiency tier correlates with build quality. Hitting Gold or Platinum requires better transformers, better capacitors, and usually a modern topology, and those same parts give you tighter voltage regulation and better ripple suppression. The tier is a proxy, not a guarantee, so read a review with actual load-testing before buying.

Also note that PSUs are most efficient near 50% load, which is another mild argument for the headroom you were already adding.
