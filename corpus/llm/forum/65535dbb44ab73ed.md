Wanted to close the loop on this since I posted a while back complaining about dead zones and got a couple of useful replies. House is a two-story, roughly 2200 sq ft, brick exterior which I now understand was a big part of my problem.

Started with the mesh kit set up the "easy" way - main node next to the router in the office downstairs, one satellite in the living room, one satellite upstairs in the hallway outside the bedrooms. On paper that should have covered everything, but I had a consistent dead zone in the back bedroom upstairs and a weirdly bad spot in the kitchen, which is directly below the living room node.

First thing I tried was just moving nodes around, mostly guessing. Moved the upstairs satellite from the hallway to inside the back bedroom itself, which helped that room obviously (it's now in the same room as the device using it) but made the hallway and the other upstairs bedroom worse, so that was just shuffling the problem rather than fixing it. Put it back.

The actual breakthrough was checking what backhaul band each node was using, which I hadn't even thought to look at initially. Pulled it up in the app and the living room node - the one causing the kitchen dead zone - had dropped from a wireless 5GHz backhaul connection back to the main node down to 2.4GHz at some point, presumably because of interference or distance, and I hadn't noticed because the app doesn't exactly surface this loudly. A 2.4GHz backhaul link eats a ton of the node's available capacity just talking to the main node, leaving very little bandwidth for actually serving nearby devices, on top of the fact that 2.4 has worse throughput to begin with. That explained the kitchen: it's technically in range of the living room node's client signal, but that node itself was starved by a bad backhaul link.

Brick construction turned out to be a big part of why the 5GHz backhaul was dropping in the first place - brick and especially anything with rebar or metal lath in it absolutely murders 5GHz signal penetration compared to drywall. The living room node had a decent line of sight to the office node horizontally but there was an interior brick chimney chase between them that I hadn't accounted for.

Fixes that actually worked, in order of impact:

1. Ran a wired backhaul (ethernet, through the crawlspace) from the office to the living room node instead of relying on wireless mesh backhaul. This alone fixed the kitchen dead zone completely and freed up a ton of capacity generally.
2. Kept the upstairs satellite in the hallway (not the bedroom) but raised it - it was sitting on a low bookshelf, moved it up to a wall mount closer to ceiling height, which gave it better line of sight over the bedroom doorways instead of shooting straight into a wall.
3. Turned off band steering client-side stubbornness on a couple of older 2.4GHz-only IoT devices that were apparently holding weak connections to the wrong node instead of roaming - forced them onto static assignments.

Wired backhaul was the fix that mattered most by a wide margin. If your house has any masonry between nodes, seriously consider running at least one ethernet backhaul run even if it's a pain - wireless mesh backhaul on brick construction is fighting a losing battle no matter how good the nodes are individually.
