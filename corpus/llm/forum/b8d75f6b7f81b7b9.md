Posted by **loamandclay** | Zone 6b, heavy clay, raised beds

Finally pulling the tomatoes this week so here's my honest writeup. Third year growing, first year I actually kept notes instead of guessing.

Setup: 14 indeterminate plants across two 4x12 beds. Bed A got basil planted between every tomato (about 10" off the stem) plus French marigolds along both long edges. Bed B was tomatoes and bare soil, mulched with straw. Same varieties split between beds — Cherokee Purple, Sungold, and a paste type — same drip line, same feeding schedule.

**What seemed to actually help**

Hornworms. Bed B lost three plants to serious defoliation before I caught them. Bed A I pulled exactly two hornworms all season and neither had done much damage. I'm not going to claim the basil repelled them because I have no idea, but the difference was big enough that I noticed without looking for it. Possible it's just that the basil made the bed messier to hunt in and the wasps found them first — I did see a lot more parasitized worms in A.

Soil moisture was noticeably better in the basil bed. Living mulch effect, basically. During the two-week dry stretch in July, Bed B was cracking at the surface and A wasn't. I'd count that as the most concrete win.

**What did nothing I could measure**

Marigolds. I want them to work. I planted them because everyone says nematodes. I have no evidence of nematodes either way, and they did not appear to affect aphid pressure at all — I had aphids on the undersides in both beds in equal measure. They looked nice and the bees liked them, which is enough reason to keep planting them, but I'm not going to pretend they earned their spot on pest control.

Also no difference in early blight. Both beds got it, both beds got it at roughly the same time, and stripping the lower leaves helped equally in both. Spacing and airflow matter way more than what you plant next to what.

**Changing next year**

Going to space the basil further out — 10" was too tight and by August the basil was competing hard and going leggy trying to reach light. 16" or so.

Dropping marigolds from between the plants and keeping them only as a border where they don't cost me tomato room.

Adding a bed with nasturtium as a trap crop for the aphids. If anyone's done that with tomatoes specifically I'd like to hear whether it pulled them off or just gave the aphids a bigger party.
