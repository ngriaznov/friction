**Subject: 8-wide RAIDZ2 build — my reasoning, and the one thing I got wrong**

Posting this partly because I read about thirty threads here while planning and figure I owe one back, and partly because I want it written down somewhere before I forget why I did any of it.

**The build**

- Supermicro X11SSH-F, E3-1240v6, 64GB ECC
- LSI 9211-8i flashed to IT mode, P20
- 8 x 12TB HGST He12 (HUH721212ALE604), shucked from externals over about four months
- Single RAIDZ2 vdev, ashift=12, lz4
- Boot on a mirrored pair of cheap SATA SSDs
- Fractal Define 7 XL, which fits all of it with room to be annoyed later

Usable capacity comes out around 65TiB after the RAIDZ2 parity overhead and the usual 80% fill target I'm trying to respect.

**Why RAIDZ2 and not mirrors**

I know the standing advice around here is "mirrors unless you have a reason." I had a reason, or at least I convinced myself I did.

The workload is bulk storage. Media library, a Time Machine target, photo archive going back to 2004, and offsite backup landing zone for two family members. Sequential reads, big files, almost no random IO. There is no database on this box and there never will be — if I add VMs later they go on a separate SSD mirror, which was always the plan.

With 8 x 12TB drives:

- 4 mirrored vdevs: ~44TiB usable
- 1 x RAIDZ2: ~65TiB usable

That's 21TiB of difference, or about $400 of drives at what I was paying, and for a media pile that's real money. The IOPS penalty — a RAIDZ2 vdev gives you roughly one drive's worth of random IOPS — costs me nothing on this workload.

The resilver argument is the one that gave me pause, and it's the one people should actually weigh. A 12TB resilver in an 8-wide RAIDZ2 is a long, hot, all-drives-reading event, and it's exactly when a second drive is most likely to fall over. But RAIDZ2 tolerates two failures, and mirrors only tolerate the *right* two — lose both halves of one vdev and the pool is gone. With eight drives in four mirrors, a second failure kills the pool 1-in-7 times. RAIDZ2 survives any second failure, full stop. I'd rather have the guaranteed second-failure tolerance than the faster resilver.

Also, and I say this as someone who's been burned: none of this is a backup. I have a rotating pair of external drives and a Backblaze B2 bucket for the ~4TB that would actually hurt to lose. RAID level is an availability decision, not a durability one.

**ashift**

This one took the most reading. The He12s report 512e — 512-byte logical, 4096-byte physical. ZFS's autodetection sometimes believes the logical number, which gets you ashift=9 on a 4K drive, which gets you read-modify-write on every operation and a pool you cannot fix without a full rebuild. ashift is set per-vdev at creation and is immutable.

I checked the actual physical sector size before creating anything:

```
# smartctl -a /dev/sda | grep -i sector
Sector Sizes:     512 bytes logical, 4096 bytes physical
```

4096 physical means ashift=12. I set it explicitly at pool creation rather than trusting detection:

```
# zpool create -o ashift=12 tank raidz2 /dev/disk/by-id/ata-HGST_HUH721212ALE604_*
```

I considered ashift=13 as insurance against ever replacing a dead drive with a native-8K disk. Decided against it: it wastes space on small blocks and inflates RAIDZ padding, and 8K-sector spinning rust still isn't a thing years after people started worrying about it. If it ever becomes one I'll deal with it at replacement time.

Use `/dev/disk/by-id`, by the way, not `/dev/sdX`. Ask me how I know which drive is which after a controller reorders them on reboot.

**What I'd do differently**

Three things.

*Buy the drives faster.* Shucking over four months to catch sales saved maybe $500 and cost me a month of pool downtime waiting on the last two, plus it means all eight are from different production runs — which is arguably a feature for correlated-failure reasons, but I didn't do it on purpose and I'd rather have had the pool up.

*Plan for expansion I actually understood.* I built this before RAIDZ expansion was widely usable and told myself I'd just add a second 8-wide vdev later. Then I looked at the case, the HBA ports, and the PSU, and realized "later" means a new chassis. If I were starting over I'd either build the second vdev's worth of bays in from day one or accept up front that this pool's capacity is fixed at 65TiB forever.

*Set up monitoring on day one, not month four.* I ran for months with no scrub schedule and no SMART alerting. Found out about a drive throwing reallocated sectors because I happened to run `zpool status` for an unrelated reason. Now: monthly scrubs via cron, smartd emailing me, and a Prometheus exporter feeding a dashboard I actually look at.

The thing nobody tells you is that the hard part isn't the vdev layout. It's remembering to check on it eighteen months later.
