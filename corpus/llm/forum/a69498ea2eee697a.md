**Re: Post your builds — finished my first hot-swap board, long-ish writeup**

Been lurking here for a couple of months while I collected parts, so I figure I owe the thread an actual build post. Nothing exotic — 65% aluminum case, hot-swap PCB, PC plate, screw-in stabs — but a few decisions I went back and forth on that might be useful to somebody else in the same spot.

**Linear vs tactile**

This ate up more of my time than anything else in the build, and I'd bet I'm not alone.

I came from a rubber dome and a cheap board with clicky blues that I hated after about a month. So I knew I didn't want clicky. That left linear or tactile, and the internet was no help because every thread is people telling each other their preference is objectively correct.

I ended up buying a switch tester and a few loose singles, which I'd recommend to anyone on the fence — the ten bucks or so is cheap compared to buying 70 switches you end up hating. Tactiles felt great for the first ten minutes and then started feeling like work. That bump is information, and I found my fingers kept slowing down to wait for it. Linears felt boring in isolation but noticeably better once I was actually typing sentences instead of mashing individual keys.

Went with 63.5g linears, factory lubed. Would have hand-lubed 70 switches if this were a second board, but for a first build I wanted to remove one variable.

**Stabilizers**

Everyone in this subforum says to mod the stabs before installing them and everyone is right. I ignored it initially, put the stock ones in, tested the board, and the spacebar sounded like a plastic bag. Pulled everything back out and did it properly.

For anyone who hasn't:

1. Clip the little feet off the bottom of the inserts — flush cutters, don't get greedy.
2. Band-aid mod: I cut small squares from a fabric bandage and stuck them to the PCB where the stab housing lands. The rattle you hear on a stock spacebar is largely the housing bottoming against the board, and a bit of padding kills it.
3. Lube. Dielectric grease on the wire ends where they sit in the housing, a thin dab of thicker grease inside the housing itself. Do *not* get lube on the stem where it contacts the keycap.

Difference is not subtle. Spacebar went from rattly to a low thock, and the backspace and enter keys stopped sounding like a completely different keyboard from the alphas. If you do one mod on a build, do this one.

**A week in**

Typing on it now. Honest impressions:

- The sound is the thing I underestimated. It's a lower, denser sound than I expected from an aluminum case, mostly thanks to a strip of foam between plate and PCB.
- My typing speed dropped for two days and then came back a little higher than before. Roughly 95 wpm before, sitting around 102 now on the same test. Some of that is probably enthusiasm rather than the board.
- 65% layout: took about three days to stop reaching for a dedicated arrow cluster that isn't there. Now I don't think about it. The one thing I miss is a physical delete key, though I remapped it onto a layer and that's mostly fine.
- Hot-swap was worth it purely for peace of mind. I've already pulled and reseated two switches that felt scratchy out of the bag. No soldering iron, thirty seconds each.

Total cost was about $210 including keycaps, which is more than I meant to spend and less than I feared. Happy to answer questions if anyone's putting together a similar spec.
