**Re: Anyone actually gotten an RTO exception approved?**

Yes, and it took about seven weeks, so settle in.

Background: we went from fully remote since 2020 to a 4-day mandate announced in September, effective January. I live 90 minutes from the office each way and I took this job as a remote role in writing. Roughly forty of us in the same boat.

**What I did first, which was mostly wrong**

My opening move was a long email to my manager about commute time, fuel cost, and the fact that my offer letter said "remote." I felt very righteous about it. He forwarded it to HR and I got a form response about "the policy applies uniformly" within a day.

Lesson: fairness arguments and contract arguments both route straight to HR, and HR's entire job in that moment is to not create a precedent. You want your manager solving a problem, not adjudicating a claim.

**What actually worked**

I rewrote the whole thing as a coverage problem instead of a me problem.

I'm one of two people who can debug the batch settlement jobs. The other one is in Kraków. Our overlap window is 07:00–10:00 my time. If I'm commuting three of four mornings, that window drops to roughly one usable day a week, and every settlement escalation waits until afternoon. I pulled the last six months of incident tickets and showed that 11 of 17 P2s in my area were opened before 09:30.

Then I brought a proposal, not a request. Two office days a week, both Tuesday/Wednesday so I'm there for planning and the architecture sync. Mornings protected. I offered to be the primary on-call for the EMEA-hours rotation, which nobody wants.

My manager responded to that immediately. Turns out he'd been quietly worried about the same coverage gap and hadn't connected it to the RTO change.

**Where HR pushed back**

Two places.

First, they would not call it an exception. The word came back every time as "role-based work arrangement," documented against the job requirements rather than against me. Fine by me — I didn't care what it was called. But it meant my manager had to write a justification tied to the *position*, which took three drafts and a skip-level review.

Second, they refused anything permanent. Everything is reviewed at the six-month mark. I pushed on this and got nowhere, so I dropped it.

**The compromise**

Two days on site, Tue/Wed, reviewed every six months, and I agreed to come in for the quarterly planning week in full. Not the three-days-remote I opened with, but I keep my mornings and I stopped burning fifteen hours a week in the car.

**If you're about to try this**

Frame it as what breaks for the business if you're on a train. Bring numbers you didn't invent. Get your manager on your side before HR ever sees it, because once it's an HR matter it's a policy question and you will lose a policy question. And decide in advance what you'd actually accept, because they will meet you somewhere short of your opening position and you don't want to be figuring out your floor in the meeting.

Good luck. Happy to answer questions.
