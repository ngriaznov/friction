Finally got the 40 meter dipole up over the weekend and thought I'd write it up properly, since a lot of the threads here end at "it works great" without any actual numbers. Long post, sorry.

**Length calculation**

Started with the usual approximation:

Total length (ft) = 468 / f (MHz)

I wanted the dip near the middle of where I actually operate, which for me is CW at the bottom and some SSB ragchewing higher up. Compromised on 7.100 MHz.

468 / 7.100 = 65.9 ft total, so **32 ft 11 in per leg** (about 10.03 m).

I cut each leg at 34 ft to leave room for trimming, because you can always take wire off and you cannot put it back. Ended up trimming 9 inches off each side before I was happy, which put the finished legs at roughly 33 ft 3 in. A bit longer than the formula predicted — not surprising, mine is only about 30 ft up and fairly close to a metal shed on the east end. Height above ground and nearby junk both shift the resonant point, and the 468 number is a starting guess, not gospel.

Wire is 14 AWG stranded copperweld. Center insulator is a scrap of HDPE cutting board with three holes drilled in it, which cost nothing and has survived a winter already.

**Feedline and the balun question**

Fed with 100 ft of RG-8X. I considered ladder line and a tuner for multiband use, but I wanted this thing simple and single-band, and the coax run has to go past a metal downspout, so ladder line was going to be a headache.

Put a **1:1 current balun** (really a choke balun) right at the feedpoint. This is the part I'd argue with anyone about. A dipole is balanced, coax is not, and without a choke the outside of the shield becomes a third radiator. Symptoms are the classic ones: SWR that changes when you move the coax, RF getting into the shack, and a radiation pattern that isn't what you drew on paper.

Mine is 12 turns of RG-8X on an FT-240-31 core, taped down and hung in a small plastic enclosure so weight is on the support rope rather than the connector. Before I added it, I had a noticeable difference in reading depending on how the coax was dressed at the tower base. After, that went away completely. That test alone convinced me.

**SWR results**

Measured at the shack end with a NanoVNA after final trim, so these include ~100 ft of RG-8X loss. Real feedpoint SWR is a bit worse than shown, which is worth remembering — long coax runs flatter your numbers.

```
7.000 MHz  ...  1.6 : 1
7.050 MHz  ...  1.3 : 1
7.100 MHz  ...  1.1 : 1
7.150 MHz  ...  1.2 : 1
7.200 MHz  ...  1.5 : 1
7.250 MHz  ...  1.8 : 1
7.300 MHz  ...  2.2 : 1
```

Minimum sits at about 7.095, close enough to what I aimed for. Under 2:1 from 7.000 to about 7.270, which covers essentially everything I do. The rig's internal tuner handles the top of the phone band without complaint.

**Notes for anyone doing this next**

- Cut long, trim in small increments, and measure after every cut. Two inches per side moves the resonant frequency more than you expect.
- Trim both legs equally or you throw off the balance you just built a balun to protect.
- Support the coax weight at the center insulator, not at the PL-259.
- Seal the connector properly. Self-amalgamating tape then electrical tape over it. Water in coax is a slow, miserable failure.

Worked into Colorado on 40 the first evening with 80 watts. For maybe $40 of wire and a weekend, hard to beat.
