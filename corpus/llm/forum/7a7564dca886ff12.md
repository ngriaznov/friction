**Q: How do I write a systemd unit file for a custom daemon that should restart itself and be monitored for hangs, not just crashes?**

A: There are two separate problems here — restarting after a crash, and detecting a hang where the process is still running but no longer doing useful work. systemd handles both, but they use different mechanisms.

**Restart=on-failure**

This controls what happens after the process exits. Setting `Restart=on-failure` tells systemd to restart the unit if the process exits with a non-zero code, is killed by a signal, or times out — but *not* if it exits cleanly with status 0. That distinction matters: if your daemon can legitimately shut itself down (say, on `SIGTERM` during a planned stop), you don't want systemd fighting you by restarting it anyway. Compare that to `Restart=always`, which restarts unconditionally regardless of exit reason — usually too broad for anything other than the simplest daemons.

**Type=simple vs Type=notify**

`Type=simple` is the default: systemd considers the service "started" as soon as it forks the process, with no further communication. That's fine for basic cases, but it means systemd has no idea whether your daemon actually finished initializing or is just sitting there.

`Type=notify` requires your daemon to speak a small protocol to systemd via `sd_notify()`. When your process is ready to serve traffic, it calls `sd_notify(0, "READY=1")`. Until that call happens, systemd holds any dependent units waiting on `After=`/`Wants=` ordering, so you get correct startup ordering instead of guessing with a `Type=simple` unit and a `Sleep` hack.

**WatchdogSec and keepalives**

This is the piece that catches hangs rather than crashes. Set `WatchdogSec=30` in the unit, and your daemon must call `sd_notify(0, "WATCHDOG=1")` at an interval shorter than that (systemd recommends half the watchdog timeout, so every ~15 seconds here). If the daemon stops sending keepalives — because it deadlocked, got stuck in an infinite loop, or is blocked on a resource that will never free up — systemd treats the missed watchdog as a failure and restarts the unit per your `Restart=` policy, even though the process technically never exited.

**Example unit file**

```ini
[Unit]
Description=Custom telemetry daemon
After=network-online.target
Wants=network-online.target

[Service]
Type=notify
ExecStart=/usr/local/bin/telemetryd --config /etc/telemetryd/config.toml
Restart=on-failure
RestartSec=5
WatchdogSec=30
NotifyAccess=main

[Install]
WantedBy=multi-user.target
```

`NotifyAccess=main` restricts the notify socket to the main PID, which is usually what you want unless you're forking additional worker processes that also need to send notifications.

On the code side, wiring up `sd_notify` is a few lines with `libsystemd` (or the `sd-notify` crate if you're in Rust, or `systemd-notify` bindings in most other languages) — call it once at startup after `READY=1`, then on a timer for the watchdog pings. If your language doesn't have a convenient binding, you can shell out to the `systemd-notify` CLI tool, though a native call is cheaper if you're pinging every 15 seconds.
