**Re: Dropped stitch six rows down — do I have to rip back?**

No, don't rip. Six rows is nothing, and laddering it back up is genuinely one of the easier repairs once you've done it once. You need a crochet hook a bit smaller than your needle size — if you're on 4mm needles, a 3mm hook is about right. Too large and you'll fight it through the stitch, too small and you'll split the yarn.

First thing: secure the runaway. Put a locking stitch marker or a safety pin through the live loop before you do anything else, so it can't drop further while you're setting up. Nothing worse than turning a six-row problem into a twelve-row one because you sneezed.

Now look at the ladder. You'll see the dropped stitch as a loop with a series of horizontal strands above it — one strand per row it fell. Work bottom to top. Insert the hook into the loop from the front, catch the lowest strand, and pull it through the loop. That's one row done. The loop now sits on your hook. Repeat with the next strand up, and keep going until you've used the last one, then slip the final loop back onto your left needle in the correct orientation.

The bit that trips people up on stockinette or ribbing: knowing which side you're working from. Here's the tell. Hold the fabric with the knit side facing you. If the stitch you're rebuilding shows as a V, you're on the knit side and you pull the strand through from back to front. If it shows as a bump or purl bar, flip the work over and ladder it up from that side instead — don't try to do purls from the front, it's fiddly and you'll get the twist wrong.

In ribbing you'll be flipping back and forth every column, which is annoying but works fine.

Last thing: after you're done, the repaired column will look slightly loose. Give it a tug lengthwise and then leave it — blocking evens it out and you genuinely won't find it afterwards.
