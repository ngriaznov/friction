**Re: ABS corner lifting on tall prints — finally solved it (long post)**

Posting this because I searched this forum a dozen times over three months and every thread ended with "build an enclosure" and no follow-up. I built the enclosure. It was not enough on its own. Here is everything I changed, in the order I changed it, with what actually mattered.

**The problem:** printing an ABS enclosure part, roughly 60mm tall with a 140 x 90mm footprint. Bottom 20mm printed fine, then corners started curling up off the bed and by 40mm the part had visibly lifted at three of four corners. Two of those prints ended with the nozzle knocking the part loose entirely.

**Printer:** bed-slinger, stock hotend, glass bed. Nothing exotic.

---

**1. The enclosure**

Built one out of 2020 extrusion and 3mm acrylic panels, about 500 x 500 x 600mm, with a hinged front door. Sealed the gaps with adhesive foam weatherstripping. Total cost was under $90 since I already had the extrusion.

Chamber temp with the bed at 105C stabilizes around 44–46C after 30 minutes. That is not a heated chamber, it is just retained waste heat, but it made a real difference — corner lift went from "print fails" to "print completes but corners are visibly raised about 0.6mm."

Important: I did **not** put the power supply or the control board inside. They vent out the back through a cutout. Cooking your stepper drivers to fix warping is a bad trade.

**2. Bed adhesion, in order of what I tried**

- *Bare glass, 100C bed.* Baseline. Failed.
- *ABS slurry (acetone + scrap ABS).* Works well — genuinely tenacious grip. Also a pain to apply evenly and a pain to remove, and I got tired of smelling acetone. Would use again for a one-off critical part.
- *Kapton tape.* Marginal improvement over bare glass, not worth the bubble-free application hassle.
- *Glue stick, thin even layer.* Roughly as good as slurry for this part, far easier. This is what I settled on.
- *Bed temp 105C instead of 100C.* Small but consistent improvement. Above 105C the first layer got squishy and elephant-footed.

I also added a 6-line brim. Brim is doing real work here — it gives the corners more anchored area than the part itself has, and the corners are exactly where the lift starts. Skirt does nothing for this.

**3. Cooling — this was the actual fix**

I had the part cooling fan at 30% because "ABS doesn't like cooling" and some slicer profile told me so years ago. Dropping it to **0% after the first layer** is what took the remaining corner lift from 0.6mm to something I cannot measure with calipers.

The reasoning made sense once I stopped and thought about it: the enclosure exists to keep the print at a uniform elevated temperature so the layers shrink together instead of the cool upper layers pulling on the warm lower ones. Then I was actively blowing 45C air at 30% across the part and undoing that. Any fan at all inside an enclosure is fighting the enclosure.

The tradeoff is bridging and small overhangs. On this part it did not matter. For a part with fine detail I bump to 15–20% for those layers only via a slicer height range modifier, and accept slightly worse warping there.

**Current profile, for the record:** 245C nozzle, 105C bed, 0% fan after layer 1, 6-line brim, glue stick on glass, enclosure door closed and left closed until the bed is under 50C.

That last part matters more than I expected — opening the door to look at a finished print while it is still at 60C will crack a large flat part right off the bed. Ask me how I know.
