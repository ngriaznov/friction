**Re: Torn first curtain on a Praktica-era body — done, with pictures pending**

Posting this partly as a write-up and partly because I searched this board for three evenings before starting and found bits and pieces but nothing end to end. Body is a 1974 Mirandex ST-3, cloth focal plane shutter, the usual story: sat in a cupboard for thirty years, rubberised silk went brittle, and the first curtain had split along a fold about 8mm in from the ribbon end. At 1/60 you got a lovely bright stripe down the frame.

**Sourcing the curtain**

I looked at buying replacement rubberised silk by the sheet and cutting my own. Two people here have done that successfully and I salute them, but the material I could find was either 0.09mm stuff meant for larger formats or unobtainium at any sensible price. So: donor body. Picked up a wrecked ST-3 with a smashed prism and a seized wind lever for £22 including postage. Prism damage doesn't touch the shutter, and the seizure turned out to be dried grease in the wind train, which meant the curtains themselves had barely moved in decades and were still supple.

Worth saying — check the donor's curtains before you commit. Open the back, set B, hold it open and shine a torch through from the front. Pinholes show up instantly and they are much more common than tears.

**Getting in**

Order that worked for me:

1. Bottom plate off (four screws, one hides under the tripod bush trim ring on this model — it unscrews, it isn't glued).
2. Wind the shutter and *let it off* before going further. Working on a tensioned shutter is how you lose a fingertip's worth of skin and a curtain ribbon at the same time.
3. Top plate: rewind knob off first (hold the fork in the film chamber with a screwdriver, unscrew the knob anticlockwise), then the wind lever's decorative disc, then the accessory shoe, then the four perimeter screws.
4. Release the curtain drum tension by counting turns as you back off the ratchet pawl. **Write the count down.** Mine was 3¾ turns on the first curtain drum and 4¼ on the second. Getting this wrong is the difference between correct speeds and a shutter that limps.
5. The curtain ribbons attach to the drums with tiny cemented tabs. Soften with a drop of isopropyl on a cocktail stick, ten minutes, then they peel rather than tear.

Transferring the donor curtain took about ninety minutes of extremely fiddly work with tweezers and a magnifier lamp. The critical thing is the slit width — the gap between first and second curtain when both are travelling. I marked the original positions on the drums with a fine paint pen before disassembly and matched them exactly. Guessing here is not viable.

Re-cemented the ribbons with shellac-based cement thinned slightly. Left it 24 hours. Retensioned to the counts I'd written down.

**Checking the speeds**

Built the standard cheap tester: a photodiode and a resistor into a phone headset input, audio recorded in Audacity, count samples between the edges. At 44.1kHz you get 22.7µs resolution, which is plenty down to 1/1000.

Results after three attempts at tensioning:

| Marked | Measured | Error |
|---|---|---|
| 1s | 1.06s | +0.08 stop |
| 1/30 | 1/28 | +0.1 |
| 1/60 | 1/54 | +0.15 |
| 1/125 | 1/104 | +0.26 |
| 1/250 | 1/190 | +0.4 |
| 1/500 | 1/330 | +0.6 |
| 1/1000 | 1/560 | +0.83 |

Slow speeds fine, top end soft — classic under-tension. Another quarter turn on the second curtain brought 1/1000 to about 1/780, which for a fifty-year-old cloth shutter I am calling done. Also worth measuring at three points across the frame to check for taper; mine varied by about 8% left to right, which is invisible on negative film.
