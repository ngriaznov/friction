The short version: set the jig once, write down the numbers, and stop changing them. Most inconsistent grinds come from re-setting the fixture by eye every session rather than from anything wrong with technique at the wheel.

**Angles**

For a fingernail-profile bowl gouge, the nose bevel is normally somewhere between 55° and 65°, with 60° being the default most people land on. Shallower than 55° and the bevel stops riding the inside curve of a bowl — you get chatter and a lot of catches near the bottom. Steeper than 65° cuts fine but leaves a surface that needs more sanding, and pushes you toward a heavier cut.

The swept-back wings run considerably lower, around 40–45° at the tips. That is not something you set directly; it falls out of how far the tool projects from the jig and how much you rotate.

**Jig setup**

With a Wolverine-style Vari-Grind arm or a clone:

1. Set the tool projection from the jaws of the pivot arm. Most jigs want 2 in. (50 mm) for a standard grind. Use a setup block or a marked stick, not a tape measure held at arm's length.
2. Set the jig's leg angle. On a Vari-Grind that is the numbered scale on the arm — position 2 is a good starting point for a 60° nose on a 1/2 in. gouge.
3. Set the distance from the base pocket to the wheel face. This is the number that actually determines your bevel angle, and it is the one people forget. Measure it, then mark the V-arm with a scratch or a piece of tape so you can return to it exactly.

Once those three numbers are fixed, blacken the existing bevel with a marker and touch the tool to a stopped wheel. If the marker only comes off the heel, your pocket distance is too short; only at the edge, too long. Adjust until it wipes evenly across the whole bevel, then record the setting.

**Grinding**

Light pressure, wheel running, start with the nose square to the wheel and roll the tool through the flute to one wing, then back through the nose to the other. One continuous motion, not a series of stops. Two or three passes per side on a CBN wheel is plenty for a touch-up. Let the jig do the geometry — you are only providing the rotation.

**Checking consistency across the wing**

Three checks, in order of usefulness:

- **Marker test again.** Re-blacken and take one very light pass. Any spot where the marker survives is a spot the wheel is not reaching, which means the roll paused or sped up there.
- **Look at the grind lines.** On a well-rolled edge the scratch pattern runs in a smooth fan. A band of lines at a different angle marks where you hesitated.
- **Thumbnail or paper.** Drag the edge lightly along a thumbnail from nose to wing tip. It should bite uniformly. A section that skates is dull or blunted.

Also sight down the flute from the handle end: both wings should mirror each other. If one is longer, you rolled further on that side, and evening it up will take a couple more passes on the short wing before you go back to symmetric rolls.
