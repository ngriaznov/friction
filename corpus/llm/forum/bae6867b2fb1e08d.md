**Re: Najdorf English Attack — why do I keep getting mated?**

Posted by **rookendgame_ted** — Tue 14 Oct, 19:42

> I play the Najdorf and I keep losing to the English Attack. I castle, I play b5, and then suddenly there are three pieces near my king and I'm dead. What am I doing wrong?

I get this question at my club roughly once a month, and nine times out of ten it's the same move. Let me set up the position.

```
1. e4 c5  2. Nf3 d6  3. d4 cxd4  4. Nxd4 Nf6
5. Nc3 a6  6. Be3 e5  7. Nb3 Be6  8. f3
```

Standard English Attack. White's plan is not subtle and never has been: Qd2, O-O-O, g4, g5, h4-h5, and the knight on f6 gets kicked so the queen and rook can come to h-file with tempo. Black's plan is equally straightforward: ...b5-b4 hitting the c3 knight, ...Nbd7-b6-c4 or ...a5-a4, and get at the white king first. It's a race, and the whole opening is about who arrives first.

**Here is the mistake.** At move 8, an enormous number of club players — I'd say most under 2000 — play

```
8... Be7
```

and then castle kingside on move 9 or 10. It looks completely natural. Develop the bishop, tuck the king away, connect the rooks. Every beginner's book you ever read told you to do this.

It's close to losing the game strategically, and here's why.

White answers 9.Qd2 O-O 10.O-O-O and now plays g4 with **absolute freedom**. He has no counterplay to worry about, because Black spent his two most valuable tempi on a plan (castling short) that does nothing to attack the white king. The g4-g5 push comes, the knight goes to d7 or e8 or h5 depending on your taste, and then h4-h5-h6 pries open the g-file or the h-file. Black is defending a position where every single one of White's pieces has an obvious target and none of Black's do.

Count the race. From move 10, White needs roughly g4, g5, h4, h5, h6 — five moves — to have a decisive attack, and he doesn't need to spend any on prophylaxis. Black, starting the standard ...b5, ...Nbd7, ...b4, ...Nb6, ...Nc4 plan from move 10, needs about six, and several of them get answered by Bxc4 or Nd5 tricks that cost more time. You lose the race by two tempi, and in a mutual-attack position two tempi is not a small disadvantage, it's the whole game.

**What to play instead.** The main lines are 8...Nbd7 or 8...h5. The point of 8...Nbd7 is that it's a useful developing move that also supports ...b5 and heads for c4 — it does not commit the king. Black very often ends up castling *queenside*, or simply leaving the king on e8 while the position is closed in the centre, which is entirely legal and entirely fine here.

8...h5 is the modern treatment and it's blunt: it stops g4 in its tracks. Yes, it looks anti-positional, and yes, it weakens g5. It also removes White's entire plan, and after ...h5 the game becomes a much slower manoeuvring struggle where Black's structure is perfectly healthy.

**The general principle**, which is worth more than the specific line: in opposite-side-castling positions, "safe king" and "castled king" are not the same thing. If your opponent's pawns are pointed at your king, castling into them is not development, it's picking a side of the board to be attacked on.

Play through a couple of Anand's Black Najdorfs from the late 90s and watch how long he leaves the king on e8. It'll rewire something.
