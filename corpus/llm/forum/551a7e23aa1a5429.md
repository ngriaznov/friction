At 40,000 rows the two approaches are not equivalent, and the differences show up in both speed and maintenance.

**Why INDEX/MATCH is usually faster here**

`VLOOKUP(A2, Data!A:Z, 14, FALSE)` asks Excel to hand back a 26-column array and then pick column 14 out of it. `INDEX(Data!N:N, MATCH(A2, Data!A:A, 0))` touches exactly two columns. With a wide table and tens of thousands of rows copied down tens of thousands of formulas, that difference in referenced cells is what you feel on recalculation.

The bigger win is dependency scope. Excel recalculates a formula when a cell it depends on changes. A VLOOKUP over `A:Z` depends on all 26 columns, so editing anything in that block marks every lookup dirty. INDEX/MATCH depends on the key column and the return column only, so unrelated edits don't trigger a recalc storm.

If you must keep VLOOKUP, at least stop using whole-column references and stop pointing at more columns than you need. `VLOOKUP(A2, Data!$A$2:$N$40000, 14, FALSE)` is dramatically better than the `A:Z` version.

**The column-insertion problem**

`VLOOKUP(..., 14, FALSE)` means "the 14th column of that range," counted positionally at the moment you wrote it. Insert a column anywhere between columns A and N of the source and the 14th column is now something else. The formula doesn't error — it happily returns the wrong field, which is the dangerous failure mode. You find out when someone notices the phone numbers are showing up in the email column.

`INDEX(Data!N:N, ...)` names the return column as a reference. Insert a column before N and Excel rewrites the reference to O automatically. The formula keeps pointing at the same data.

`COLUMN()` tricks and `MATCH` on the header row can patch VLOOKUP's index argument, but at that point you've written a longer, harder-to-read formula that does what INDEX/MATCH already does natively.

**Two other practical points**

INDEX/MATCH can look leftward. VLOOKUP requires the key to be in the first column of the range, which routinely forces people to duplicate a key column just to satisfy the function.

If you're doing many lookups against the same key column, `MATCH` once into a helper column and feed that row number to several `INDEX` calls. One search, several retrievals — often the largest single speedup available on a big sheet.

**If your Excel has XLOOKUP**

`XLOOKUP(A2, Data!A:A, Data!N:N, "", 0)` gives you the leftward lookup, the reference-based return column, and a built-in not-found value in one function. It behaves like INDEX/MATCH structurally, so the column-insertion issue doesn't apply. Use it if your whole audience is on a version that has it; otherwise INDEX/MATCH remains the compatible choice.
