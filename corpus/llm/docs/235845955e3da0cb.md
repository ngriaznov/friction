# Sizing a custom sweater with Woolgauge

Woolgauge takes your swatch measurements and a set of body measurements and gives you back stitch and row counts for every piece of a sweater. This tutorial walks through a plain drop-shoulder pullover in one size, then shows how to redo the numbers when you swap to a different yarn weight.

You will need a washed and blocked gauge swatch of at least 4 inches square, a ruler, and the chest, length, and sleeve measurements for the person the sweater is for.

## 1. Enter your swatch

Open **Swatches → New swatch**. Measure across the middle of your blocked swatch and count stitches over 4 inches. Do the same vertically for rows. Enter both, along with the needle size and the yarn you used.

A worsted swatch might read:

- Stitches over 4 in: 18
- Rows over 4 in: 24
- Needle: US 8 / 5.0 mm

Woolgauge stores this as 4.5 stitches and 6 rows per inch. Tap **Save**, and give the swatch a name you will recognise later — "Cascade 220, US 8, stockinette" is more useful in six months than "swatch 3."

If your swatch is smaller than 4 inches, use the **Partial swatch** toggle and enter the actual measured width. Counting over a small span exaggerates errors, so measure twice.

## 2. Enter body measurements

Go to **Projects → New → Drop-shoulder pullover**. Fill in:

- Finished chest circumference
- Body length from underarm to hem
- Sleeve length from underarm to cuff
- Upper arm circumference
- Cuff circumference

Enter the *finished garment* measurements, not the wearer's body. If you want 4 inches of positive ease over a 38-inch chest, enter 42.

## 3. Read the stitch counts

Tap **Calculate**. Woolgauge multiplies each measurement by your stitch or row gauge and rounds to the nearest whole stitch, adjusting to keep the front and back even.

For a 42-inch chest at 4.5 stitches per inch, the body is 189 stitches, rounded to 190 so it splits cleanly. Woolgauge shows the raw number in grey beside the rounded one, so you can see how far it moved.

The results screen breaks the sweater into body, sleeves, and neckband, and lists a cast-on count, a shaping schedule, and a bind-off for each. Sleeve shaping appears as "increase 1 stitch each side every 6th row, 14 times."

## 4. Adjust for a different yarn weight

Say you decide to knit the same sweater in DK. Don't edit the existing project — knit a new swatch and save it, then open the project and tap **Swatch** at the top of the results screen. Pick the new swatch from the list.

Every count recalculates against the new gauge while your body measurements stay fixed. At 5.5 stitches per inch, that same 42-inch chest becomes 231 stitches.

Two things to check after a swap. First, row-based shaping schedules change independently of stitch counts, so re-read the sleeve instructions rather than assuming the old ones still fit. Second, yardage. Tap **Estimate yarn** to get a rough total; finer yarn at the same dimensions almost always needs more.

Both versions stay in your project list, so you can compare them side by side before casting on.
