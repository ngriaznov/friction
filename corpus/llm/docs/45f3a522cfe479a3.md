# Logging Your First Batch with Barmwatch

Barmwatch is a mobile app for tracking beer fermentation — temperature, gravity readings, and tasting notes over the life of a batch. This tutorial walks through logging a new batch from pitch day to bottling, setting a temperature alert, and exporting the finished log as a PDF.

## Creating a batch

Open Barmwatch and tap the **+** button on the home screen to start a new batch. You'll be asked for:

- **Batch name** — anything you like; "American Pale Ale #4" or similar works fine.
- **Style** — pick from the built-in BJCP style list, or choose "Other" if you're brewing something that doesn't fit neatly into a category.
- **Pitch date** — defaults to today, but you can backdate this if you're entering a batch that's already underway.
- **Target original gravity (OG)** and **target final gravity (FG)** — optional, but useful later since Barmwatch will flag when your logged readings are drifting from plan.

Once you save the batch, it appears on your home screen with a running fermentation timeline that fills in as you log readings.

## Logging readings

Tap into the batch and use **Log Reading** to record a data point. Each entry captures:

- Gravity (as SG or Plato, depending on your unit setting under **Settings > Units**)
- Temperature
- A free-text note field, useful for recording anything visual — krausen height, airlock activity, off smells you want to keep an eye on

Barmwatch doesn't require a fixed schedule — log as often as makes sense for the batch. Daily readings during active fermentation, tapering to every few days as gravity stabilizes, is a reasonable default. The app plots gravity and temperature on a shared timeline graph so you can see fermentation activity taper off visually, which is often a clearer signal that fermentation is finishing than gravity numbers alone.

## Setting a temperature alert

Fermentation temperature swings can produce off-flavors, so it's worth setting an alert if you're using a connected thermometer (Barmwatch supports a handful of Bluetooth fermentation thermometers under **Settings > Connected Devices**; without one, alerts can still be set manually against logged readings).

From the batch screen, tap **Alerts > Add Temperature Alert** and set a range — for example, 66–70°F for a typical ale fermentation. If a connected thermometer reports a reading outside that range, Barmwatch sends a push notification. If you're logging temperature manually instead, the same range is checked against each reading as you enter it, and out-of-range entries are flagged in the timeline with an orange marker so you can spot them when reviewing the batch later even if you missed the moment itself.

You can adjust the alert range mid-fermentation — useful if you're intentionally raising temperature for a diacetyl rest toward the end of fermentation and don't want every reading in that window to trip the alarm.

## Exporting a brew log as a PDF

Once a batch is complete (mark it **Done** from the batch menu, which locks further readings but doesn't delete anything), tap **Export > PDF Brew Log**. This generates a single-page summary containing:

- Recipe details and target numbers, if you entered them
- The full gravity and temperature graph
- Every logged reading with its timestamp and note
- Final calculated ABV, based on your recorded OG and FG

The PDF is saved to your device and can be shared directly from the export screen — useful for keeping a printed brew log binder, or for posting to a homebrewing forum alongside tasting notes. If you didn't mark the batch Done, Export still works, but the PDF is stamped "In Progress" and will reflect whatever readings exist at export time, which can be handy if you want a snapshot partway through a long lager fermentation rather than waiting until bottling day.
