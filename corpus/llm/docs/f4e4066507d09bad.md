# Fernbridge Mesh Troubleshooting

This guide covers the three problems that account for most Fernbridge support tickets: weak links between nodes, devices that stay stuck on a distant node, and firmware updates that fail partway through. Work through the section that matches your symptom. If none of them fit, the diagnostics bundle described at the end will help support narrow it down.

Before starting, open the Fernbridge app and check **Network > Node Health**. Every node reports a backhaul signal strength in dBm to its parent. You will need those numbers below.

## Weak signal between nodes

Fernbridge nodes talk to each other on a dedicated 5 GHz backhaul channel. That channel carries all traffic from every device attached to a satellite node, so a marginal backhaul link degrades everything downstream even when the client-facing signal looks fine.

Check the backhaul reading for the node that feels slow:

| Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|
| −35 to −60 dBm | Healthy |
| −61 to −70 dBm | Usable, some throughput loss |
| Below −70 dBm | Move the node |

If a node reads below −70 dBm, the usual cause is distance or an obstruction rather than interference. Two interior walls at an angle attenuate more than the straight-line distance suggests, and anything with foil backing, a large mirror, or a water heater in the path will cost you 10 dBm or more on its own.

Move the node closer to its parent and re-check. A good rule is that each satellite should sit roughly halfway between the router and the area you want covered, not in the dead zone itself. A node parked in a weak spot inherits that weak spot.

If moving the node is not practical, try changing the backhaul channel under **Advanced > Backhaul Channel**. Fernbridge picks a channel at setup and does not re-evaluate it automatically. In dense apartment buildings the initial choice is often crowded by the time you notice a problem.

## Devices not roaming to the closest node

Clients decide for themselves when to switch nodes. Fernbridge can encourage a handoff, but it cannot force one on a device that refuses. This is why a phone carried from the living room to the basement often clings to the upstairs node at −78 dBm while a much closer node sits idle.

Two settings affect this.

**Roaming assist** (on by default) sends an 802.11v transition request when a client's signal drops below the threshold set in **Advanced > Roaming**. The default threshold is −72 dBm. Lowering it to −68 dBm makes handoffs happen sooner. Set it too aggressively and devices will bounce between nodes, which is worse than a sticky client.

**Band steering** can interact badly with roaming on older devices. If a specific device roams poorly and nothing else does, exclude it: **Devices > (device) > Steering: Off**.

Some clients ignore transition requests entirely. Older smart plugs, e-readers, and a few printers fall into this category. For those, turning the device's Wi-Fi off and on after you move it is the only reliable fix, and in practice that rarely matters since they do not move.

## Firmware update failures

An update that reaches 100% and then reports "Verification failed" almost always means the download was corrupted in transit. Retry it once. If it fails a second time, the cause is more likely one of these:

- **A satellite is on a marginal backhaul link.** Updates push to satellites over the backhaul. Below −70 dBm the transfer frequently times out. Fix the signal problem first, then update.
- **Less than 15% free storage on the node.** Check **Node Health > Storage**. Clearing the device log from the same screen usually frees enough.
- **An outbound firewall rule.** If you have an upstream firewall, the router needs HTTPS access to `updates.fernbridge.net`.

If a node loses power mid-update it will boot into recovery and blink amber twice per second. It is not bricked. Hold the reset pin for three seconds — not the full ten, which factory-resets it — and the node will re-request the firmware from the router.

For anything else, generate a diagnostics bundle from **Settings > Support > Export Diagnostics** and attach it to your ticket. It includes node topology, signal history for the last seven days, and the update log, which is usually enough to identify the problem without a back-and-forth.
