# MoltenPath Troubleshooting Guide

This guide covers three of the most common problems reported by MoltenPath users: layer shifting mid-print, nozzle clogs, and bed adhesion failures. Work through the diagnostic steps in order for whichever problem matches what you're seeing — most causes are mechanical, not firmware bugs, but a couple of settings are worth checking first since they're quick to rule out.

## Layer shifting mid-print

Layer shifting shows up as one or more layers suddenly offset horizontally from the layers below, as if the print head "skipped" partway through.

**Diagnostic steps:**

1. Check the print for a consistent shift direction. A shift that always happens in the same axis (always X, never Y) points to a mechanical issue on that axis specifically, rather than a firmware or power problem.
2. Inspect the belt on the affected axis for slack. With the printer powered off, pluck the belt gently — it should have a firm, consistent tension along its length, similar to a guitar string tuned low. A belt that's gone slack partway through a long print (as plastic components warm and settle) is the single most common cause.
3. Check the pulley set screws on the stepper motor shaft for that axis. A pulley that's slipping on the shaft will cause the belt to move less than the motor commands, producing a shift that tends to get worse as the print goes on rather than happening as a single sharp jump.
4. Rule out a mechanical obstruction: check that cabling isn't snagging on the frame or on a completed part of the print as the head moves through its full range of travel.

**Fixes:** Retension the belt per your printer's manual (MoltenPath doesn't control belt tension, but `Settings > Motion > Axis Steps/mm` can help you confirm current stepping is being translated into the expected physical distance, which is a good sanity check after retensioning). Tighten pulley set screws with the printer powered off. If the shift only ever happens on long prints, also check that stepper driver current isn't set so low that the motor loses steps under load — `Settings > Motion > Driver Current` in the MoltenPath config menu.

## Nozzle clogs

A clog usually presents as thin or missing extrusion, a sudden change to under-extrusion partway through a print, or the extruder motor clicking as it tries to push filament that isn't moving.

**Diagnostic steps:**

1. Confirm it's a clog and not a filament runout: check the spool has feed remaining and that filament is actually reaching the extruder gear.
2. Perform a cold pull: heat the nozzle to your filament's normal printing temperature, then let it cool to roughly 90°C for PLA (higher for PETG or ABS) before manually retracting filament firmly. A clean pull with a consistent thin taper at the tip suggests the clog has cleared; a pull that comes out short or with an irregular shape suggests debris is still lodged in the nozzle.
3. Check `Diagnostics > Extruder > Flow Estimate` in MoltenPath, which compares commanded extrusion against the thermistor-inferred flow rate on printers with a flow sensor installed. A flow estimate persistently below 70% of commanded is a strong signal of partial blockage even before you see it on the print.

**Fixes:** Repeat the cold pull two or three times if the first attempt is inconclusive. For a stubborn clog, a cold pull won't be enough — use a nozzle cleaning needle (0.4mm or matched to your nozzle diameter) inserted from the top while the nozzle is hot, or remove and soak the nozzle in acetone (for ABS residue) or a dedicated nozzle-cleaning solution. Afterward, run a short test extrusion from `Maintenance > Extrude` before starting a real print.

## Bed adhesion failures

This shows up as a print that detaches from the bed partway through, most often at a corner first, or that never sticks in the first place.

**Diagnostic steps:**

1. Check bed leveling first — uneven adhesion across the bed (sticking well in the center, failing at the edges) almost always points here. Run `Calibration > Bed Level` and check the mesh output for any point deviating more than 0.1mm from its neighbors.
2. Check first-layer height directly: a first layer squished too thin won't adhere; one printed too high will have poor contact regardless of bed temperature. MoltenPath's `Calibration > First Layer` walks through a manual paper-test calibration if you don't have an inductive probe.
3. Confirm bed temperature is actually being reached, not just commanded — `Diagnostics > Heated Bed > Temp Log` shows a graph of actual vs. target temperature. A bed that's slow to reach temperature (common with a worn heating element or a loose thermistor) can silently start a print before it's actually at the right temperature.

**Fixes:** Relevel the bed and recalibrate first-layer height using the steps above. Clean the bed surface with isopropyl alcohol between prints — oils from handling the bed surface are a common and easy-to-miss cause of otherwise-mysterious adhesion loss. For materials that need it, increase bed temperature by 5°C increments and add a brim (`Slicer Settings > Adhesion > Brim`) rather than relying on bed temperature alone to solve a marginal case.
