# Logging your first season with Combwatch

This tutorial walks through the three things most beekeepers do in Combwatch every week: record an inspection, update queen status, and schedule a mite treatment reminder. It assumes you have already installed the app and added at least one hive. If you haven't, tap **Apiary → Add hive** and give it a name you'll recognize at the hive stand — "Back fence, left" beats "Hive 3" when you're wearing a veil.

## Log a weekly inspection

Open the hive card and tap **New inspection**. The form opens with today's date and, if location services are on, the current temperature pulled from the nearest station. Both are editable — beekeepers who write notes at the kitchen table an hour later often correct the time.

Work down the form in the order you actually inspect:

1. **Frames of bees** — a whole number, 0 to the frame count you set for the box. Combwatch uses this to draw the colony strength curve on the hive card.
2. **Brood pattern** — choose Solid, Spotty, or None. Spotty over two consecutive inspections triggers a soft flag on the hive card; it is not an alarm, just a nudge to look harder next time.
3. **Stores** — frames of capped honey and frames of pollen, entered separately.
4. **Temperament** — a 1–5 slider. Useful later when you're deciding which colony to breed from.
5. **Notes** — free text. Photos attach here; tap the camera icon.

Tap **Save**. Inspections are stored locally first and sync when you're back on a network, so a dead cell signal in the bee yard costs you nothing.

## Track queen status

Queen state lives on its own tab rather than inside the inspection form, because it changes less often and you want its history separate.

On the hive card, tap **Queen**. The status picker offers:

- **Laying** — you saw eggs, larvae, or the queen herself.
- **Present, not seen** — eggs but no sighting. Most common entry.
- **Queenless** — no eggs, no open brood.
- **Virgin / mating** — after a supersedure or a split.
- **Queen cells present** — with a count field.

Set **Marked color** and **Year introduced** once, when you install or find her. Combwatch computes her age from that and shows it on the hive card, which is the number you actually want in year three when you're deciding whether to requeen.

Changing status to Queenless or Queen cells present adds a dated entry to the queen timeline. That timeline is what you'll scroll through in August when you're trying to remember whether this colony superseded in May or June.

## Set a mite treatment reminder

Tap **Treatments → Schedule**. Pick the hive (or **All hives** — treating one colony in an apiary and not its neighbors rarely ends well), choose the product, and set the start date.

Enter your treatment interval and the number of applications. For a three-application oxalic dribble at seven-day spacing, enter 7 and 3; Combwatch creates all three reminders and a follow-up alert for the post-treatment mite wash two weeks after the last one.

Reminders fire as device notifications at the hour you set under **Settings → Notification time**. Set it for an evening the night before, not the morning of — you'll want to check the forecast before you commit to opening hives.

Completed treatments appear in the hive's treatment log, which is also where you'll find the export button if your state inspector asks for records.
