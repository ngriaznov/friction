# Bake Your First Sourdough Loaf with Levain Log

The hard part of sourdough isn't kneading or shaping. It's timing. A starter peaks and falls on its own schedule, dough ferments faster in a warm kitchen than a cold one, and the recipe that told you "bulk ferment for four hours" was written in somebody else's house. Levain Log exists to keep track of all that for you: you tell it what you fed and when, and it tells you when the next thing should happen.

This tutorial assumes you have never kept a starter before.

## Step 1: Get a starter

You need actual starter — a jar of flour and water colonized by wild yeast and lactic bacteria. Either beg some from a baker friend (fastest, and the culture is already mature) or grow your own, which takes ten to fourteen days of daily feeding before it's reliable.

If you're growing one from scratch, open Levain Log and tap **New Culture → Establish from flour and water**. Give it a name. The app will nag you once a day, and it will not let you skip ahead to baking until the culture has logged three consecutive doublings — a deliberate guardrail, because almost every failed first loaf is a starter that wasn't ready.

## Step 2: Log your feedings

Every time you feed, record it. Tap the culture, hit **Feed**, and enter three numbers: grams of starter kept, grams of flour, grams of water. A 1:5:5 feeding means 20 g of old starter, 100 g flour, 100 g water.

Then enter your kitchen temperature. This is the part people skip, and it's the part that makes the predictions work. Fermentation roughly doubles in speed for every 8 °C you go up. Levain Log uses your logged temperature and your feeding ratio to estimate a **peak time** — the moment the starter has risen as far as it will go, before it starts collapsing. That estimate shows up as a countdown on the culture card.

Feed at the same ratio for a few days and the app narrows its estimate. Mine settled around 5 h 40 min at 21 °C.

## Step 3: Plan the bake backwards

Once your culture has passed the readiness check, tap **Plan a Bake**. Levain Log asks one question first: when do you want bread out of the oven?

Say Sunday at 11:00. The app schedules backwards from there:

- **Bake** — Sunday 09:20
- **Cold retard ends** — Sunday 09:00, straight from fridge to oven
- **Shape and refrigerate** — Saturday 21:00
- **Bulk ferment** — Saturday 16:30 to 21:00, with stretch-and-fold reminders at 30-minute intervals for the first two hours
- **Mix dough** — Saturday 16:15
- **Levain peaks** — Saturday 16:00
- **Build levain** — Saturday 10:20

That last time is the one that matters. It's the only step you have to remember unprompted; everything after it is a notification.

Bulk ferment length is temperature-driven, not fixed. If your kitchen drifts, tap **Adjust temperature** and every downstream step shifts. Warm afternoon means the app pulls shaping earlier.

## Step 4: Bake day

Sunday morning, take the dough from the fridge and put it straight into a preheated Dutch oven — 250 °C, lid on for 20 minutes, then lid off at 230 °C for another 20 to 25 until the crust is genuinely dark.

Before you eat it, tap **Log Result** and rate crumb openness, sourness, and oven spring. After three or four logged bakes, Levain Log starts tuning its bulk-ferment estimate against your feedback. Rate a loaf as dense and gummy, and it lengthens bulk next time.

## A note on trusting the clock

The predictions are good, not psychic. Use the poke test: a properly proofed dough springs back slowly and incompletely. If the app says 20 minutes left and the dough looks ready now, the dough wins. Log the discrepancy — that's how it learns your kitchen.
