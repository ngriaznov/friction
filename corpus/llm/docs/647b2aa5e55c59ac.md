# Logging Your First Batch in Barmwatch

This tutorial walks you through the three things most new users want on day one: recording a batch, getting a phone alert when your fermentation temperature drifts, and pulling a PDF of the whole log when the beer is done. It assumes you have already installed Barmwatch and created an account. Budget about fifteen minutes, ideally on brew day itself while the numbers are fresh.

## Create the batch

Open Barmwatch and tap the **+** button on the Batches screen. You will be asked for four things:

- **Batch name.** Anything you will recognize later. "Ordinary Bitter #3" beats "Test".
- **Style.** Pick from the list or choose *Custom*. This only affects the suggested temperature range, so don't agonize over it.
- **Batch size.** In liters or gallons, set under Settings > Units.
- **Brew date.** Defaults to today.

Tap **Save**. The batch is now in your list with a status of *Planned*.

## Record your gravity readings

The batch detail screen has a **Readings** section. Tap **Add reading** and enter your original gravity along with the wort temperature at the time you measured it. If you are using a hydrometer rather than a refractometer, leave *Temperature correct* switched on — Barmwatch will adjust the reading toward your hydrometer's calibration temperature, which is 20°C / 68°F by default and adjustable under Settings > Hydrometer.

Once you pitch yeast, tap **Start fermentation**. The batch status changes to *Fermenting* and the app starts drawing a gravity curve. Add a reading every couple of days. Two or three points is enough to see the shape; you do not need to open the fermenter daily, and you shouldn't.

When gravity is stable across two readings taken 48 hours apart, tap **Record final gravity**. Barmwatch calculates ABV and apparent attenuation for you and moves the batch to *Conditioning*.

## Set a temperature alert

Alerts live under the **Monitoring** tab inside the batch.

1. Tap **Add alert**.
2. Choose **Temperature range**.
3. Set a low and high bound. For a typical ale yeast, 18–22°C / 64–72°F is a reasonable starting window.
4. Choose how you want to be notified: push notification, email, or both.

If you have a supported Bluetooth thermometer paired under Settings > Devices, Barmwatch reads it automatically every ten minutes and fires the alert on its own. Without a probe, the alert becomes a reminder: the app prompts you once a day to enter a temperature manually, and warns you if the value you type falls outside the range.

A note on tuning: set the bounds a little wider than the yeast's ideal range for the first batch. Alerts that fire three times a night get muted, and a muted alert protects nothing.

## Export the brew log

When the batch is finished, open it and tap the share icon in the top right, then **Export as PDF**. You will be offered two layouts:

- **Summary** — one page with the recipe details, OG/FG, ABV, and the gravity chart.
- **Full log** — everything above plus every reading, note, and alert event with timestamps.

Pick a destination and you're done. The full log is the one to send if you are entering a competition or asking a more experienced brewer why something tasted odd — the timestamps usually answer the question before anyone has to guess.
