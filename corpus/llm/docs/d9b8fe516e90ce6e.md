# Submitting and tagging sightings in Featherlog

This guide walks through logging a single sighting from the field: attaching a photo, tagging the species and location, and getting entries off your phone once you have signal again.

## Before you start

Open Featherlog at least once while you have a connection. The app downloads a regional species list on first launch, and without it the species picker falls back to a much slower search that queries the server. If you are heading somewhere remote, tap **Settings → Regions** and download the region you will be birding in.

## Creating a sighting

1. Tap the **+** button on the main list.
2. Choose **Quick log** or **Full entry**. Quick log records species, count, and a timestamp, and is meant for logging while birds are still moving. Full entry adds fields for behavior, habitat, and notes.
3. Enter a count. If you did not get an exact number, tap the count field and use the **estimate** toggle — this marks the number as approximate, and reviewers treat estimated counts differently from exact ones.
4. Tap **Save**.

A sighting saved without a species is kept as a draft. Drafts do not sync and do not appear in regional totals until you assign a species.

## Adding a photo

From the sighting detail screen, tap **Add media**. You can take a new photo or pick one from your camera roll.

Featherlog reads the EXIF timestamp and coordinates from photos you import. If the photo's timestamp differs from the sighting time by more than fifteen minutes, the app asks which one to trust. Pick the photo's timestamp if you are logging after the fact from the car.

Photos are uploaded at reduced resolution by default. To keep full resolution for a record shot of something unusual, toggle **Upload original** on the media item before syncing. Originals count against your storage quota.

## Tagging species

Tap the species field and start typing either a common or scientific name. The picker matches on both, plus four-letter banding codes.

For birds you could not identify to species, use a **slash tag** or a **spuh**:

- `Greater/Lesser Scaup` — you narrowed it to two species.
- `scaup sp.` — genus-level only.
- `duck sp.` — family-level only.

These are real, useful records. Do not guess a species to avoid an ambiguous tag; a confident wrong ID is worse for the dataset than an honest `sp.`

If you believe you have something outside its normal range, the app flags it as **needs documentation** and will not sync until you add either a photo or a written description of the field marks you saw.

## Tagging location

By default the sighting takes your GPS position. Two adjustments matter:

- **Snap to hotspot.** If you are within roughly 200 meters of a known hotspot, Featherlog offers to attach the sighting to it. Accepting this makes your record comparable with everyone else's from that site.
- **Personal locations.** For a yard list or a patch nobody else visits, tap **New personal location** and name it. Personal locations stay private to your account.

If you walked a transect, switch the effort type from **Stationary** to **Traveling** and enter the distance covered. Distance is what turns a species list into something usable for density estimates.

## Syncing offline entries

Sightings created without a connection are stored locally and marked with a small cloud-and-slash icon.

Featherlog attempts a sync whenever the app is foregrounded on a network it considers usable. To force one, pull down on the main list. The header shows `n pending` while uploads are in progress.

If a sighting fails to sync, tap it to see the reason. The two common ones are a missing species tag on a draft and an unresolved out-of-range flag. Both are fixed inside the entry itself. Photos retry independently of the sighting record, so a sighting can appear synced while its media is still uploading — the media item shows its own progress indicator until it completes.

Do not delete and re-enter a stuck sighting. Doing so discards the original timestamp and, if the entry had already partially uploaded, can leave a duplicate on the server.
