# Troubleshooting common print failures

This guide covers the three failure modes we see most often in support tickets for MoltenPath firmware: layer shifting partway through a print, nozzle clogs, and prints that come loose from the bed. Each section lists the symptoms, the diagnostic steps to narrow down the cause, and the fixes.

Before working through any of these, confirm which firmware build you are running. Send `M115` over the serial console or check **Menu → About → Build**. Several of the fixes below reference settings that only exist in 2.4 and later.

## Layer shifting mid-print

**Symptom:** The print looks correct up to a certain height, then every layer above that point is offset in X, Y, or both. The offset usually stays constant for the rest of the print.

Layer shifting is almost always a lost-step problem. The controller told the motor to move, and the motor did not go where it was told, so every subsequent coordinate is wrong by that amount.

**Diagnose:**

1. Power off and push each axis by hand. It should glide. If you feel a grabby spot at a particular position, you have a mechanical obstruction — a loose belt clip, debris in a linear rail, or a binding lead screw.
2. Check belt tension. Pluck the belt like a guitar string; a healthy belt on most bed-slinger frames sounds around a low thrum, not a slack flap.
3. Look at where in the print the shift happened. If it lines up with a tall infill region or a fast travel move, the head likely collided with a curled-up part of the print.
4. Feel the stepper motors after a failed print. A motor too hot to hold your hand on for five seconds is running above its safe current and may be thermally cutting out.

**Fix:**

- Reduce `max_accel` and `max_velocity` in your printer profile by 25% and reprint. If the shift disappears, raise them back gradually.
- Retension belts, then re-run `G28` and confirm the head returns to the same physical spot every time.
- If motors are overheating, lower the stepper driver current with `M906 X800 Y800` (values in mA) and save with `M500`. Start at roughly 60–70% of the motor's rated current.
- Enable Z-hop on travel (`travel_lift: 0.4`) if the head is catching on curled perimeters.

## Nozzle clogs

**Symptom:** Extrusion thins out or stops entirely. The extruder motor clicks or the filament grinds flat where the drive gear grips it.

**Diagnose:**

Heat the hotend to printing temperature and push filament through by hand with the extruder disengaged. If it extrudes freely by hand but not under motor power, the problem is grip or tension, not a blockage. If it will not push through by hand, you have a real clog.

Partial clogs — where extrusion works but is visibly under-volume — are usually heat creep. These get worse the longer a print runs, and often clear after the printer cools completely, which is the tell.

**Fix:**

- For a full clog: heat to 240°C, insert an 0.4 mm cleaning needle from the nozzle side, then do three or four cold pulls at 90°C with PLA.
- For heat creep: confirm the hotend fan is actually spinning at 100% whenever the hotend is above 50°C. Check `heater_fan_threshold` in your config.
- Replace the PTFE liner if the printer has run high-temperature material. A shrunken or charred liner leaves a gap where filament pools.

## Bed adhesion failures

**Symptom:** Corners lift during the print, or the whole first layer detaches and the head drags a blob around.

**Diagnose:**

Look at the first layer. Lines that are round and separated mean the nozzle is too far from the bed. Lines that are translucent and rippled mean it is too close. Either extreme reduces adhesion.

**Fix:**

- Re-run `G29` bed mesh calibration on a bed heated to your normal printing temperature, not cold. Glass and spring steel both move as they warm.
- Adjust `z_offset` in 0.02 mm steps using `M851 Z-0.06`, then `M500`.
- Wash the build surface with dish soap and warm water. Isopropyl alcohol smears finger oils around rather than removing them.
- For materials prone to warping, raise the chamber or enclosure temperature and add a brim of at least 8 mm.

If none of this resolves the failure, capture the full serial log from the failed print and open a ticket with the log attached — the timestamped move commands usually make the cause obvious.
