# Getting Started with Barmwatch

Barmwatch is a mobile app for home brewers that tracks fermentation over the life of a batch, alerts you when temperatures drift outside your target range, and produces a clean brew log you can share or archive. This tutorial walks through logging your first batch, setting up temperature alerts, and exporting a finished log as a PDF.

## Logging a New Batch

Open Barmwatch and tap the **+** button in the bottom right corner of the Batches tab. You'll be prompted for a few basics: batch name, style (chosen from a searchable list or entered freely), target original gravity, and pitch date. None of these fields are required to save the batch, but filling in the original gravity now makes the attenuation calculations more useful later.

Once the batch is created, tap into it to reach the **Log** screen. This is where you'll record readings throughout fermentation. Each entry accepts a gravity reading, a temperature, and an optional note. Tap **Add Reading**, enter the values from your hydrometer or refractometer, and save. Barmwatch plots these points automatically on a gravity-over-time chart, so after a few days you'll start to see the fermentation curve take shape.

If you're using a Bluetooth hydrometer, such as a Tilt or an iSpindel, connect it from **Settings > Devices** instead of entering readings by hand. Once paired, Barmwatch polls the device on an interval you choose and logs gravity and temperature automatically, which is especially useful overnight.

## Setting Temperature Alerts

Fermentation temperature swings are one of the most common causes of off-flavors, so Barmwatch lets you set a target range per batch. From the batch detail screen, tap **Alerts**, then enter a low and high threshold in degrees. You can set these based on the yeast strain's recommended range — for example, 64–70°F for a typical American ale strain.

With alerts enabled, Barmwatch checks incoming readings (whether logged manually or pulled from a connected device) against your thresholds. If a reading falls outside the range, you'll get a push notification along with a short suggestion, such as moving the fermenter or adjusting a temperature controller. Alerts are batch-specific, so you can run a lager in one corner of the house and an ale in another without either one triggering the wrong notifications.

## Exporting a Brew Log as PDF

When a batch is done — whether it's finished fermenting, kegged, or bottled — you can generate a shareable record of the whole process. From the batch detail screen, tap the overflow menu in the top right and select **Export Log**. Choose **PDF** as the format.

The exported document includes your recipe notes, the full gravity and temperature chart, every logged reading with timestamps, and any tasting notes you added under the **Notes** tab. You can save the PDF to your device, email it directly from the share sheet, or upload it to a cloud folder if you keep a running archive of past batches.

That covers the core workflow: create a batch, log or auto-import readings, set sensible temperature alerts, and export a log when you're done. From here, the **Recipes** tab lets you save common grain bills and hop schedules so future batches take even less setup.
