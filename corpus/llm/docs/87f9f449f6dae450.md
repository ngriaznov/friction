# Calibrating Your CNC Router with Sawline

A router that cuts a 100 mm square as a 99.4 mm square is not broken — it just hasn't been told the truth about its own hardware. This tutorial walks through the three calibration steps that matter most: setting work-zero, running a test cut, and correcting steps-per-millimeter in Sawline.

You'll need a piece of scrap at least 150 mm square (MDF is ideal — it cuts predictably and doesn't move), a sharp 1/4" straight or upcut bit, and a set of digital calipers. Analog calipers work, but you'll be reading to the hundredth of a millimeter, and a dial is harder to trust at that resolution.

## Step 1: Set work-zero

Work-zero is the corner of your material that all G-code coordinates are measured from. Sawline keeps machine coordinates (referenced to your limit switches) and work coordinates separate, so homing the machine does not set work-zero for you.

Clamp your scrap down. Jog the spindle using the arrow keys in the **Jog** panel until the bit sits roughly over the front-left corner of the stock. Drop the jog step to 0.1 mm and creep in until the edge of the bit is flush with the left edge of the material, then click **Zero X**. Repeat against the front edge and click **Zero Y**.

For Z, the paper method is enough for a first calibration. Slide a sheet of printer paper under the bit, jog down in 0.1 mm steps, and stop the moment the paper drags. Click **Zero Z**, then use **Offset Z** and enter `0.1` to account for the paper thickness. If you have a touch plate, Sawline's **Probe Z** button handles this and stores the plate thickness from Settings → Probing.

The status bar should now read `WCS: X0.000 Y0.000 Z0.000`. If it still shows machine coordinates, you're in the wrong coordinate system — press `G54` in the MDI box.

## Step 2: Run the test cut

Sawline ships with a calibration job under **File → Templates → Calibration Square**. It cuts a 100 mm × 100 mm pocket 2 mm deep, climb-milling the perimeter in a single pass. Load it, confirm the feed rate matches your machine (600 mm/min is a safe default), and run it.

When it finishes, don't unclamp anything yet. Measure the pocket in X at three places — near the front, the middle, and the back — and average them. Do the same in Y. Write both numbers down.

A pocket measuring 99.4 mm in X means the machine moved less than it thought it did. A pocket measuring 100.6 mm means it moved too far. Either way, the fix is the same arithmetic.

## Step 3: Adjust steps-per-millimeter

Open **Settings → Motion**. Each axis has a `steps/mm` value — commonly 80 for a belt-driven axis or 200 for a leadscrew, though yours may differ.

The correction is:

```
new_steps = old_steps × (commanded / measured)
```

For an X axis currently at 80.000 steps/mm that cut 99.40 mm instead of 100.00 mm:

```
80.000 × (100.00 / 99.40) = 80.483
```

Enter `80.483` in the X field, do the same for Y, and click **Apply & Persist**. The persist step writes the value to the controller's EEPROM — without it, the change is lost at the next power cycle.

Now cut the calibration square again on fresh stock. You should land within 0.05 mm. If you don't, run the arithmetic once more on the new numbers; two iterations converge on almost every machine.

## When the numbers won't settle

If repeated calibration keeps producing different errors, the problem isn't steps-per-millimeter — that value is a constant, and chasing it will only mask something mechanical. Check for a loose pulley set screw, a belt you can pluck like a slack guitar string, or backlash in a worn anti-backlash nut. Sawline's **Diagnostics → Repeatability Test** jogs an axis back and forth 20 times and reports the drift; anything over 0.1 mm points at hardware, not software.

Re-run this calibration after any change to belts, pulleys, or motor drivers.
