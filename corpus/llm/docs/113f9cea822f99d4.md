# Troubleshooting Brewtemp

This page covers the three problems that account for most Brewtemp support requests: erratic temperature readings, a boiler that stalls below its setpoint, and profile uploads the firmware refuses.

Before working through any section, power the machine down at the wall and let the group head cool. Brewtemp's probe and relay wiring sit next to mains-voltage boiler leads.

## Erratic or jumping probe readings

Symptom: the displayed temperature swings by several degrees per second, drops to a floor value like `-40 °C`, or briefly reads a number far above anything the boiler could produce.

Brewtemp expects a two-wire PT100 or a K-type thermocouple, depending on which probe kit you installed. In both cases, noise almost always comes from the connection rather than the sensor element.

1. Open the diagnostic screen (hold the encoder for three seconds, then select **Diag → Probe**). Note the raw ADC counts, not the converted temperature. Steady counts with a wild converted value means a calibration problem; jumping counts means a wiring problem.
2. Check the crimps at the terminal block. A thermocouple junction that has been re-crimped over an oxidised tip will read intermittently. Cut back 5 mm and re-terminate rather than re-tightening the screw.
3. Route the probe leads away from the pump and the boiler heating element leads. Crossing them at right angles is fine; running them parallel for 20 cm is not. On several machines this alone removes the noise.
4. If your kit shipped with a ferrite bead, it belongs on the probe cable close to the controller, not at the probe end.

A reading pinned at the extreme low value usually means an open circuit — a broken conductor inside the sheath, most often where the cable flexes near the group.

Persistent small oscillation (±0.5 °C, smooth rather than jumpy) is not a probe fault. That is the PID hunting, and it is addressed under **Tuning** in the configuration reference.

## Machine does not reach the setpoint

Symptom: the temperature climbs, then flattens out several degrees short and stays there. The heating indicator may show the element as active.

Work through these in order:

- **Confirm the setpoint is what you think it is.** If you are running a profile, the active target is the profile's current stage value, not the idle setpoint shown on the home screen.
- **Check the duty-cycle cap.** Brewtemp limits the element duty cycle to protect the SSR. If `max_duty` is set low (the default on the 900 W boiler profile is 70%), the machine can be thermally unable to reach a high setpoint. Raise it in 5% steps and watch the SSR heatsink temperature.
- **Verify the SSR is actually switching fully.** A solid-state relay that has partially failed will conduct enough to warm the boiler but not enough to reach setpoint. With the machine hot and short of target, the SSR body should be noticeably warm; if it is hot enough to be uncomfortable to touch, replace it.
- **Rule out the mechanical thermostat.** Many machines retain their original bimetallic stat in series with the element. If it is opening at, say, 95 °C, no amount of Brewtemp configuration will get you past that. Bypassing it is a decision with real safety consequences — do not do it unless you have added a thermal cutoff elsewhere in the circuit.

Descaling matters here too. A heavily scaled element transfers heat poorly, and the symptom looks identical to an undersized duty cycle.

## Firmware rejects a profile upload

Symptom: the uploader reports `profile rejected: schema` or the transfer completes but the profile does not appear in the list.

Check these in order:

1. **Schema version.** Profiles carry a `version` key. Firmware 2.x will not accept `version: 1` profiles; run them through `brewtemp-convert` first.
2. **Stage count.** The controller allocates a fixed eight stages. A ninth is rejected outright, with no partial write.
3. **Monotonic timestamps.** Each stage's `at` value must be strictly greater than the previous one. Two stages at the same offset fail validation.
4. **Encoding.** The uploader reads UTF-8. A file saved as UTF-16 by a Windows editor will fail to parse, usually reported as an unexpected byte at offset 0.

If the upload still fails, put the controller into recovery mode (hold both buttons at power-on) and reflash. Recovery mode preserves stored profiles but resets the active selection to the built-in default.
