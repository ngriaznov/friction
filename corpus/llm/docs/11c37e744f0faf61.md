# Aligning your mount with Starwake

Starwake talks to a computerized equatorial or alt-az mount over Bluetooth or a serial cable and walks you through a two-star alignment. Once the alignment finishes, the mount knows where it is pointing, and "go to" commands land the target somewhere in a low-power eyepiece.

This guide assumes the mount is already assembled, roughly levelled, and — if it is equatorial — polar aligned well enough for visual work. Starwake does not replace polar alignment.

## Before you start

- Set the date, time, and location in **Settings > Site**. If you let the app pull location from the phone, check that the time zone matches; a one-hour error will throw every pointing model off by roughly 15 degrees.
- Balance the tube in both axes. An unbalanced load causes the drive to slip under its own weight, and the alignment you build will drift out within twenty minutes.
- Put a low-power eyepiece in the focuser — something in the 25 mm to 32 mm range. Save the high-power views for after the model is built.
- Wait until you can see at least three or four stars by eye. Starwake needs real stars, not planets.

## Running the two-star alignment

1. Power the mount, then open Starwake and tap **Connect**. The status pill at the top turns green when the handshake succeeds.
2. Point the tube at the celestial pole (for an equatorial mount) or level and due north (for alt-az). This is the home position. Tap **Set Home**.
3. Tap **Align > Two-Star**. Starwake proposes a first star from the stars currently well placed in your sky. Pick one that is at least 30 degrees above the horizon and away from rooftops or trees.
4. Tap **Slew**. The mount swings to its best guess. The star will usually be somewhere in the finder but not centred.
5. Use the on-screen arrows to centre the star in the finder, then in the eyepiece. Always finish your centring with the up and right arrows so the gears take up backlash in a consistent direction.
6. Tap **Confirm Star 1**.
7. Starwake now suggests a second star. Accept the suggestion or choose your own. Centre it the same way and tap **Confirm Star 2**.

When both stars are confirmed, the app reports a pointing residual in arcminutes. Anything under about 15 arcminutes is a good alignment for visual use. Above 40 arcminutes, redo it — something was off.

## The second star won't show up

This is the most common failure, and it almost always has a mundane cause.

**The mount slews to the wrong part of the sky.** Nearly always a home-position problem. Return to home, check that the tube really is pointing where the mount expects, and start over. On an equatorial mount, also confirm the counterweight bar is pointing down.

**The mount slews close, but the star is not in the finder.** Check that the finder itself is aligned with the main tube. Do this in daylight on a distant chimney; it takes five minutes and saves an hour at night.

**You centred the wrong object for star 1.** Bright planets are the usual culprits, as is the mismatch between what the app draws and what the sky looks like at low power. Use the app's **Identify** view to compare the field pattern before confirming.

**Star 2 is too close to star 1.** The app tries to prevent this, but manual picks can end up in the same region of sky. Choose a second star 60 to 90 degrees away in azimuth for a much stronger model.

**Time or location is wrong.** Recheck **Settings > Site**. A daylight-saving mistake is the classic offender.

If none of this helps, clear the model under **Align > Reset** and run the whole procedure again from home position. A fresh start is faster than debugging a bad model.
