# Passing the AWS Solutions Architect Associate While Working Full Time

I sat the SAA-C03 exam on a Saturday morning in March and passed with a 812. I'd been studying since early January, which works out to about ten weeks at six to eight hours a week. Here's what that actually looked like, since every "how I passed" post I read beforehand was either from someone with three months of free time or someone claiming they crammed it in a weekend.

## The constraint

I have a job and two kids. My study time was 6:00 to 7:00 a.m. on weekdays, sometimes not all five, plus a two-hour block on Sunday afternoons. That's it. Any plan requiring evening study was fiction — by 8:30 p.m. I'm useless.

So the schedule had to survive being interrupted. I broke everything into units that fit in a single hour, and I never planned a session that needed a running start from the previous day.

## Weeks 1–4: video course, on 1.5x

I worked through a standard video course covering the exam domains. One hour a morning got me through two or three lectures. I took notes by hand in a cheap notebook — not because handwriting is magic, but because it stopped me from tabbing away.

The Sunday block I used for hands-on work in a personal account: build a VPC with public and private subnets by hand, put an ALB in front of two EC2 instances, break it, fix it. Four Sundays, four small builds. This was the single highest-value thing I did. The exam asks a lot of "which of these four architectures satisfies the requirement" questions, and having actually wired a NAT gateway makes those read differently.

Set a billing alarm at five dollars before you do any of this.

## Weeks 5–7: services I'd been avoiding

I made a list of everything I couldn't explain out loud: Kinesis vs. SQS vs. SNS vs. EventBridge, the storage classes and their retrieval times, the difference between a gateway endpoint and an interface endpoint, Aurora's failover behavior. One service per morning. I wrote a paragraph on each in my own words. If the paragraph was vague, I didn't understand it yet.

## Weeks 8–10: practice exams only

This is where I'd spend the money if I were only spending it once.

I bought two practice exam sets. The first was a well-known third-party bank of six full-length tests with detailed explanations, and it was worth every cent — not for the questions themselves, but because the explanations tell you *why the other three answers are wrong*. That's the actual skill the exam tests. My first attempt was 61%. By the sixth I was in the low 80s.

The second set I bought was cheaper and, honestly, a waste. Questions were poorly worded, several answers were flat wrong, and one referenced a service limit that had changed two years earlier. I stopped after two tests.

The free question dumps floating around are worse than useless. Half are outdated and they train you to recognize question text rather than reason about architectures.

## What I'd do differently

I'd start practice exams in week 4, not week 8. Sitting one early would have shown me that my weak areas were security and cost optimization, not the networking I kept re-reading because I found it interesting.

Also: book the exam before you feel ready. I scheduled mine in week 6 and the deadline did more for my consistency than any study plan.
